## User stories

- **US-1.** As the player, I need three distinct, small repair facilities — a road repair base, a train repair yard, and a school/store workshop — each with its own running cost, so maintenance infrastructure is a real, per-domain build choice rather than a single invisible "repair" line item (FEAT-092 design; §10 "services buildings with upgrade paths").
- **US-2.** As a depot, I need to be growable — upgradeable in place through a small size ladder that extends my crew capacity and my reach — so a small city buys a small depot and grows it as the city grows, instead of being forced into a fixed-size build (FEAT-092 "GROWABLE"; §51's in-place-upgrade doctrine carried to a structure).
- **US-3.** As the maintenance budget, I need every depot to carry an OPEX cost that scales with its size and type, so a depot is a real ongoing claim on the city's money — not a one-off purchase that then runs free (FEAT-092 "own running cost"; §20's "maintenance budget is real").
- **US-4.** As `feat.maintenance` (the multi-fix crew model, FEAT-089), I need a queryable surface that answers "how much crew capacity and what reach does this domain have from its depots" — a depot of size N supporting a crew capacity of M(N) in its own domain — so crew dispatch limits are computed from real depot state, never invented by the crew layer (FEAT-092 "extends the reach/capacity of the maintenance crews in its domain").
- **US-5.** As a player reading the F4 services / build screens, I need depot OPEX, size, and domain coverage to be visible figures sourced from this module, so the running-cost trade-off is legible rather than buried (GR#17 — a user-visible status field must be freshness-monitored; the figure must come from real state).

## Scope

Three growable depot types — road repair base (domain `road`), train repair yard (domain `rail`), school/store workshop (domain `building`) — each a small size ladder (S1 → S2 → S3, directional), an in-place upgrade command, a per-size/per-type OPEX figure, and a domain reach/capacity surface consumed by the (unbuilt) `feat.maintenance` crew layer. The depots are placed/upgraded through `engine.build`'s `BuildAPI` (they are catalogue structures, GR#15), their OPEX is exposed for `feat.opexintegration`/`engine.finance` to drain, and their reach/capacity is exposed for `feat.maintenance` to consume. This feature does **not** implement the maintenance crews themselves, the aging/repair-demand model, the staffing pool, or the finance-side OPEX drain — those are FEAT-089/090/094 respectively.

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional

- **AC-1 (GR#20, GR#15; catalogue-driven depot types).** Exactly three depot types exist — road repair base, train repair yard, school/store workshop — loaded from `buildings.json` via `foundation.data`, each carrying a domain tag (one of `road`/`rail`/`building`), a size ladder, per-size crew-capacity and reach figures, and a per-size OPEX figure. No depot-type name, ladder rung, or numeric figure is hardcoded in Go source. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/repair DepotType` (or equivalent enum) lists exactly these three cases; a passing test asserts the catalogue resolves all three by name, that each has a non-empty size ladder, and that each carries a domain tag (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]epotCatalogue\|func Test.*[Tt]hreeDepotTypes" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`). False-pass risk: a grep for the three type-name strings would match doc-comment prose citing the design without a single type actually registered — the check requires a count assertion (three) plus a named-lookup test against the loaded catalogue.

- **AC-2 (Growable model — size ladder + in-place upgrade).** Every depot type has a documented, data-driven size ladder (directional: at least two rungs, canonical three — S1 small, S2 medium, S3 large), and a depot can be upgraded in place through a command that moves it to the next rung, increasing its crew-capacity and reach figures strictly (S2 > S1, S3 > S2). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/repair UpgradeDepotCommand` (or equivalent) exists; a passing test upgrades a fixture depot S1→S2 and asserts the reported crew capacity and reach radius both increase, with the increase sourced from the catalogue rather than a Go literal (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Gg]rowable\|func Test.*[Uu]pgradeDepot" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`). False-pass risk: a depot whose "size" label increments while the reported capacity/reach stay flat would pass a bare "upgrade returns success" check — the check requires the capacity and reach figures to actually move.

- **AC-3 (Running cost — OPEX per depot, per size).** Every depot instance carries a running-cost (OPEX) figure that is a function of its type and current size, data-driven, and exposed via a query. Directional: OPEX(S2) > OPEX(S1) for the same type, and the three types may differ in their per-size cost (a train repair yard is not priced the same as a school/store workshop). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/repair` shows an OPEX query (e.g. `OpEx(depotID)`); a passing test asserts OPEX(S2) > OPEX(S1) for a fixed type, and that the figures change when the loaded `buildings.json` fixture's per-size cost is edited (GR#15 data-driven) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Oo]pex\|func Test.*[Rr]unningCost" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`). False-pass risk: a hardcoded OPEX table in Go that happens to be monotonic today would pass a bare "S2 > S1" check while ignoring `buildings.json` — the check requires the fixture-edit assertion.

- **AC-4 (Coupling — depot of size N supports crew capacity M in its domain).** A query surface maps a domain to its aggregate crew capacity, computed from the depots placed in that domain: a depot of size N contributes a documented capacity M(N) (data-driven), and the domain's capacity is the aggregation of its depots' contributions. Directional: M(S3) > M(S2) > M(S1). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/repair CrewCapacity` (or `DomainCapacity(domain)`) exists; a passing test places a S1 depot and a S2 depot in the same domain and asserts the aggregate capacity equals M(S1) + M(S2) per the catalogue, and separately that M(S2) > M(S1) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]rewCapacity\|func Test.*[Dd]omainCapacity" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`). False-pass risk: a capacity surface that returns a fixed per-domain constant regardless of how many/sized depots exist would pass a bare "query returns a number" check — the check requires the aggregate to respond to added/upgraded depots.

- **AC-5 (Domain separation — a depot only extends its own domain).** A depot contributes crew capacity and reach to its own domain only: a road repair base affects `road`, never `rail` or `building`; a train repair yard affects `rail`; a school/store workshop affects `building`. Check: a passing test places a road depot and asserts the `rail` and `building` domain-capacity figures are unchanged by the placement, while `road` increases (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]omainSeparation\|func Test.*[Dd]omainIsolation" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`). False-pass risk: a single flat "maintenance capacity" pool that ignores domains would satisfy "placing a depot adds capacity" while silently letting a road depot service rail crews — the check requires the cross-domain no-op assertion.

- **AC-6 (GR#20; placement via `engine.build` — this feature consumes `BuildAPI`, never writes build state directly).** Depot placement and size-upgrade are submitted as build orders through `engine.build`'s registered `BuildAPI` (materials + labour + lead time per `engine.build.md` AC-4), and this module reads structure/zone state back through the same interface — it never writes the build queue or zone state directly. Check: `grep -n "build\.\|BuildAPI" internal/engine/repair/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds calls into the registered `engine.build` surface; `grep -rln "build\.(zoneState\|queue)\.\w\+ = " internal/engine/repair/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no direct exported-field write bypassing the interface. False-pass risk: a module that directly mutates the zone/build struct to "place" a depot would pass a bare "depots get placed" check while violating GR#20 — the check targets the write bypass.

- **AC-7 (Reach — a depot's coverage radius extends with size).** Each depot carries a reach figure (a coverage distance/radius within which a crew based at that depot can service domain jobs), data-driven and increasing with size; the domain's reachable coverage is derived from its depots' reach, not a citywide constant. Check: a passing test asserts a size-2 depot's reach radius is greater than a size-1 depot's, and that a domain with no depots reports no reachable coverage (zero, not "unbounded" or a placeholder "everywhere") (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]each" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`). False-pass risk: a reach figure that defaults to citywide when no depot exists would make depot siting meaningless — the check requires the zero-depot case to report zero reachable coverage.

### Error handling

- **AC-8 (GR#7).** Upgrading a depot already at its top rung, querying an unknown domain, or referencing a nonexistent depot ID returns a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code) — never a panic or a silent no-op the caller could mistake for success. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/repair/*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]axSizeUpgrade\|func Test.*[Uu]nknownDomain\|func Test.*[Nn]onexistentDepot" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly (BUG-100):** the test asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no depot was mutated as a side effect, not merely that a matching-named test function exists.

- **AC-9 (GR#7).** A malformed or schema-invalid depot catalogue entry (a depot type missing its domain tag, a size ladder with a non-monotonic OPEX/capacity sequence, an unrecognised domain string) produces a registry-sourced error at load time, never a silent default substitution that would mask a data-authoring bug as "depot works fine". Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/repair/*.go` finds a second distinct registry code reference; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]alformedDepot" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly (BUG-100):** the test asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no default-substituted depot entry was loaded in place of the malformed one.

### Determinism & safety

- **AC-10 (GR#21).** Domain-capacity and reach aggregation is a deterministic function of depot state (the set of placed depots, their sizes, and the loaded catalogue) — no `for range` over a Go map feeds a depot into the aggregation without a prior deterministic sort/ordering step, and repeated aggregation over an identical depot set produces byte-identical results. Check: `grep -rn "for .* := range" internal/engine/repair/*.go` — every map-range site that affects output is preceded by a `sort.` call (`grep -n "sort\." internal/engine/repair/*.go` confirms the ordering step); a passing determinism test asserts the same depot set aggregated twice yields the identical capacity/reach figures (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]eterminis" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go`).

- **AC-11 (SG-7 scoped; GR#21).** `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since" internal/engine/repair/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches — OPEX, capacity, and reach are functions of depot state and loaded data only, never wall clock.

- **AC-12.** `go test ./internal/engine/repair/... -race -count=1` passes with no data race when the domain-capacity/OPEX query surface is read concurrently with an upgrade command mutating a depot. Check: `grep -n "go func()" internal/engine/repair/*_test.go` finds at least one concurrency test.

### Documentation

Check: `grep -n "feat.repairfacilities" internal/engine/repair/doc.go` and `grep -n "§47\|section 47" internal/engine/repair/doc.go` and `grep -n "feat.maintenance\|opexintegration" internal/engine/repair/doc.go` all match.

## Out of scope

- The maintenance crews themselves, the per-instance aging/efficiency-decline/lifetime model, and the multi-fix crew dispatch logic — `feat.maintenance` (FEAT-089); this feature only supplies the depot reach/capacity surface the crew layer reads.
- Repair staffing (citizens as repair staff, citywide repair demand, off-map contractors) — `feat.staffing` (FEAT-090).
- The finance-side OPEX drain (folding maintenance + wages + materials into `engine.finance`'s ledger so the drain is visible) — `feat.opexintegration` (FEAT-094); this feature computes and exposes the OPEX figure and documents the seam, it does not wire the ledger.
- Heavy works and mega-facility-scale repair infrastructure — §47's "heavy works" rung and the mega-facility permit system (`feat.megafacilities`/`feat.facilitypermits`) are separate; these depots are explicitly *small* and top out at the local-depot scale.
- The materials/labour/lead-time mechanics of placing or upgrading a depot — `engine.build`'s own contract (`engine.build.md` AC-4); this feature consumes `BuildAPI`, it does not reimplement construction.
- Balance figures for the size ladder, M(N) crew capacity, reach radius, and OPEX — data-driven placeholders pending Aaron's approval (see Escalations, ASM below).

## Escalations

- **For Aaron — balance numbers (ASM, "Balance number regime" class).** The size-ladder rung count, the M(N) crew-capacity-per-size curve (directional placeholder: roughly 1 / 2 / 4 crews at S1 / S2 / S3), the reach radius per size, and the OPEX per type/size are all spec-silent and given only directionally here. Every AC above checks shape/monotonicity/data-driveness only, per GR#15. The concrete figures need Aaron's row-by-row approval and the M2 balance pass — do not let a junior hardcode these.

- **For Bill — code.json registration gap (BUG-058 class).** `feat.repairfacilities` has **no code.json module entry** today (not in the `feat.*` module list), and none of its three collaborators is registered either: `feat.maintenance`, `feat.staffing`, `feat.opexintegration` are all absent. The edges this feature needs, once registered, are: (a) `feat.repairfacilities` → `engine.build` (inbound consumer — precedent exists: `feat.facilitypermits` is already an `engine.build` consumer in code.json), (b) `feat.maintenance` → `feat.repairfacilities` (the crew layer consuming the reach/capacity surface), and (c) either `feat.repairfacilities` → `engine.finance` directly or — more likely, per FEAT-094's ownership — `feat.opexintegration` consuming the OPEX seam. Until these land, the ACs are written consumer-agnostic (testable against `RepairAPI` directly), matching `engine.rail.md`'s own stance on its empty consumers list. Recommend registering the `feat.repairfacilities` → `engine.build` edge at minimum before dispatch, since AC-6's BuildAPI check needs it to be legal.

- **For Bill — scope boundary with `engine.rail`'s maintenance depot (overlap flag).** §47 already names a "maintenance depot" in its stabling → depot → heavy-works ladder, and `engine.rail.md` AC-2/ASM-311 already cover fleet-size-scaled works capacity. Aaron's FEAT-092 design calls the rail-domain facility a "train repair yard" and frames *all three* depots as crew-side facilities ("extends the reach/capacity of the maintenance crews"). The boundary I have drawn: **this feature owns the growable crew-side depot and its reach/capacity/OPEX; `engine.rail` owns fleet-serviceability** (whether the fleet is maintained *well enough to run*). The two are related but distinct surfaces, and the "train repair yard" must not silently re-implement `engine.rail`'s works-capacity accounting. Confirm at dispatch which module owns the rail-depot *capacity* figure, since a junior could reasonably build the same number twice (GR#3).

- **For Bill — dependency readiness (stub-first).** `engine.build` (open), `engine.roads` (in-progress), `engine.rail` (open), and `engine.services` (open) are all still landing, and the three collaborating features are unbuilt. Recommend stub-first build against interface stubs with Tester re-verification once the real dependencies land, consistent with `engine.logistics.md`'s and `feat.disasters.md`'s identical escalations.
