## 1. Why this item exists

Aaron's stated goal is a **100 million** population city. This is the single piece of work that decides whether that is reachable, because the blocker is not land — it is that the tick cost is proportional to the number of buildings in the city rather than to the number of things that changed.

### 1.2 The arithmetic that makes this a P1

- 100M people at Aaron's current density mix ⇒ 100 × 15,701 ≈ **1.57 M buildings**.
- At 1.853 µs/building that is a **2.91 second tick**. Unplayable by two orders of magnitude.
- A playable budget of **100 ms/tick** buys 100 ms ÷ 1.853 µs ≈ **54,000 buildings** today. 100M needs **29×** that.
- **Space is already solved on paper.** A ×50 map is 14,300 km² (≈ Tokyo metro), holding 100M at ~7,000 people/km² — denser than London (5,700), sparser than Manhattan (28,000). Land is not the constraint.
- **The constraint is the O(buildings) tick.**

### 1.3 What "O(buildings)" means concretely today

`memoOnState` (`data.ts:2490-2498`) caches on the **`SimState` object identity**. That is a correct and deliberate design (its 30-line header explains why keying on a hand-enumerated field list rots), and it eliminates *repeated* walks within one unchanged state. But **the reducer returns a new top-level state object every tick**, so every memo misses once per tick and every walk runs again in full.

Counted in-tree on 2026-09-03: **26 `memoOnState(...)` wrappers** (24 in `data.ts`, 2 in `engine.ts`) plus **3 `WeakMap`-on-`s.buildings` caches** (`occupiedSet`, `roadTileSetOf`, `computeRoadConnectivity` at `data.ts:610-617`). Every one of them is a whole-array walk. Named, the hot ones are:

## 2. The design in one paragraph

Divide the map into **fixed 1000 m sectors** that nest exactly 5×5 into the consolidator's 200 m sections. Each sector holds a **`SectorAggregate`** — an integer-domain record of everything a citywide number is a sum of (population capacity, jobs by sector, service capacities, power draw and generation, waste generated and processed, counts by kind, tiles used). A sector's aggregate is recomputed **only when something inside it changed**, and the reducer — which already knows exactly which building it touched — hands the dirty set forward as a side-band keyed on the new state object. A citywide number becomes a **fold over sector aggregates**, not a walk over buildings. Because every aggregate field is an **integer** (or a fixed-point integer numerator in a registered unit), the fold is exactly associative and commutative, so the answer provably cannot depend on sector iteration order, and byte-identity with the current whole-city computation is achievable rather than aspirational. Aggregates are **derived, never persisted**; a load rebuilds them. The old whole-city path stays live as the oracle behind a comparison harness for at least a full milestone.

## 3. Sector partitioning (the ask, part **a**)

### AC-1 — the sector size is derived from promoted constants, never a literal

`TILE_METRES = 50` and `CONSOLIDATOR_SECTION_METRES = 200` are already specified as exports of `data.ts` by FEAT-2326609761 AC-3. This item adds:

```ts
export const SECTOR_METRES = 1000;
export const SECTOR_TILES = Math.round(SECTOR_METRES / TILE_METRES);            // 20
export const SECTOR_SECTIONS = Math.round(SECTOR_METRES / CONSOLIDATOR_SECTION_METRES); // 5
```

**No literal `20`, `1000` or `5` appears in any logic (GR#15).** A test asserts `SECTOR_TILES === 20`, `SECTOR_SECTIONS === 5`, that `SECTOR_METRES % CONSOLIDATOR_SECTION_METRES === 0` (the nesting invariant — see AC-3), and that changing `SECTOR_METRES` to `2000` yields `SECTOR_TILES === 40` with the sector grid recomputing accordingly.

If FEAT-2326609761 has not landed when this starts, this item promotes `TILE_METRES` itself and FEAT-2326609761 consumes it — **one** definition, whichever lands first (GR#3).

### AC-2 — the 1000 m size is derived from a cost model, and the derivation is recorded in the source

Per-tick cost under this design is

> **C(S) = D · (N / S_occ) · c + S_occ · f**

where `N` = buildings, `S_occ` = occupied sectors, `D` = dirty sectors this tick, `c` = 1.853 µs (the measured per-building cost, §1.1), `f` = the per-sector fold cost. Minimising over `S` gives **S\* = √(D · N)**.

| Scenario | N | D (ASM-1487) | S\* = √(D·N) | Sectors at `SECTOR_METRES = 1000` | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today's map, Aaron's city | 30 k | 50 | 1,225 | ceil(440/20) × ceil(260/20) = **22 × 13 = 286** | fold term negligible either way |
| ×5 map (~984 × 581 tiles) | 150 k | 50 | 2,739 | **50 × 30 = 1,500** | within 2× of optimum |
| ×50 map (~3,111 × 1,838 tiles, 14,300 km²) | 1.5 M | 100 | 12,247 | **156 × 92 = 14,352** | **within 17% of optimum** |

The ×50 row is the one that matters and 1000 m lands on it. The other rows are insensitive because the `S_occ · f` term is small there. **Crucially, `SECTOR_TILES` is tied to *land*, not to building count, so mean buildings-per-sector stays ≈ 100 at every scale in the table** — the design is scale-free in its inner loop, which is exactly the property that makes one fixed constant defensible instead of a runtime-tuned one.

The derivation above is reproduced as a comment block on `SECTOR_METRES`, with the measured `c` and its commit citation, so a future tuner can re-derive rather than guess (the `scale-gate.test.mjs` BOUND DERIVATION header is the precedent).

### AC-3 — sectors nest exactly into consolidator sections; neither owns the other

`SECTOR_METRES` is an exact multiple of `CONSOLIDATOR_SECTION_METRES`, so every 200 m section lies wholly inside exactly one 1000 m sector and `sectorKeyOf(x, y)` and `sectionKeyOf(x, y)` (FEAT-2326609761 AC-3) are consistent by construction. A test asserts, for every tile on the map, that all four corners of that tile's section map to the same sector key.

**They are deliberately different sizes and neither is derived from the other's grid.** The consolidator's 200 m is a *gameplay* constant (Aaron's words: "e.g. 200m x 200m" — a neighbourhood-scale planning unit). 1000 m is a *performance* constant derived in AC-2. Coupling them to one number would let a balance decision silently retune the tick budget, or vice versa. The nesting invariant is what they share; the values are independent. **ASM-1485.**

### AC-4 — the sector index is one memoised walk and holds only occupied sectors

```ts
sectorIndexOf(s: SimState): ReadonlyMap<number, SectorAggregate>
```

`memoOnState`-wrapped, walking `s.buildings` **exactly once** on a cold build, bucketing each building by `sectorKeyOf(b.x, b.y)` — its **origin tile**. A building whose footprint spans a sector boundary is owned entirely by its origin sector (the same rule FEAT-2326609761 ASM-1493 sets for sections). **Sectors with zero buildings are absent from the map**, so the index and every fold over it are O(occupied sectors), never O(14,352). On a sparsely-built ×50 map that is the difference between a 7 ms floor and a 0.1 ms floor.

Origin-ownership is sound here because **every aggregate in `SectorAggregate` is a whole-building quantity** (a building's jobs, residents, capacity, power draw all belong to the building, not to its tiles). It would *not* be sound for any future per-tile aggregate; **AC-25** forbids adding one without a halo rule.

### AC-5 — `SectorAggregate` is an explicit, inspectable, integer-domain record

```ts
interface SectorAggregate {
  key: number;              // sectorKeyOf
  x0: number; y0: number;   // sector origin tile
  buildingCount: number;
  tilesUsed: number;
  // LAYER S — structural: a pure function of this sector's buildings. See AC-9.
  s: {
    countByKind: Record<ZoneKind, number>;
    residentsCapacity: number;
    jobsBySector: SectorJobs;
    childrenCapacity: number;
    servedCapacity: number;
    serviceCapacity: ServiceCapacityAggregates;   // nursery/primary/tertiary/gp/hosp/police/fire/clean/waste
    parksCapacity: number;
    powerCap: number; powerNeed: number;
    waterCleanCap: number; waterWasteCap: number;
    wasteGenerated: number; collectionCapacity: number;
    processCapacities: ProcessCapacities;
  };
  // LAYER G — gated: the same sums restricted to isOnline() buildings. See AC-9.
  g: { /* identical shape to s */ };
  // Provenance — see AC-11.
  builtAtGlobalVersions: GlobalInputVersions;
}
```

Every numeric field is an **integer** (AC-15). The aggregate for the current pass's sectors is surfaced in the debug JSON (`debugjson.ts`) so Aaron can see what it saw. **Building the aggregate never mutates state.**

## 4. Dirty tracking (the ask, part **b** — the hard part)

### AC-6 — the reducer produces the dirty set; nothing infers it by comparison

The reducer already knows precisely which building each action touched. Every `reduceCore()` case that changes `s.buildings` also records the affected sector keys into a side-band:

```ts
// data.ts — module-level, NOT a SimState field (AC-19).
const dirtySectorsByState = new WeakMap<SimState, ReadonlySet<number>>();
export function dirtySectorsSince(next: SimState): ReadonlySet<number> | null;
```

keyed on the **new** state object, exactly the `memoOnState`/`occupiedSetCache` idiom already established. Deriving the dirty set by diffing two building arrays is explicitly rejected: it is O(N), which defeats the entire feature.

### AC-7 — absence of information means FULL recompute, never a stale answer

If `dirtySectorsSince(next)` returns `null` — a state built by a test, loaded from a savepoint, produced by `genesisReplay`, or constructed by any code path that predates or bypasses this feature — the implementation **rebuilds every sector from scratch**. Fail-safe, never fail-stale. This is the single most important safety property in the design and it is asserted directly: a test constructs a `SimState` by hand (no reducer involved), asks for a citywide number, and proves it equals the whole-city path.

### AC-8 — the exhaustive list of what marks a sector dirty

| Trigger | Reducer cases | Sectors marked | Layer invalidated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Place / `placeMany` / `stampRegion` | placement | origin sector of each placed building | S and G |
| Bulldoze | bulldoze | origin sector of each removed building | S and G |
| Relocate / move | `movingId` commit | origin sector of **both** the old and the new position | S and G |
| Auto-scale tier change | `evaluateBuildingMonitors` | that building's sector | S and G |
| Pipe-tier upgrade (`pipeTierOf`) | pipe upgrade | that building's sector | S and G |
| Construction completion (`s.tick - b.builtTick` crosses `constructionTicks(sp)`) | tick advance | see AC-10 | G only |
| Road-topology change (any road/motorway/rail/station/pylon placed or removed) | placement/bulldoze of a `CONNECT_EXEMPT_KINDS` spec | see AC-12 | G only (plus S for the road building itself) |
| `autoConnect` / `sweepOrphanConnects` synthesised roads | `engine.ts:2029`, `engine.ts:2214` | same as road-topology change | G only |
| Anything else | — | **nothing** | — |

That last row is the load-bearing one. Population change, money flows, wellbeing, crime, demand, brownout, policy toggles, congestion and every other per-tick scalar movement **do not dirty any sector**, because no `SectorAggregate` field depends on them. AC-9 is what makes that true, and AC-11 is what stops it silently ceasing to be true.

### AC-9 — the layer split is what keeps non-local effects from invalidating everything

Non-local effects are the place a naive design breaks: a road change can connect or disconnect buildings anywhere; a power plant feeds the whole grid. The answer is to split the aggregate so a global input invalidates only the layer that reads it.

- **Layer S** is a pure function of `(this sector's buildings, SPECS)`. It reads **no** global input at all. It is therefore invalidated *only* by the structural triggers in AC-8 — never by a road change, never by a tick, never by anything citywide.
- **Layer G** is Layer S's sums restricted by `isOnline(s, b)`. `computeIsOnline` (`data.ts:501-545`) reads exactly three things: `b.builtTick` + `constructionTicks(sp)` (construction), `isRoadAdjacent` (local — a tile-neighbourhood test), and `isRoadConnected` (**global** — membership of `s.roadConnectivity.connectedRoadTiles`). Verified 2026-09-03: **power/brownout is NOT an online gate** (`brownoutOf` is a separate citywide scalar and `computeIsOnline` never reads it), and nothing else in the state is read. So Layer G's only global input is road connectivity, and its only time input is `s.tick` vs `builtTick`.
- **Layer C** — every citywide scalar (population, brownout, congestion factor, wellbeing, crime, demand) — is computed *after* the fold, from folded totals plus `SimState` scalars, in exactly the code that computes it today. It is applied at read time, not pushed back down into per-building recomputation.

**A power plant "feeding the whole grid" costs nothing under this design** precisely because power is a fold (`powerCap`/`powerNeed` summed to a citywide `powerStats`), not a per-building gate. If a future feature makes brownout an online gate, that becomes a Layer-G-wide invalidation on every brownout transition — an honest O(N) event, rare, and it must land with a bound and a test. **ASM-1486.**

### AC-10 — construction completion dirties a bounded, precomputed set

Buildings complete construction at a *known future tick*: `builtTick + constructionTicks(sp)`. The sector index therefore carries a **completion schedule** — a `Map<tick, Set<sectorKey>>` maintained on placement (one insert per placed building) and consumed on tick advance. At tick `T` the dirty set gains exactly the sectors in `schedule.get(T)`, which is empty on the overwhelming majority of ticks. **No scan of `buildings` for "is anyone finishing" is permitted**; a test asserts that advancing 100 ticks over a city with no completions marks zero sectors dirty.

### AC-11 — global-input versioning makes staleness structurally impossible

`GlobalInputVersions` is a small record of monotonically increasing counters — today exactly one entry, `roadConnectivityVersion`, bumped whenever `computeRoadConnectivity` produces a set that differs from the previous one. Every cached `SectorAggregate` stores the versions it was built under (`builtAtGlobalVersions`, AC-5). Reading a sector whose stored version is behind the current version is a **hard error**, not a silent recompute: the read path asserts the sector was refreshed by AC-12's diff and `recordError`s (GR#1, registry code per GR#7 via `tools/plan/add-error.js`) if it was not.

Adding a new global input without adding it to `GlobalInputVersions` is caught by **AC-25**'s structural test.

### AC-12 — a road change re-gates only the sectors whose connectivity actually flipped

The naive rule ("road change ⇒ all sectors dirty") is correct but degrades to a full recompute on every road placement, which in a growing city is most ticks. The real design exploits what `computeRoadConnectivity` already returns:

1. `computeRoadConnectivity(s)` (`data.ts:611`) returns `{ connectedRoadTiles: string[] }` — the flood-fill remains O(road tiles) and is unchanged by this item.
2. Diff the new connected-tile set against the previous one → `ΔT`, the tiles that gained or lost connectivity. `ΔT` is empty for a road placed on an already-connected stub, and small for most placements.
3. Map `ΔT` to sectors: a tile at `(x, y)` can only change `isRoadConnected` for buildings **adjacent to it**, so the affected sectors are the sectors of `ΔT` expanded by a one-tile halo — bounded by `|ΔT| · 4` sector lookups.
4. Mark those sectors **Layer-G dirty only**. Layer S is untouched.

Worst case — a single bulldozed road tile severing a trunk and disconnecting half the city — `ΔT` is genuinely large and the cost is genuinely proportional to it. That is correct behaviour, not a defect, and it is **bounded by the size of the connectivity change, not by the city**. A test builds exactly that scenario and asserts (i) the answer is identical to the whole-city path and (ii) the dirty count equals the halo of `ΔT` and not `S_occ`.

**inc3 may ship the naive "road change ⇒ all sectors Layer-G dirty" rule** to get the family landed; **inc4 replaces it with the diff.** Both are correct; only the second is fast. The increment split (§10) is explicit about this so nobody mistakes the interim rule for the design.

### AC-13 — a periodic self-audit catches staleness in production, loudly

Equality tests cannot catch "the cache went stale in a way only a 400-tick action sequence produces". So:

- **Test/CI mode:** the comparison harness (AC-17) recomputes the whole-city path after **every** tick and asserts equality.
- **Production:** every `SECTOR_AUDIT_INTERVAL_TICKS` (placeholder 300, ≈ 10 game months) the full whole-city path is recomputed and compared. A mismatch `recordError`s a registry code (GR#1/GR#7), surfaces in the errors panel, and **falls back to the whole-city path for the remainder of the session** — degrade to slow-and-correct, never continue fast-and-wrong. GR#17: the audit's own failure to *run* also writes a registry error.
- The audit is off by default only if Aaron rules it too expensive; the recommendation is **on**, because 300 ticks of amortisation makes it under 0.2 ms/tick equivalent at 30 k buildings.

## 6. Exactness (the ask, part **d**)

### AC-16 — integer domain is what makes byte-identity possible at all

This is the crux, and it is why the design is what it is.

Floating-point addition is **not associative**: `(a+b)+c ≠ a+(b+c)` in general. A fold over sectors necessarily re-associates the sum relative to today's left-to-right walk over `s.buildings`. **If the aggregates were floats, byte-identity with the current computation would be mathematically impossible**, and the whole "prove it's identical" acceptance criterion would collapse into "prove it's within some ULP bound" — a far weaker and far more argued-about property.

Therefore: **every `SectorAggregate` field is an integer.** IEEE-754 doubles represent every integer up to 2⁵³ exactly, and integer addition within that range is exactly associative *and* commutative. The fold's result is then **provably independent of sector iteration order**, and identical to any other summation order including today's.

Headroom check: the largest plausible aggregate is citywide residents capacity at 100 M ≈ 10⁸, or a micro-unit money quantity at 10⁸ × 10⁶ = 10¹⁴. 2⁵³ ≈ 9.0 × 10¹⁵. **Two orders of magnitude of headroom at the 100 M goal.** An `assertIntegerDomain` test asserts every aggregate field is `Number.isSafeInteger` on the real savepoint and on the 1.5 M-building fixture, and a dedicated test proves a deliberately-overflowing synthetic city trips a loud registry error rather than silently losing precision.

### AC-17 — fractional quantities are carried as fixed-point integers, or are not partitioned

- A quantity that is genuinely fractional is carried as an **integer numerator in a unit registered in `code.json`'s `units` section** (the micropound precedent; `/units-lint` must pass — GR#15). The division happens **once**, at the top, in the same expression that computes it today, so the final float operations are bit-identical.
- A quantity that **cannot** be integerised is **not partitioned**. It stays on the whole-city path, with a comment saying so and a named entry in a `NOT_PARTITIONED` list. inc1 must publish that list with a measured cost for each entry, so the residue is visible rather than discovered late. Current expectation: the residue is small (the ratio-producing wrappers — `collectionCoverageOf`, `diversionRateOf`, `unemploymentOf`, `congestionFactorOf` — all divide *folded* integers and so partition cleanly by AC-14).

### AC-18 — the differential harness, and what it runs against

```ts
// test/partition-differential.mjs
compareAllDerivations(s: SimState): { field: string; whole: unknown; partitioned: unknown }[]
```

Runs **both** implementations of **every** derived value listed in §1.3 and compares with a canonical serialisation that distinguishes `-0` from `0`, `NaN` from `NaN`, and preserves `Map`/`Set` insertion order, then `assert.deepStrictEqual`. Any non-empty result fails.

Corpora:

1. **Seeded random cities** — ≥ 500 cities across 3 orders of magnitude (100 / 3 k / 30 k buildings), seeded so a failure is reproducible from its seed alone, spanning: zero buildings of a kind, all-offline cities, disconnected-road cities, mid-construction cities, cities with sector-spanning footprints on every boundary, and cities occupying exactly one sector.
2. **The scale fixture** — `test/scale/fixture.mjs` at 13 k buildings / 1.42 M population, **after** the BUG-653 and BUG-654 repairs of AC-22.
3. **Aaron's real savepoint** — `C:\Users\aarongarcia\.claude\jobs\f9ac9353\tmp\aaron-savepoint.lz` (550,307 bytes, verified present 2026-09-03), decoded via `saveCodec.decode()` (the `LZv1:` prefixed `compressToUTF16` format, `saveCodec.ts:37`). See **Q3** — this file is outside the repo and is Aaron's private city; the recommendation is an **env-gated local-only test** (`METRO_AARON_SAVEPOINT=<path>`) plus a repo-committed synthetic fixture *reconstructed to match its shape distribution* for CI, rather than committing the save itself.
4. **A 200-tick scripted action stream** on each of the above — place, `placeMany`, bulldoze, relocate, road sever, road reconnect, tier upgrade, pipe upgrade, construction completion boundary, save→load→continue — comparing **after every tick**, not just at the end. This is the corpus that catches staleness; the others catch cold-build arithmetic.

### AC-19 — the harness must be proven capable of failing (RED proof)

Mandatory, per the project's verification standard. Three separately-reintroduced defects, each proven to redden the harness and then reverted via a scratch copy (never a tree-destroying git command — GR#24):

1. An **off-by-one** in one `SectorAggregate` sum.
2. A **missing dirty trigger** — delete the relocate case from AC-8 and prove the 200-tick stream reddens (this is the staleness class; if it does not redden, the corpus is inadequate and must be extended until it does).
3. A **float aggregate** — change one integer field to accumulate a float and prove AC-16's order-independence test reddens.

### AC-20 — order independence is asserted directly, not merely reasoned about

A test folds the same sector index in ascending key order, descending key order, and a seeded shuffle, and asserts all three produce **byte-identical** `CityAggregate` records. This is the mechanical proof of AC-16's reasoning, and it is the test that will catch the day someone adds a float field.

## 7. Save and replay (the ask, part **e**)

### AC-21 — sector aggregates are NOT persisted, and `SimState` gains no field

**Recommendation: do not persist.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. A persisted aggregate is a **second source of truth for a derived value** (GR#3). It cannot be validated against anything at load time without doing the full recompute that persisting was meant to avoid.
2. It would enter the **save format and the journal**, requiring a migration for every existing dogfood save and a version bump — for a value that rebuilds in one walk.
3. A stale persisted aggregate is **unrecoverable and undetectable** in a way a rebuilt one never is.

Concretely: `sectorIndexOf`'s cache, the dirty side-band, the completion schedule and `GlobalInputVersions` all live in **module-level `WeakMap`s keyed on state objects** (the `memoOnState` idiom), **not** in `SimState`. A cold load rebuilds by AC-7's `null` → full-recompute path.

Rebuild cost is exactly one whole-array walk — 55 ms at Aaron's city, once, on a load path that already runs a chunked replay with a progress overlay (BUG-617, `75e7891`). It is not on the critical path.

### AC-22 — replay byte-identity is proven, not assumed

Because `SimState` gains no field, the journal bytes and savepoint bytes are unchanged. Prove it:

- The same action stream, run with the feature flag **off** and **on**, produces `createSavepoint()` payloads that are **byte-identical strings** (compare pre-`encode()` JSON, and again post-`encode()`).
- A genesis replay (`genesisReplay.ts`) of the same journal under both flag states produces byte-identical final states, chunked and unchunked (the BUG-617/BUG-631 divergence class — a replay that conjured an extra clinic — is exactly the failure mode to guard here; **`setReplayMode` interactions are in scope for this test**).
- A savepoint written before this feature lands, loaded after, produces the identical city.

## 8. The perf contract (the ask, part **f**)

### AC-23 — the per-tick budget table

`c = 1.853 µs/building/tick` measured (§1.1); `f = 0.5 µs/sector` assumed (ASM-1488, must be measured).

| Scale | Map | Sectors | Buildings/sector | **Today** | **Target: steady state** (D ≈ 0–5) | **Target: sustained build burst** (D per ASM-1487) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **30 k** | ×1 (440×260) | 286 | ~105 | 55.29 ms *(measured)* | **≤ 3 ms** | **≤ 15 ms** (D=50 → 50·105·1.853 µs = 9.7 ms + fold) |
| **150 k** | ×5 (984×581) | 1,500 | ~100 | ~278 ms *(extrapolated)* | **≤ 4 ms** | **≤ 25 ms** (D=50 → 9.3 ms + 0.75 ms fold) |
| **1.5 M** | ×50 (3111×1838) | 14,352 | ~104 | ~2,910 ms *(extrapolated)* | **≤ 12 ms** (fold-dominated, 7.2 ms) | **≤ 120 ms** (D=100 → 19 ms + 7.2 ms fold) |

Steady-state targets are the fold cost plus margin. Burst targets are `D · B_s · c + S_occ · f` plus ~50% margin.

**Read the two target columns together — they are the honest story of this feature.** In a *mature or paused* city the tick collapses from 55 ms to under 3 ms, a **20×+** win, because nothing is dirty. Under *sustained maximum building*, `D` scales with the build rate and the win is a **~5×** constant factor, not an asymptotic one. §11 draws the conclusion.

### AC-24 — the CI gate is a same-run RATIO gate, and it inherits neither BUG-653 nor BUG-654

The existing `test/scale-gate.test.mjs` bounds absolute wall-clock at ~4× a CI-measured figure (`TICK_MEDIAN_BOUND_MS = 400`), which **BUG-654** records as letting a 5–20× regression through. That looseness exists only because CI hardware is ~7.3× slower than local and an absolute bound must straddle both. This gate does not have that problem:

1. **Ratio, same run, same fixture, same process.** Measure the whole-city path and the partitioned path back-to-back and assert
   `median(partitioned) · MIN_SPEEDUP ≤ median(wholeCity)`
   with `MIN_SPEEDUP` per scale: **≥ 10×** at 30 k steady state, **≥ 4×** under the AC-23 burst profile. The hardware factor cancels exactly, so the bound can be tight without flaking. **This is the fix BUG-654 asks for, and this gate must ship with it from day one rather than inheriting the loose form.**
2. **The fixture must exercise the road path (BUG-653).** Every fixture building gets a `builtTick`, spread across a range so both mid-construction and completed buildings exist, and the fixture builds a real road network so `isRoadAdjacent`/`isRoadConnected` are actually evaluated. Asserted structurally: a test counts `computeIsOnline` entries reaching the road branch and requires it to be a majority of buildings. Without this the gate is blind to Layer G entirely.
3. **The fixture must contain water plants and consolidatable clusters (BUG-644 / FEAT-2326609761 AC-39's lesson).** A fixture with ~0 water plants could never have caught BUG-642; a fixture with no clustered infrastructure cannot catch a Layer-S regression.
4. **A per-selector budget table**, the BUG-644 precedent already in `scale-gate.test.mjs`, so one regressed derivation cannot hide inside a green total.
5. **The structural gate that actually protects this feature** — a correctness-preserving degradation to "everything dirty" is invisible to every equality test in §6 and would silently return the tick to O(buildings). So: after a scripted single-building placement at **every** scale in AC-23, assert `dirtySectorsSince(next).size === 1` exactly; after a scripted road placement on an already-connected stub, assert it is `0`; after 100 ticks with no completions, assert `0` (AC-10). **These assertions are the gate. Without them the perf gate can go green on a feature that has quietly stopped working.**

## 9. Structural guards

### AC-25 — adding a global input or a per-tile aggregate must fail closed

Two structural tests, because both are silent-staleness generators:

- **New global input:** a test enumerates the fields `computeIsOnline` and every Layer-G predicate actually read (by AST inspection of the source, the `tool.astgate` precedent) and asserts the set equals `GlobalInputVersions`' keys. Adding a read without adding a version counter reddens.
- **New per-tile aggregate:** a test asserts every `SectorAggregate` field is derived by the origin-ownership rule (AC-4) and reddens on any field whose computation touches a tile coordinate outside the owning building's origin sector. A per-tile or radius-based aggregate requires a halo rule and a separate BA pass.

### AC-26 — GR#21 determinism discipline

No `for (const x of someMap) { ... break; }` anywhere in the new code — the project's recurring nondeterminism blind spot. Every iteration is an order-independent fold (AC-16 makes this provable, not asserted) or a walk over an explicitly sorted array. No `Date.now()`, no `Math.random()`, no storage read in any partition code path (BUG-602's lesson: one `localStorage.getItem` per building was 95% of a tick).

## 11. The honest ceiling, and the recommendation

**Asked plainly: does O(changed) alone get 100M people to a playable tick in JavaScript? No. It is necessary and it is not sufficient — and the binding constraint is memory, not time.**

**1. O(changed) is a constant-factor win under growth, not an asymptotic one.** Per AC-23, a mature or paused city goes 20×+ faster because nothing is dirty. But under sustained maximum building the dirty count `D` scales with the build rate, and the win settles at ~5×. 1.5 M buildings under sustained building lands at ~26 ms — inside budget, actually — so *time* is survivable.

**2. Memory is not.** 1.5 M plain JS building objects at ~200 bytes of V8 overhead each is ~300 MB, before the `buildingDisplayStates` `Map` of 1.5 M entries (~100 MB+), the 26 memoised derived structures, the journal, and the savepoint. Realistically **0.6–1.0 GB of live heap** for one tab that must also hold a browser. Worse, the savepoint: Aaron's 30 k-building city compresses to **550 KB** (measured, §6 AC-18). Linear extrapolation puts 1.5 M buildings at **~27 MB compressed — 5.5× over the entire 5 MB `localStorage` quota**, which `saveCodec` exists to fit under in the first place. **A 1.5 M-building city cannot be saved by the current webconsole at any tick speed.**

**3. Therefore density is mandatory, not an alternative.** At a ~10× rise in people-per-building — the combined target of the consolidator (FEAT-2326609761) and ultra-dense employment towers (FEAT-2326609763) — 100 M needs **~157,000 buildings**, not 1.57 M. At 157 k: ~35 MB of building objects, a ~3 MB savepoint (inside quota, at the edge), and by AC-23's table a steady-state tick of ~4 ms and a build-burst tick of ~25 ms. **That is a playable 100 M city.**

### Recommendation (not a survey)

1. **Build this item.** It is the prerequisite: without it, 157 k buildings is still a 291 ms tick and the game is unplayable long before density lands.
2. **Treat FEAT-2326609761 (consolidator) and FEAT-2326609763 (ultra-dense towers) as on the 100 M critical path, not as flavour.** They are the memory and storage answer, and no amount of tick optimisation substitutes for them. Recommend a BOW dependency link from this item's 100 M claim to both.
3. **Do NOT move the heavy path to the Go engine for this.** The webconsole sim is the SSOT for the city Aaron actually plays; porting the derivations to Go duplicates ~29 derivation functions across two languages (GR#3), creates a byte-identity obligation across a language boundary far harder than the one in §6, and buys a constant factor that density supplies for free. Revisit only if, after inc6 *and* density, measurement still shows the tick over budget — and then as a fresh BA pass with `code.json` edges registered first (GR#25).
4. **The GPU deep-dive (`docs/planning/proposals/gpu-acceleration-deepdive-2026-09-03.md`) is complementary, not competing.** It addresses *render* cost (13 k buildings × ~3 Canvas2D draw calls × 20 fps ≈ 780 k calls/second); this item addresses *derivation* cost. Both are real and neither substitutes for the other. At 157 k buildings the render path needs its instancing work as much as the tick needs this.
5. **Add one storage follow-up now:** a ~3 MB savepoint at 157 k buildings sits at the `localStorage` quota edge. A move to IndexedDB is a separate P2 item and should be raised before density lands, not after a player loses a city to a quota failure.
