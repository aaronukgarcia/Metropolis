**Design (Aaron, 2026-08-15, BOW FEAT-102):** an oil refinery + integrated chemical works as a **distinct modelled facility** — crude by tanker to tank farm to refinery (fuel + feedstock) to petrochemical works to plastics — carrying top blight class and the hazmat fire category, with **make-vs-buy at its largest scale** as the headline decision: no refinery means importing refined product at margin and skipping the chain.

It does **not** re-specify MOD-063's five-stage chain, pipeline network, leak risk, or import-margin machinery (ASM-702), MOD-062's fuel fleet/duty/EV machinery, `engine.freight`'s port/tanker accounting, or `engine.dispatch`'s incident curves.

## North-star vetting (dev-team-process.md v1.12 BA mandate)

- **Q5 (long bet) — the headline, and §50's own words.** "Sell raw now or invest in the works and sell refined dear later" is the make-vs-buy doctrine at its largest scale. Building the refinery is a huge, permit-gated, blight-heavy, decommission-liability-carrying capex bet against an always-available import-at-margin alternative. AC-3 requires that bet to have both directions reachable from real data — never a strictly-dominant option.
- **Q1 (conflicting demand) — sharpened.** Refinery capex + land + permit is a pound not spent on housing, transit, wellbeing, or the next district — and the top-blight footprint *spends* neighbour goodwill at the same time.
- **Q2 (felt no) — both directions.** Build and you accept top blight + hazmat fire + a clean-balance-sheet liability; import and you pay the margin forever and forgo the value-add and the fuel-security it would bring.
- **Q3 (consequence sticks) — directly.** The decommission liability accrues from day one (§7), and the top-blight "max" footprint + hazmat-fire risk is a standing, queryable hazard (AC-7), not a one-time flavour tag.
- **Q4 (snowball) — the chain head.** The refinery is the upstream rung that unlocks petrochemical works → plastics → rubber → tyre plant; building it is the step that makes the rest of the §50 chain domestic rather than imported.

## User stories

- **US-1.** As the player, I need the refinery + integrated chemical works as a distinct, data-sourced facility (crude by tanker to tank farm to refinery → fuel + feedstock → petrochemical works → plastics), so it is a specific modelled facility, not one shared "chemicals" row.
- **US-2.** As the player deciding make-vs-buy, I need importing refined product at margin to be a real, always-available alternative to building the refinery, with the trade-off a genuine decision (neither strictly dominant) at the facility level.
- **US-3.** As `engine.freight`/§33, I need crude to arrive by tanker to the tank farm as ordinary freight tonnage, so the refinery consumes the existing port/tanker machinery rather than a refinery-local port.
- **US-4.** As `engine.fuel` (MOD-062), I need the refinery's fuel output to feed the fuel system's supply (tanker → forecourts → duty), so the refinery is the fuel system's upstream, not a parallel fuel source.
- **US-5.** As `engine.dispatch`/`engine.mining`, I need the refinery's top-blight "max" class and hazmat-fire category to be real, queryable incident-risk inputs, so a refinery fire dispatches like any other fire and the blight footprint follows the real blight model.
- **US-6.** As the player placing it, I need the refinery permit-gated (FEAT-053) and decommission-liability-carrying (FEAT-054), so it is a real lifecycle commitment like every other large facility.

## Scope

The `refinery` + `petrochemical_works` catalogue rows enriched into distinct, typed, data-sourced facilities (footprint/throughput/jobs/utility-draw); the make-vs-buy decision at facility level (build vs import-at-margin, directionally); the crude-tanker→tank-farm→refinery flow as ordinary freight tonnage; the refinery-as-chain-head wiring (refinery → petrochemical → plastics via MOD-063's `ChemAPI`); the fuel-output feed to MOD-062's `FuelAPI`; the top-blight/hazmat wiring to `engine.dispatch` + `engine.mining`'s `BlightAPI`; and the permit (FEAT-053) / decommission (FEAT-054) inheritance. Does **not** include: MOD-063's five-stage chain machinery, pipeline network, leak risk, and import-margin economics; MOD-062's fuel fleet composition, duty erosion, EV transition, and forecourt coverage; `engine.freight`'s port/berth/customs accounting; or `engine.dispatch`'s incident-outcome curves.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1 (GR#3/GR#20; distinct modelled facilities extending the existing catalogue rows, not a fork).** The existing `refinery` and `petrochemical_works` rows in `data/buildings.json` (supplementCategory `"fuel-chemicals"`, section `SUP3`) gain **typed per-type behavioural modelling** — footprint, throughput t/day, jobs, utility draw — rather than a second parallel facility registry, a parallel JSON, or second rows. Their `blightClass` (`"max"` for the refinery, `"high"` for the works), `unlock`, and `section` remain single-sourced in `data/buildings.json` (GR#3: this feature does not re-declare them). Check: `grep -n "refinery\|petrochemical_works" data/buildings.json` still resolves the one existing row each; `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` (or the feature's package doc) shows the refinery and petrochemical works as distinct facility types with their own footprint/throughput/jobs/utility-draw fields; a passing test asserts the refinery and the petrochemical works resolve to **distinct** behavioural profiles, and that the refinery's throughput/jobs differ from the works' (they are different chain stages, not one shared row) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Dd]istinct\|func Test.*[Ff]acility.*[Pp]rofiles" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a build that leaves the thin catalogue rows alone and puts a "refinery" label on a generic chain-stage struct would pass a shallow "a refinery exists" read while providing none of the per-type modelling — the check requires the per-type fields to exist and differ between the two stages.
- **AC-2 (GR#15; balance-number placeholders — every figure in the data file, none in code).** The refinery/petrochemical-works capex/opex/throughput/jobs/utility-draw figures are all sourced from a data file (`data/refinery.json`, ASM-704), none hardcoded as Go numeric literals, and each numeric entry carries a non-empty placeholder/pending-tuning disclosure field per the balance-number regime. Check: `grep -rn "0\.[0-9]\+\|[0-9]\{2,\}" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go` and array indices/enum ordinals) shows no bare balance-shaped numeric literals; the package doc names the data file path explicitly; a passing test edits a throughput figure in the data file between two runs with the same seed and asserts the facility's derived throughput changes (proving the parameter is actually read) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Dd]ataDriven\|func Test.*[Dd]ataDriven.*[Rr]efinery" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

### Functional — make-vs-buy at facility level (the headline)

- **AC-3 (§50/§6 make-vs-buy — a genuine decision, directionally, not a pinned value).** Building the refinery (capex + permit + blight + decommission liability) and importing refined product at margin (no capex, but margin paid per unit) are two **independently queryable** paths to satisfying the city's fuel/feedstock/plastics demand, and the data must admit **both** directions being rational — neither strictly dominates at all scales. This feature exposes the facility-level decision (the refinery's build cost and its domestic refined-product cost vs the import-at-margin cost, the latter reusing MOD-063's own import path and ASM-321's margin data — ASM-703); it does **not** reimplement the import-at-margin economics (that is `engine.chemicals.md` AC-4's). Check: a passing directional test asserts (a) a city with no refinery can satisfy plastics/fuel-chain demand via import at the documented margin, and (b) at a documented placeholder demand/scale, the domestic refined path is strictly cheaper per unit than import (so build wins at scale), while at a low placeholder scale the import path is not dominated by build (so import stays rational at small scale) — the two directions are both reachable from the data, never one hardcoded winner (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]akeVsBuy.*[Rr]efinery\|func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Mm]akeVsBuy\|func Test.*[Nn]otDominant" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`); `grep -n "import\|margin" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows the import-cost read going through the registered MOD-063 surface, not a refinery-local import-price table. **False-pass risk:** a build that hardcodes "always build" (import never cheaper) or "always import" (build capex never repaid) would pass a shallow "there is a make-vs-buy choice" read while gutting §50's "the make-vs-buy doctrine at its largest scale" — the check requires the *data* to make both directions reachable, the same non-degeneracy discipline `feat.commoditymarket.md` AC-9 applies to its refine-vs-export decision.
- **AC-4 (§50/§33 crude arrives by tanker as freight tonnage — GR#20, not a refinery-local port).** Crude supply to the tank farm is consumed as ordinary freight tonnage through the registered chain/freight edge (`engine.chemicals`→`engine.freight`, per `engine.chemicals.md` AC-2's "crude-oil tanker landings ... ordinary freight tonnage"), not a refinery-local port/berth/tank-farm model. Check: `grep -n "freight\.\|FreightAPI\|tanker" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows the crude-supply read going through the registered freight edge; `grep -rn "berth\|craneRate\|customs" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no local port-capacity reimplementation; a passing test constrains crude throughput (via the freight edge) and asserts the refinery's output falls proportionally rather than continuing unconstrained (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]rude.*[Tt]anker\|func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Ii]nputBound" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

### Functional — the chain head and the fuel feed

- **AC-5 (GR#20; the refinery is the head of the registered chain, not a parallel chain).** The refinery's feedstock output feeds the petrochemical works and onward plastics stages through MOD-063's registered `ChemAPI` chain-stage machinery (the same "each stage a firm with input t/day → output t/day" surface `engine.chemicals.md` AC-2 specifies) — this feature registers the refinery/petrochemical works as chain stages against that machinery, it does not author a second, refinery-owned chain with its own tonnage accounting. Check: `grep -n "ChemAPI\|RegisterStage\|chemicals\." internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows the stages registered through the `ChemAPI` machinery; a passing test asserts the refinery's feedstock output is queryable as the petrochemical works' input through the same surface, with tonnage conserved (input ≤ upstream routed output, matching `engine.chemicals.md` AC-3's identity) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]hainHead\|func Test.*[Ff]eedstock.*[Pp]etrochemical" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a refinery that produces fuel/feedstock into a refinery-local buffer that the rest of the §50 chain never reads would pass a "the refinery makes feedstock" grep while breaking the chain — the check requires the output to resolve through `ChemAPI`'s own stage surface.
- **AC-6 (GR#20; fuel output feeds `engine.fuel`'s `FuelAPI`, not a parallel fuel source).** The refinery's fuel output is the upstream supply the fuel system consumes (tanker truck → forecourts → fuel duty, per §49), expressed through the registered `engine.fuel` edge — this feature feeds MOD-062's fuel-supply surface, it does not reimplement fuel distribution, duty, or the EV transition. Check: `grep -n "fuel\.\|FuelAPI" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows the fuel-output call into the registered fuel edge; `grep -rn "fuelDuty\|forecourt\|charging" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no fuel-system reimplementation; a passing test cuts the refinery's fuel output (e.g. via constrained crude, AC-4) and asserts the fuel system's own supply figure (read through `FuelAPI`) degrades (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]uel.*[Ff]eed\|func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Ff]uelSupply" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **Dependency note:** whether `feat.refinery`'s own registered edge set includes `engine.fuel` directly, or the feed flows through MOD-063's existing machinery, is an open registration question — see ES-1/ASM-701.

### Functional — top blight + hazmat fire (wired, not narrated)

### Error handling

- **AC-9 (GR#7).** Building or operating the refinery against an unknown facility key, a malformed data file (missing throughput figure, negative utility draw, an unrecognised stage name), routing feedstock to a stage that doesn't exist yet, or feeding fuel before the refinery is built, produces a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code) and no partial state (no facility created, no stage registered, no fuel emitted) — never a panic or a silently-created zero-value stage. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` finds registry code references; passing test coverage asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no facility/stage/fuel state was mutated on failure, not merely that a matching-named test function exists (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Uu]nknownRefinery\|func Test.*[Mm]alformedRefinery\|func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Rr]eject" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

### Determinism & safety

Check: `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since\|rand.New\|math/rand\"" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches; a passing determinism test runs an identical refinery-build-and-operate sequence twice and asserts byte-identical stage/tonnage/fuel/risk state, and asserts a different seed produces a measurably different outcome set (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]eterminis.*[Rr]efinery\|func Test.*[Rr]efinery.*[Dd]eterminis" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

Check: `grep -n "float32\|float64" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` (excluding `_test.go` and comments) finds no monetary/tonnage field; `grep -n "foundation/num\|satAdd\|sat\.Add" internal/engine/chemicals/refinery*.go` shows the saturating-arithmetic route matching `engine.finance.md` AC-2 and `engine.maintenance.md` AC-6's standard.
