## User stories

- **US-1.** As the player, I need the finite monthly budget to be a real trade-off: diverting funds from Police & Clearance to Education/Wellbeing must raise crime while easing those sectors, and the reverse must lower crime while tightening them — so "safe but uneducated" vs "educated but crime-ridden" is an observable choice, not a cosmetic set of sliders.
- **US-2.** As a cash-strapped city with spare jail capacity, I need an "Import Out-of-Tile Inmates" contract I can toggle, paying a fixed monthly rate per imported inmate, so spare cells become a revenue lever — and I need the risk (riots/escapes/recidivism under low staffing) to make it a gamble, not free money.
- **US-3.** As the simulation, I need a single bounded Death-Spiral score S in [0,1] derived each tick from real live signals (crime, refuse/blight, uncollected dead bodies, burnt-down structures, fiscal distress), so the city's trajectory is readable at a glance and the spiral's severity is a number, not a narrative.
- **US-4.** As the player watching the spiral, I need S > 0.2 to slow growth, S > 0.6 to suppress attraction and start emigration, and S ≥ 0.8 for a consecutive run of months to warn and then end the city with a distinct game-over — each threshold an escalating, reversible-until-the-end consequence.
- **US-5.** As `code.json`, I need FEAT-142's cross-module reads (refuse→spiral, wellbeing→spiral, prison revenue→finance) to arrive through registered edges or documented seams — never unregistered direct calls (GR#25).

## Scope

The sector-budget trade-off (police vs education vs wellbeing) observable through each module's existing funding surface; prison spare-capacity accounting plus the import contract (toggle, imported-inmate line, per-inmate payout, staffing-linked riot/escape risk looping back into crime); the Death-Spiral score S (bounded [0,1], data-sourced weights, three escalation thresholds, consecutive-month death gate wired to WarningLedger with a distinct verdict). Not in scope: re-speccing the four modules' existing acceptance files, or the master-doc §12 amendment / `ErrGhostCity` rename (both queued per ASM-1232/ASM-1233).

### A. The resource-allocation trade-off (police / MI5 / education / wellbeing)

- **AC-A1 (the trade-off is real and observable through each sector's EXISTING funding surface).** The finite monthly budget, shifted between Police & Clearance and Social Infrastructure, produces the documented inverse movement: raising police funding lowers crime-generation/persistence outputs while, at constant total budget, reducing the funding that reaches education/wellbeing — measurably tightening their documented bottleneck/deficit outputs (education's capacity vs funding→quality per `engine.education.md`; wellbeing's deficit per `engine.wellbeing.md`); the reverse shift raises crime while easing those sectors. Sector funding arrives through each module's existing public input surface (crime's `DistrictInput` funding fields per `engine.crime.md`; education's stage-funding command; wellbeing's budget input) — no new direct `engine.finance` import is introduced (AC-A3 tripwire). Check: a passing test drives a fixed-total budget across two allocations (police-heavy vs education/wellbeing-heavy) and asserts (a) the crime output is strictly lower in the police-heavy allocation, (b) an education/wellbeing deficit or bottleneck output is strictly worse in the police-heavy allocation, and (c) both allocations were applied through the modules' public input surfaces, not a bespoke test-only setter (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Bb]udgetTradeoff\|func Test.*[Pp]oliceVsEducation" internal/engine/crime/*_test.go internal/engine/education/*_test.go internal/engine/wellbeing/*_test.go`). **Lazy violation this rejects:** a build where the two sliders move their own sector's output but share no real budget constraint — the fixed-total assertion is what forces the inverse movement.

- **AC-A2 (the MI5/security-liaison line is distinct from street policing).** The high-tier Security Service line is funded independently: raising it suppresses the threat dial / terror-cyber trigger probability (per `engine.crime.md` AC-11's `TriggerProbabilityFor` funding term) WITHOUT moving the street-crime generation figures the police/education trade-off moves; raising street-police funding moves street-crime generation WITHOUT damping the threat dial. ### B. Prison spare capacity & the Inmate Importation Contract

- **AC-B1 (spare capacity is queryable; sold places AND imported inmates count as occupied).** `PrisonAPI` exposes `SpareCapacity()` = `max(0, capacity − population − soldPlaces − importedInmates)` and an `ImportedInmates()` accessor distinct from domestic population and from §36 sold places. Imported inmates count as occupied for the capacity denominator and for the overcrowding-degrades-ALL-regime-effects rule (`engine.prison.md` AC-7). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/prison PrisonAPI` shows `SpareCapacity` and `ImportedInmates`; a passing test asserts spare capacity decreases one-for-one when sold places OR imported inmates rise at fixed capacity/population, and that pushing the prison over capacity degrades all three regime-effect accessors in the same tick (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]pareCapacity\|func Test.*[Ii]mportOvercrowd" internal/engine/prison/*_test.go`).

- **AC-B2 (the import contract: a toggle that fills spare capacity, bounded by it).** A player command toggles the "Import Out-of-Tile Inmates" contract on/off. When ON, the module fills spare capacity with imported inmates at a data-sourced per-month inflow rate, and never exceeds spare capacity (an import beyond spare is rejected or capped, never silently overflowed). Toggling OFF stops further inflow. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/prison` shows the toggle command; a passing test enables the contract and asserts imported inmates rise to exactly spare capacity then stop, and that toggling OFF halts further inflow (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]mportContract\|func Test.*[Ii]mportToggle" internal/engine/prison/*_test.go`).

- **AC-B3 (GR#15 — revenue: fixed monthly payout per imported inmate, data-sourced).** Each imported inmate pays the city a fixed monthly rate (e.g. `importPayoutPerInmateMicropounds`) loaded from `data/prison.json` — never a Go literal. The monthly revenue is a queryable exported value (`ImportRevenueMicropounds()` or equivalent) so finance can consume it (via the legal path, AC-B5). Check: `grep -n "importPayoutPerInmateMicropounds\|importRevenue\|ImportRevenue" internal/engine/prison/*.go` shows the data-loaded payout and exported revenue; `grep -n "prison.json" internal/engine/prison/config.go` shows the data-load path; a passing test asserts revenue equals `payout × importedCount` for a fixed month and that raising the data-file payout raises the revenue output (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]mportRevenue" internal/engine/prison/*_test.go`).

- **AC-B4 (the risk: low staffing/funding → riots, escapes, recidivism leaking offender IDs back to crime).** Imported inmates raise the prison's load; when staffing/funding is below a data-sourced threshold, the module generates riot/escape events that (a) reduce the imported (and domestic) inmate count, and (b) leak escaped offender IDs (`uint64` citizen IDs — previously-admitted offenders) back into crime generation through the REGISTERED `engine.prison`→`engine.crime` edge, exposed as a queryable escaped-inmate stream on `PrisonAPI`. Recidivism output also rises under the load (per `engine.prison.md` AC-5). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/prison` shows the escaped-inmate stream and the staffing-threshold config; a passing test drives staffing below threshold at high import load and asserts (a) a nonzero escaped-ID stream appears, (b) the escaped IDs are previously-admitted offender IDs crossing the registered crime edge, and (c) above-threshold staffing produces no escapes in the identical scenario (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]iotEscape\|func Test.*[Ee]scapedInmate" internal/engine/prison/*_test.go`).

- **AC-B5 (GR#25 — import revenue reaches finance through a legal path; tripwire).** `engine.prison`→`engine.finance` is NOT registered today. Until the Architect registers it, AC-B3's revenue is an EXPORTED VALUE on `PrisonAPI` delivered to the money stock by the composition root (a registered consumer of prison and finance) or through the services hub (prison→services and services→finance are both registered) — never a direct `engine.prison` import of `engine.finance`. **Tripwire (mechanical):** `node -e "const m=require('./code.json').modules.find(x=>x.key==='engine.prison'); process.exit(m.outbound.calls.some(c=>c.key==='engine.finance')?1:0)"` must exit **0** (edge absent — verified 2026-08-20). ### C. The Death-Spiral score S (engine.spiral)

- **AC-C1 (S exists, bounded [0,1], per tick, data-sourced weights — ASM-1232's "leading indicator").** `DecayAPI` exposes a `SpiralScore()` (or `S()`) returning S in [0,1], recomputed each tick as a weighted aggregate of live inputs: (a) local crime rates — REGISTERED `engine.crime`→`engine.spiral` edge; (b) refuse accumulation / blight — SEAM today (`RefuseInput`, AC-C6); (c) uncollected dead bodies / wellbeing failure — SEAM today (`WellbeingFailureInput`, AC-C6); (d) burnt-down / unrepaired structures — REGISTERED `engine.spiral`→`engine.build` edge; (e) financial distress / insolvency warning — REGISTERED `engine.spiral`→`engine.finance` edge. The per-input weights live in `internal/engine/spiral/spiral.json` under a `spiralScore` block (GR#15) — no player-felt weight as a Go literal. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/spiral` shows the S accessor; `grep -n "spiralScore" internal/engine/spiral/spiral.json` matches; a passing test asserts (a) S stays in [0,1] across a wide range of input fixtures, and (b) raising exactly one input at fixed others strictly raises S (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]piralScore\|func Test.*[Ss]coreBounded" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`). **Lazy violation this rejects:** a single blended "doom dial" that moves with every input at once (or a fixed timer) would pass a shallow "S exists" check — the single-variable isolation test is what catches it.

- **AC-C2 (S > 0.2 dampens growth — real, observable, reversible).** Above the data-sourced `growthDampThreshold` (placeholder 0.2), urban growth measurably slows; when S falls back below it, growth recovers. The dampening is a function of the real S value through the REGISTERED `engine.spiral`→`engine.build` and `engine.spiral`→`engine.attract` edges — never a latched flag that stays set after S recovers. Check: `grep -n "growthDampThreshold" internal/engine/spiral/spiral.json` matches; a passing test drives S above then below the threshold and asserts growth output slows then recovers (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Gg]rowthDamp\|func Test.*[Gg]rowthRecover" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`).

- **AC-C3 (S > 0.6 suppresses attraction and starts emigration — proportional, reversible).** Above the data-sourced `attractionSuppressThreshold` (placeholder 0.6), citizen attraction is suppressed and net emigration begins, through the REGISTERED `engine.spiral`→`engine.attract` edge (attract's inbound already includes spiral). Suppression is proportional to how far S is above the threshold and reverses when S falls below it (the same bidirectionality `engine.spiral.md`'s Escalations already require of attract). Check: `grep -n "attractionSuppressThreshold" internal/engine/spiral/spiral.json` matches; a passing test drives S past 0.6 and asserts the attraction/migration output moves in the documented direction, then drives S back below and asserts it recovers (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Aa]ttractionSuppress\|func Test.*[Ee]migrationOnset" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`).

- **AC-C4 (the consistency death gate: S ≥ 0.8 for N consecutive months → WarningLedger, then a DISTINCT game-over verdict).** When S ≥ the data-sourced `deathGateThreshold` (placeholder 0.8) for a data-sourced `consecutiveMonths` run (placeholder 24) of consecutive month-end values, the module (a) records a warning to `engine.projections`' `WarningLedger` (the REGISTERED `engine.spiral`→`engine.projections` edge, mirroring `engine.spiral.md` AC-15's FEAT-068 gate), and (b) if uncorrected, transitions to a NEW `DeathVerdict` — `DeathServiceCollapse` (String `"service-collapse"`) via `EvaluateDeath`, with a new registry-sourced error distinct from `ErrGhostCityNoWarning` (MET-G1102) per ASM-1233. This is ADDED to the existing verdict set (`DeathNone`/`DeathInsolvency`/`DeathGhostCity`), not a rename of the population ghost-city condition. Check: `grep -n "DeathServiceCollapse\|service-collapse\|ErrServiceCollapse" internal/engine/spiral/*.go` matches; `grep -rn "MET-G11" internal/engine/spiral/errors.go` shows a new registry code distinct from MET-G1102; a passing test drives S ≥ 0.8 for exactly `consecutiveMonths−1` consecutive months and asserts NO game-over, then for the full run and asserts the warning fires first (WarningLedger entry) and the verdict fires after (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]erviceCollapseGate\|func Test.*[Dd]eathGateConsecutive" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** firing game-over on a single S ≥ 0.8 reading, or an internal latch that never clears, would pass a naive "S high → game over" check — the N−1 negative case and AC-C3's reversibility are what rule it out.

- **AC-C5 (ASM-1232 — S is derived from real live signals, never a magic counter, never a duplicate of DecayAPI).** S is recomputed from live input values every tick; it does not accumulate independent of the inputs, and it composes WITH — does not replace — the existing DecayAPI decay-state/blight/spread machinery (`engine.spiral.md` AC-3/AC-4). Reversing the causal inputs lowers S. Check: a passing test holds all inputs high for several ticks and asserts S does NOT drift upward on its own; a second test lowers the causal inputs and asserts S strictly decreases (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]coreNoAccumul\|func Test.*[Ss]coreReversible" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`).

- **AC-C6 (GR#25 — refuse→spiral and wellbeing→spiral edges are needed; SEAMS + tripwires until registered).** The S computation's refuse/blight input and uncollected-dead-body/wellbeing-failure input arrive through Go seam interfaces (`RefuseInput`, `WellbeingFailureInput` — or equivalent) satisfied by the composition root, NOT direct `engine.refuse`/`engine.wellbeing` imports while those edges are unregistered. This keeps the S math testable in isolation today and re-wirable when the edges land. **Tripwires (mechanical):** `node -e "const m=require('./code.json').modules.find(x=>x.key==='engine.refuse'); process.exit(m.outbound.calls.some(c=>c.key==='engine.spiral')?1:0)"` must exit **0** (edge absent — verified 2026-08-20), AND the identical check for `engine.wellbeing`→`engine.spiral` must exit **0**. A nonzero exit means the edge has landed and this AC's seams must be replaced with real calls before the next commit touching `internal/engine/spiral/`. Until then: Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/spiral` shows the two seam interfaces; `grep -rn "engine/refuse\|engine/wellbeing\|refuse\.\|wellbeing\." internal/engine/spiral/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds NO direct cross-package import — every input crosses a seam.
