## User stories

- **US-1.** As the city's traffic model, I need fleet composition (car/van/truck ICE-vs-EV split) to shift by era with trucks electrifying last, so the EV transition reads as a real, staged mechanic rather than a single toggle.
- **US-2.** As `engine.logistics` (a registered consumer relationship: fuel's outbound calls include `engine.logistics`), I need a fuel shortage to genuinely strand JIT deliveries, so §49's "a fuel shortage strands the logistics that fix shortages" is a real circular-fragility mechanic, not a one-way cost.
- **US-3.** As `engine.tax` (this item's registered outbound edge), I need fuel-duty revenue to be a real, queryable flow that erodes as EV share grows, so the fiscal sting §49 describes is a real, measurable trend, not narration.
- **US-4.** As `engine.consumption` (`UtilityAPI`, this item's registered outbound edge), I need EV charging load to be real evening-peak demand that stacks with electric heating, so the winter grid-crisis mechanic is reachable through the same utility-network machinery every other load uses.
- **US-5.** As the player watching the balance of trade, I need imported fuel's cost to be a real, bidirectional-capable ledger flow (the city can be a heavy net importer in the ICE era and a much lighter one as EV share rises), so this item's contribution to the exit gate's "flip" is genuine, not asserted.

## Scope

Fleet composition per era (car/van/truck, ICE vs EV share) feeding `engine.traffic`, forecourt network coverage tracking city growth, JIT fuel-shortage fragility via `engine.logistics`, fuel-duty revenue via `engine.tax` with erosion as EV share rises, and EV charging load coupling into `engine.consumption`'s `UtilityAPI` including the evening-peak/winter-crisis stack. Fuel's own per-unit commodity price (needed for duty calculation and strategic-reserve costing) and the home-charging equity mechanic (dwelling-typology-gated) are named by spec but have no registered call edge to `engine.market`/`engine.households` respectively — scoped out, blocked-pending-fix (see Escalations).

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional

- **AC-1 (GR#20; code.json inbound contract).** A `FuelAPI` exists matching code.json's registered pattern — fleet composition per era; charging load into `UtilityAPI` — with fleet ICE/EV split consumed by `engine.traffic`, duty revenue posted through `engine.tax`, JIT fuel demand routed through `engine.logistics`, and charging load pushed into `engine.consumption`'s `UtilityAPI`, exclusively through their registered interfaces. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/fuel FuelAPI` shows a fleet-composition query, a duty-posting path, and a charging-load accessor; `grep -rln "consumption\.\(networkState\)\.\w\+ = \|tax\.\(instrumentState\)\.\w\+ = " internal/engine/*/ internal/ui/*/` (excluding `internal/engine/fuel`, `internal/engine/consumption`, `internal/engine/tax`, and `_test.go`) finds no direct exported-field write bypassing either interface.
- **AC-2 (§49 fleet composition by era — EV share genuinely rises, and truck lags car/van).** Fleet composition is loaded/derived per milestone era with a documented, data-driven EV-share curve (ASM-307), and a passing test asserts (a) EV share strictly increases across at least three successive eras for the car segment, and (b) at any given era, the truck segment's EV share is less than or equal to the car segment's EV share (the spec's explicit "trucks last" ordering) — not merely that all three segments independently trend upward at the same undifferentiated rate. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]leetEraShift\|func Test.*[Tt]rucksLast" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`. A lazy-but-plausible violation: a single citywide "EV adoption %" applied uniformly to car/van/truck — this check rejects it by requiring the truck segment's figure to lag the car segment's at the same era, which a single undifferentiated percentage cannot produce.
- **AC-3 (§49 JIT fragility — shortage strands the logistics that fix it, made reachable).** A passing test deliberately injects a fuel-supply shortfall (reduced tanker throughput from the port tank farm/refinery source) and asserts a measurable degradation is read back from `engine.logistics`'s own delivery/queue state for OTHER commodities whose replenishment trucks are fuel-dependent — not merely that `engine.fuel`'s own internal shortage flag is set. The test must also show a strategic-reserve buffer (if stocked) delays or prevents that degradation in an otherwise-identical scenario, proving the reserve is a real mitigant, not a cosmetic dial. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]uelShortageStrand\|func Test.*[Ss]trategicReserve" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`. A lazy-but-plausible violation: a shortage that reduces `engine.fuel`'s own reported "fuel availability" number without ever touching `engine.logistics`'s actual delivery throughput — this check rejects it because it specifically reads the degradation back from `engine.logistics`'s state, the module whose fragility §49 names explicitly, not from `engine.fuel`'s own bookkeeping.
- **AC-4 (§49 fuel-duty revenue and its erosion — the fiscal sting is a real trend).** Fuel-duty revenue is posted through `engine.tax`'s registered edge as a function of ICE-fleet fuel consumption, and a passing test holds the duty *rate* constant while raising EV share (per AC-2's era curve) and asserts posted duty revenue strictly decreases — proving the erosion is a consequence of the shrinking taxable base, not a rate cut. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]utyErosion\|func Test.*[Ff]uelDutyRevenue" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`. False-pass risk: a test that only asserts duty revenue is nonzero would pass a build with no erosion mechanic at all — the check requires a strictly-decreasing trend across the same rate held fixed.
- **AC-5 (§49 grid coupling — evening-peak stacking with heating, the winter-crisis mechanic).** EV charging load pushed into `UtilityAPI` is time-of-day-aware (an evening-peak concentration, not a flat daily average), and a passing test asserts that stacking EV charging load with a separately-modelled electric-heating peak (via the same `UtilityAPI`, consistent with §17/§49's shared grid) produces a combined peak-load figure strictly greater than either load alone at the same hour — the mechanical basis for the "winter peak crisis" the spec names. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Gg]ridCoupling\|func Test.*[Ee]veningPeakStack" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`. A lazy-but-plausible violation: charging load modelled as a flat daily-average addition to grid demand — this check rejects it by requiring an hour-of-day figure where charging load is demonstrably concentrated, not smeared evenly across 24 hours.
- **AC-6 (§49 forecourt network coverage tracks city growth).** Forecourt coverage is queryable per district/area and a passing test asserts that as served population grows (holding forecourt count fixed), a coverage-adequacy figure degrades — "a growing city needs forecourts like it needs substations" as a real, checkable relationship, not a one-time static count. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]orecourtCoverage" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`.

### Error handling

- **AC-7 (GR#7).** A fuel-shortage event with no available strategic reserve and no alternative supply path returns a registry-sourced error/event distinct from a silent stall in `engine.logistics`'s dependent deliveries. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/fuel/*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Nn]oReserveShortage\|func Test.*[Uu]nmitigatedShortage" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly (BUG-100):** the test asserts the returned error/event's registry code matches AND that no silent stall occurred in dependent engine.logistics deliveries without a corresponding recorded event, not merely that a matching-named test function exists.
- **AC-8 (GR#7).** A malformed fleet-era or charging-load fixture (missing EV-share figure for a defined era, negative charging-load value) produces a registry-sourced load-time error distinct from AC-7's code, never a silent default-to-zero that would mask a data-authoring bug as "no charging demand". Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/fuel/*.go` finds a second distinct code; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]alformedFuelData" internal/engine/fuel/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly (BUG-100):** the test asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no default-to-zero EV-share/charging-load value was silently loaded, not merely that a matching-named test function exists.

## Out of scope

- **Fuel's own per-unit commodity price and import-cost booking — STILL BLOCKED after c36778b; BUG-100 tripwire applied.** No `engine.fuel → engine.market` edge is registered (c36778b did not touch `engine.fuel` or `engine.market`), despite Fuel being one of `engine.market`'s own registered nine commodities (import-only supply-mode, `engine.market.md` AC-2/AC-3) and `engine.market`'s registered consumers list omitting `engine.fuel`. Duty calculation (AC-4) is posted as a function of consumption volume and the duty *rate* (a tax-instrument figure, legitimately `engine.tax`'s), not the underlying commodity price, so AC-4 is unblocked; but the imported-fuel cost itself (the "money leaves via imports" half of the balance-of-trade picture, line 682) cannot be booked without either this edge or a confirmed routing-through-`engine.logistics` pattern. **Tripwire (mechanical — BUG-100):** `node -e "const m=require('./code.json').modules.find(x=>x.key==='engine.fuel'); process.exit(m.outbound.calls.some(c=>c.key==='engine.market')?1:0)"` must exit **0**; a nonzero exit means the edge landed and the imported-fuel-cost booking must be re-armed. Not building a second commodity-price table to route around it (GR#3).
- **The terraces-can't-charge equity mechanic — STILL BLOCKED after c36778b; BUG-100 tripwire applied.** No `engine.fuel → engine.households` edge is registered (c36778b did not touch either module); dwelling-typology data (which homes have driveways vs. terraced street frontage) lives in `engine.households`. AC-5's grid-coupling test covers the aggregate evening-peak mechanic but does not gate individual charger availability by dwelling typology until this edge lands. **Tripwire (mechanical — BUG-100):** `node -e "const m=require('./code.json').modules.find(x=>x.key==='engine.fuel'); process.exit(m.outbound.calls.some(c=>c.key==='engine.households')?1:0)"` must exit **0**; a nonzero exit means the edge landed and AC-5 must be re-armed to gate charger availability by dwelling typology with a real check.
- Road-pricing-per-km as a replacement tax instrument (§39/§49's "the player must replace it") — `engine.tax`'s own instrument-panel territory; this item only exposes the eroding duty-revenue trend (AC-4) for `engine.tax`/`engine.policies` to react to.
- Port tank farm/refinery construction and throughput modelling (§50) — that facility's own build/capacity model; this item consumes its supply as an input to AC-3's shortage scenario, it does not model the refinery itself.
- EV charger physical construction (street posts, rapid hubs, depot charging, forecourt conversions) as buildable objects — `engine.build`'s catalogue; this item models the resulting charging *load*, not the construction flow.

## Escalations

- **For Ben — the balance-of-trade flip, this item's contribution.** This is the module most naturally positioned to demonstrate the *import* half of the flip (capexport/fdi/rail lean export-side), but its ability to book that cost is currently blocked by the `engine.fuel → engine.market` gap — flagged rather than routed around. AC-3 and AC-4 are unblocked and demonstrate the module's own bidirectional-capable character in the meantime: AC-4's duty-erosion test shows revenue trending down as the ICE-fleet taxable base shrinks (a real, measurable directional change over time, the same shape as `engine.attract`'s AC-4/AC-5 bidirectionality precedent applied to a fiscal trend instead of population).
- **For Bill/Ben — BUG-058, two instances specific to this file (filed as part of the four-instance comment 2026-08-11).** `engine.fuel → engine.market` (fuel commodity pricing) and `engine.fuel → engine.households` (dwelling-typology gating for home charging) are both named or clearly implied by §49's text but have no registered edge. Recommend Bill confirm whether fuel pricing is meant to route directly through `engine.market` or via `engine.logistics` re-exposing commodity price to its consumers (`engine.logistics` already calls `engine.market`) — the latter would avoid adding a new direct edge but is an architectural choice, not a BA judgment call.
- **For Aaron (ASM-307).** The EV-share-by-era curve and strategic-reserve sizing (days of cover) used in AC-2/AC-3's scenarios are placeholder, data-driven figures, not spec-fixed or tuned. Real player-felt pacing numbers are Aaron's call.
