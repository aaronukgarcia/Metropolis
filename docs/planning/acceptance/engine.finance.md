## User stories

- **US-1.** As the city, I need household income (wages by sector/skill) to flow through taxes into a city budget that pays service opex, import contracts, debt service, and construction, all as int64 micro-pounds, so §7's money-flow chain is a real, auditable ledger, not a single aggregate number.
- **US-2.** As `ui.screen.finance` (a code.json consumer), I need every figure to drill through to its underlying ledger lines (per UI-SPEC §4's "the drill-through rule (absolute)"), so a cash figure on F2 genuinely opens its transaction history, not a dead end.
- **US-3.** As `engine.invariant` (MOD-019, already built in Sprint 2), I need `engine.finance` to expose a hook/interface the money-conservation invariant can query every tick, so §14's conservation assert has real ledger totals to check against, closing the loop this BA's own Sprint 2 escalation flagged as pending.
- **US-4.** As the player, I need every cell to be continuously priced by `base(terrain) × access(junction, roads) × amenity(services, coast view, pollution⁻¹) × scarcity(city size)`, so land price is a real formula the player can reason about, not a hidden number.
- **US-5.** As the player under financial pressure, I need loan facilities gated by milestone, interest set by a credit-rating function of debt/revenue + payment history + reserve months, and insolvency (3 consecutive months unable to meet obligations with no available credit) to be an actual game-over condition, so §7's and §12's failure mechanics are real and testable, not narrative flavour.
- **US-6.** As `engine.firms` (a later, Sprint 10 consumer, with a simple v1 stand-in here per MOD-022's own description "v1, superseded by engine.firms"), I need a basic firm P&L model (revenue from local demand ± export, costs from wages/inputs/rent, open/close on profitability) so commercial/industrial zones can live or die honestly before the full firms module lands.
- **US-7.** As `engine.projections`, I need investment programmes (capex parked into productivity) to carry a documented multi-year payback curve, so the Slow-Fuse Principle (A5: consequences >5 game-years out render at decision time) has real numbers for investment decisions specifically.

## Scope

Double-entry ledger money flows (wages→tax→budget→opex/imports/debt/construction), continuous land pricing, milestone-gated loan facilities with credit-rating-driven interest, the insolvency game-over condition, a v1 simple firm P&L, and investment-programme payback modelling. All monetary values in int64 micro-pounds.

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional

- **AC-1 (GR#20; code.json inbound contract).** A `FinanceAPI` (or equivalent) exists exposing a double-entry ledger where every credit has a matching debit, queryable per account/category, matching code.json's "double-entry ledger; every figure drill-through to ledger lines" pattern. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance FinanceAPI` shows a `Ledger`/`Transaction`/`Entry` type set with debit/credit pairing; a passing test asserts posting one transaction creates balanced debit+credit entries summing to zero (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]oubleEntry\|func Test.*[Bb]alance" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-2 (M0-ENG §1.2).** All monetary values are `int64` micro-pounds (1 GBP = 1,000,000 micro-pounds, or a documented equivalent fixed-point scale) — no `float32`/`float64` money field anywhere in the package. Check: `grep -n "float32\|float64" internal/engine/finance/*.go` (excluding `_test.go` and comments) finds no matches on monetary fields; `go doc ./internal/engine/finance Money` (or equivalent type) shows an `int64` underlying type.
- **AC-3 (§7 money flows).** The wages→taxes→budget→opex/imports/debt/construction chain is modelled as a sequence of ledger-posting functions each callable independently and composable into the monthly finance phase (per §3's fixed phase order: "...finance (wages, tax, interest, contracts)"). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance` lists functions/methods for each named flow stage (`PostWages`, `CollectTax`, `SettleOpex`, `ServiceDebt`, `SettleConstruction` or equivalent); a passing integration test runs the full chain for one synthetic month and asserts the budget balance reflects all stages (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]onthlyFlow\|func Test.*[Ff]ullChain" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-4 (§7 land pricing).** `LandPrice(cell) Money` (or equivalent) implements the documented formula `base(terrain) × access(junction, roads) × amenity(services, coast view, pollution⁻¹) × scarcity(city size)` as four independently-testable component functions, not a single opaque calculation. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance` shows `BaseTerrainPrice`, `AccessFactor`, `AmenityFactor`, `ScarcityFactor` (or equivalently named) alongside `LandPrice`; a passing test varies each component independently and asserts `LandPrice` responds monotonically in the documented direction (e.g. higher scarcity ⇒ higher price) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]andPrice" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-5 (§7 loans).** Loan facilities are gated by milestone tier (consuming `engine.unlocks`'s milestone state via its own interface, not a hardcoded tier check), and interest rate is computed as `f(debt/revenue ratio, payment history, reserve months)` — a documented credit-rating function, not a flat rate. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance CreditRating` (or equivalent) exists; a passing test asserts a worse debt/revenue ratio or a missed-payment history produces a lower credit rating / higher interest rate than a clean history at the same debt level (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]reditRating\|func Test.*[Ll]oan" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-6 (§7 refinancing spiral).** Missing a loan payment degrades credit rating, which raises the interest rate on new/refinanced debt, which increases future payment burden — a passing test demonstrates this feedback loop compounding over at least two simulated missed-payment months (not just a one-shot rating drop). Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]efinanc.*[Ss]piral\|func Test.*[Cc]ompound" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go` finds coverage.
- **AC-7 (§7/§12 insolvency).** Insolvency is tracked as a rolling count of consecutive months where obligations couldn't be met AND no credit was available; at exactly 3 consecutive such months, a game-over signal/event fires. A month where obligations were met, or where credit was available even if unused, resets the counter to zero (not decrements it) — matching "3 consecutive months" literally. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance IsInsolvent` (or equivalent) exists; passing tests cover: 3 consecutive failing months → game-over fires; 2 failing months then 1 successful month → counter resets, no game-over at month 4's second failure (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]nsolven" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-8 (§7 investments).** Surplus can be allocated to interest-bearing reserves (a documented reserve-account interest accrual) or to capex investment programmes carrying a multi-year payback curve exposed for `engine.projections` to render. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance InvestmentProgramme` (or equivalent) shows a payback-curve accessor; a passing test asserts the payback curve spans multiple simulated years, not a single-month lump (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]nvestment" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-9 (§7 v1 firm P&L).** A simple firm model opens firms on positive projected profitability and closes them on sustained negative P&L (revenue from local demand ± export, costs from wages + inputs + rent), documented explicitly as the v1 stand-in superseded later by `engine.firms` (per the BOW item's own description). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance SimpleFirm` (or equivalent, clearly named to signal v1-only status) exists; passing tests cover both open-on-profitability and close-on-sustained-loss paths (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]irm.*[Oo]pen\|func Test.*[Ff]irm.*[Cc]lose" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-10 (US-3; engine.invariant hook).** `FinanceAPI` exposes a total-money-in-circulation query (sum of all account balances: citizen wealth references, firm balances, city treasury, in-flight transactions) callable once per tick by `engine.invariant` (MOD-019) without walking every ledger entry from scratch each call — a maintained running total or an efficient aggregation. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance TotalMoneyInCirculation` (or equivalent) exists; a passing test asserts it matches a from-scratch sum of all entries for a synthetic ledger state (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Tt]otalMoney\|func Test.*[Cc]onservation" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-11 (UI-SPEC §4 drill-through).** Every aggregate figure `FinanceAPI` exposes (budget balance, tax revenue, opex total, debt service) carries a way to retrieve the underlying ledger line(s) that compose it — a `Lines(aggregateID)` or equivalent traceability method, not just the number. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/finance` shows a line-retrieval method keyed by aggregate/account; a passing test asserts the retrieved lines sum to the reported aggregate (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]rillThrough\|func Test.*[Ll]ines" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).

### Error handling

- **AC-12 (GR#7).** An unbalanced transaction (debits ≠ credits) is rejected at post time with a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code), never silently posted or auto-balanced with a plug entry. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/finance/*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Uu]nbalanced" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-13.** An overdraft/negative-balance attempt without available credit is rejected (contributing to the insolvency counter per AC-7) rather than silently allowed to go negative unbounded. Check: passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Oo]verdraft\|func Test.*[Nn]egativeBalance" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).

### Determinism & safety

- **AC-14 (GR#21; M0-ENG §1.2).** All cross-shard monetary summation (e.g. citywide budget totals aggregated from per-district or per-shard sub-ledgers) is performed in fixed shard order (0→255 per M0-ENG §1.2), never via unordered map iteration. Check: `grep -n "for .* := range" internal/engine/finance/*.go` on any map feeding a monetary sum is either absent or followed by sorted-key iteration (`grep -n "sort\." internal/engine/finance/*.go`); a passing determinism test asserts identical totals across worker counts (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]eterminis\|func Test.*[Ss]hardOrder" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go`).
- **AC-15 (SG-7 scoped; GR#21).** `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since" internal/engine/finance/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches — interest accrual, payback curves, and the insolvency month-counter are all driven by simulation tick/month, never wall clock.
- **AC-16.** `go test ./internal/engine/finance/... -race -count=1` passes with no data race when the ledger is posted to concurrently across shards within a single monthly finance phase. Check: `grep -n "go func()" internal/engine/finance/*_test.go` finds at least one concurrency test.

### Documentation

- **AC-17.** `internal/engine/finance/doc.go` states the module key `engine.finance`, cites §7 and §II.4, documents the int64 micro-pounds convention with its exact scale factor, and states which methods form the `engine.invariant` money-conservation hook (US-3). Check: `grep -n "engine.finance" internal/engine/finance/doc.go` and `grep -n "micro-pound" internal/engine/finance/doc.go` and `grep -n "engine.invariant\|conservation" internal/engine/finance/doc.go` all match.
- **AC-18.** The v1 `SimpleFirm` model's doc comment states explicitly that it is superseded by `engine.firms` (Sprint 10) and lists what changes when that module lands, so no later developer mistakes it for the permanent firm model. Check: `grep -n "engine.firms\|superseded" internal/engine/finance/simplefirm.go` (or equivalent) matches.

## Out of scope

- The full firms module (`engine.firms`, Sprint 10): professional services, banking, entrepreneur lifecycle — this item's `SimpleFirm` is an explicit, temporary v1 stand-in only.
- The full tax instrument panel (`engine.tax`, Sprint 9): elasticity, incidence, per-band controls — this item only needs headline income/sales/corporation tax rates to close the M1 loop, per the sprint plan's §1 "money arrives in Sprint 4... but the full §39 tax instrument panel waits until Sprint 9".
- The Fiscal Circuit's whole-economy F2 view and civil-service net-cost accounting (§54, `engine.fiscal`, Sprint 9) — this item posts the underlying ledger entries; the whole-economy visualisation and civil-service framing are that later module's job.
- Central government grants/mandates (§55, `engine.defence`/`engine.fiscal`) — out of scope; this item's ledger just needs to be able to post an inbound-grant transaction type when that module exists.
- Firm-level export/import balance-of-trade accounting (§33/§54) — this item's `SimpleFirm` handles local-demand-driven revenue only; export revenue is a later module's addition.

- **For Bill.** §7 doesn't specify the exact credit-rating formula weights (how debt/revenue, payment history, and reserve months combine) — AC-5/AC-6 check directional correctness (worse inputs ⇒ worse rating) rather than a specific formula, consistent with GR#15's "validators derive from data" doctrine for genuinely untuned balance data. Flagging in case Bill wants a specific formula mandated now.
- **For Bill.** The insolvency AC-7's "3 consecutive months" reset semantics (does ANY successful month reset the counter, or only 3 consecutive successful months?) is the BA's literal reading of §7's text ("can't meet obligations for 3 consecutive months with no available credit"). If the intended design is a leakier/harsher counter (e.g. a moving window rather than strict consecutive-reset), that's a spec clarification for Bill, not a BA judgment call, given how consequential this exact rule is (it's a game-over condition).
