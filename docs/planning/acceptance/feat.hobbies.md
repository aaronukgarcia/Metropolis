## User stories

- **US-1.** As the engine, I need a **data-driven hobby catalogue** — gym, football, tennis, walking, cycling, rock climbing, horse riding, rugby, castle visiting, each a distinct activity with its own UK-demographic participation rate and wellbeing/community benefit — so the set of hobbies is authored in data, never a hardcoded Go list (load-bearing #1).
- **US-2.** As a citizen, I need my participation in each hobby to derive deterministically from my personality/taste attributes (physicality, sociability, novelty-seeking — §5.1) and the hobby's data-driven participation rate, so who does which hobby is personality-honest, not a uniform per-capita roll.
- **US-3.** As `engine.leisure`, I need the hobby catalogue as the **activity vocabulary** my 168-hour budget (MOD-055) allocates hours across by taste weights, so a "gym" hobby and a "castle visit" hobby are two distinct leisure activities, not one undifferentiated "leisure" bucket.
- **US-4.** As the player, I need venue-anchored hobbies to sit on a **scale ladder** — stables from a couple of boxes to a full yard, football from a local pitch to a Wembley-class grand stadium — where every rung is a distinct tier with its own capacity/capex/prestige, so growing a hobby is a real investment ladder, not one building wearing a renamed tier label (load-bearing #2).
- **US-5.** As the player, I need the elite venue tiers (Wembley-class stadium, rugby stadium, Olympic arena) to be the **long-bet destination tier** — materially larger capex than the rung below, with a prestige/tourism-draw payoff that only materialises at scale — so I can "stretch early" for a payoff I cannot collect until the city around it is big enough (load-bearing #3).
- **US-6.** As `engine.tourism`, I need the elite venues and castles to feed the §44 attraction portfolio score (the "venues", "events", "heritage", and "landmarks" terms) through a registered seam, never a hobbies-local prestige number the rest of the city cannot see.
- **US-7.** As the determinism gate (GR#21), I need participation assignment and ladder resolution to be a pure function of (worldSeed, tick, citizen state, data) — never wall clock, never map-iteration order.

## Scope

A data-driven hobby catalogue in `data/hobbies.json` (each hobby a distinct activity with a placeholder UK-demographic participation rate, a wellbeing/community-benefit magnitude, and a taste-axis mapping); a deterministic participation computation (rate × eligible population, personality-weighted via `CitizensAPI`); a tiered **scale ladder** per venue-anchored hobby (stables couple-of-boxes → full yard; football local → Wembley; rugby local → national; each rung a distinct tier with capacity/capex/prestige); the elite tiers' prestige/tourism-draw term feeding §44's portfolio score through the destination/tourism seam; and castle visiting as a heritage hobby with a heritage/landmark draw term. No 168-hour-budget arithmetic (`engine.leisure`), no portfolio-score/reputation/access computation (`engine.tourism`), no citizen-record edit (`engine.citizens`, done), no build/zoning placement mechanics (`engine.build`), and no final balance numbers (Aaron's pass).

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional — the data-driven catalogue (load-bearing #1)

- **AC-1 (GR#15; the catalogue is a distinct, closed, data-driven set — never a hardcoded list).** `data/hobbies.json` defines the hobby catalogue as distinct activity records spanning the three design tiers — participation (gym, football, tennis, walking, cycling), specialist (rock climbing, horse riding), spectator/elite (football-stadium, rugby, arena) — plus heritage (castle visiting), each carrying a participation-rate field, a wellbeing/community-benefit field, and a taste-axis mapping. There is **no hardcoded hobby-name list or enum in Go**: the closed set is loaded from data, and the identity of each hobby is its data key, not a Go constant. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/hobbies HobbyAPI` (or equivalent) shows a catalogue-query surface; a passing test loads the fixture, asserts at least one hobby from each design tier is present, and that two distinct hobbies resolve to two distinct records (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]atalogue\|func Test.*[Dd]istinctHobbi" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`); `grep -nE "\"(gym|football|tennis|walking|cycling|climbing|riding|rugby|castle)\"" internal/engine/hobbies/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no hardcoded hobby-name literals outside doc-comment prose — the catalogue is data-sourced. **False-pass risk:** a `switch hobby { case "gym": ... }` dispatcher with the names as literals would pass a "the catalogue is queryable" grep while the "never a hardcoded list" promise fails — the check requires the *names themselves* to come from data, not Go.

- **AC-2 (GR#15; participation rates are UK-demographic placeholders that drive real counts — balance regime).** Each hobby's participation rate is a placeholder with a disclosure comment naming it pending Aaron's balance pass, and the derived participating-citizen count for a hobby is `rate × eligible population` computed from the loaded data — never a hardcoded per-hobby count or ratio in Go. Check: a passing test mutates one hobby's participation rate in the loaded fixture and asserts the derived participating-citizen count for that hobby (and only that hobby) moves accordingly (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]ataDriven\|func Test.*[Rr]ate.*[Rr]eload" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`); `grep -nE "[0-9]+\.?[0-9]*" internal/engine/hobbies/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no bare participation-rate/count-shaped numeric literals outside doc-comment prose. **Balance-regime note (explicit):** the rates are *placeholders*, not real UK statistics — the "UK-demographic" property is the *shape* (a per-hobby population rate, not a uniform share), and the actual figures are Aaron's balance pass; a test that pins a specific percentage is a FAIL.

### Functional — participation (cross-cuts engine.citizens + engine.leisure)

- **AC-3 (GR#20; participation derives from CitizensAPI personality/taste — not a uniform roll).** Which citizens participate in which hobby is a deterministic function of the citizen's personality/taste attributes (physicality for gym/cycling/tennis/football, sociability for team sport, novelty-seeking for climbing/castles — per each hobby's data-driven taste-axis mapping) and the hobby's participation rate, read through `CitizensAPI` only, never a direct reach into `engine.citizens.Citizen`/`ColdShard`. Check: a passing test constructs two citizens identical except for one taste-relevant personality axis (e.g. physicality) and asserts their participation in the corresponding hobby differs in the direction the axis implies (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Pp]ersonality.*[Pp]articipat\|func Test.*[Tt]asteWeighted" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`); `grep -rln "citizens\.\(Citizen\|ColdShard\)\.\w\+" internal/engine/hobbies/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches. **False-pass risk:** a per-capita uniform participation roll that ignores personality would pass a "some citizens participate" reading while the §5.1 "taste weights" promise fails — the check requires two personality-differing citizens to resolve to two different participations.

- **AC-4 (the catalogue is the activity vocabulary leisure consumes — GR#20 seam to MOD-055; GR#3 no reimplementation).** The hobby catalogue is exposed as the **activity vocabulary** `engine.leisure`'s 168-hour budget allocates hours across: each hobby resolves to a leisure venue-category activity that leisure can allocate hours to by taste weights, and the per-hobby participation signal is queryable as the input leisure consumes. The hobbies package does **not** implement the 168-hour budget, venue-category hour allocation, access-time penalty, or novelty decay — those are MOD-055's (`engine.leisure.md` AC-2/AC-3/AC-4). Check: `go doc` shows the activity-vocabulary + participation-signal surface; `grep -rn "168\|discretionary\|access.*[Tt]ime\|novelty" internal/engine/hobbies/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches — no budget/allocation arithmetic lives here. **Deferred dependency (ASM-1014/ES-2):** the end-to-end "participation fills the leisure budget" claim is MOD-055's and cannot be built until leisure lands; hobbies proves its half (the vocabulary + signal) alone.

### Functional — the scale ladder (load-bearing #2)

- **AC-5 (GR#15; every venue-anchored hobby is a tiered ladder in data, each rung a distinct tier).** Venue-anchored hobbies carry a scale ladder in `data/hobbies.json`: stables/livery from a couple of boxes to a full yard; football from a local pitch to a Wembley-class grand stadium; rugby from a local ground to a national stadium — each rung a distinct tier record with its own capacity, capex, and prestige. The ladder is data, never a Go `switch tier` with the tiers as literals. Check: `go doc` shows a ladder-resolution surface (hobby, tier) → tier record; a passing test resolves two tiers of the same hobby and asserts two distinct records, then mutates one tier's capacity in the loaded fixture and asserts only that tier reflects the change (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]adder\|func Test.*[Tt]ier.*[Rr]esolve" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a single tier record with a "tier name" field worn cosmetically would pass a "a tier exists" grep while the "each stage a distinct tier" promise fails — the check requires two tiers to resolve to two records with distinct values.

- **AC-6 (distinctness + monotonicity — two rungs are two different things).** The ladder is monotonic in capacity and capex: for the same hobby, tier N+1 has strictly greater capacity and capex than tier N — a full yard > a couple of boxes, a league stadium > a non-league ground, a grand stadium > a league stadium. Check: a passing directional test walks every hobby's ladder and asserts capacity and capex are strictly increasing across rungs, never flat or reversed (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]onotonic.*[Ll]adder\|func Test.*[Ff]ullYard.*[Gg]reater" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`). **Balance-regime note:** the magnitudes are placeholders; the *ordering* is the invariant. **False-pass risk:** a ladder that maps every rung to the same shared value would pass "a ladder exists" while collapsing the feature's whole premise — the check requires strict increase.

### Functional — the elite long-bet destination tier (load-bearing #3)

- **AC-7 (elite venues feed the §44 tourism draw through a registered seam — not a hobbies-local prestige number).** The elite tiers (Wembley-class football stadium, rugby national stadium, Olympic arena) carry a prestige/tourism-draw term that reaches §44's attraction portfolio score via the `engine.destination`→`engine.tourism` seam (the elite venue is the destination long-bet — ASM-1018), never a hobbies-local "prestige" number that `engine.tourism` cannot see. Check: `go doc` shows a draw-term surface on the elite tiers; a passing test raises an elite venue's presence (from none to present) and asserts the draw input exposed on the destination/tourism seam rises (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ee]lite.*[Dd]raw\|func Test.*[Tt]ourismDraw" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`); `grep -nE "[Pp]restige|draw" internal/engine/hobbies/*.go` finds the term exposed through the seam, not a hobbies-local scalar that nothing consumes. **Deferred dependency (ES-3):** the seam itself (a new `engine.hobbies`→`engine.destination` edge, or a direct `engine.hobbies`→`engine.tourism` edge) is **unregistered today** — see ES-3; this AC is written against the destination/tourism interface shape so it is buildable as soon as the edge lands.

- **AC-8 (the long-bet shape — stretch early for the payoff, direction only).** An elite venue's full payoff is gated on the same multipliers §44 names — the draw term is realised as `draw × reputation × access`, so a player *can* build (stretch) early but the tourism payoff only materialises once reputation and access are sufficient — while its capex is materially larger than the rung below (the "huge capex" half). Check: a passing directional test holds the elite venue fixed and varies the access (or reputation) multiplier, asserting the realised draw term is strictly higher at high access than low access (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]ongBet\|func Test.*[Aa]ccessGated" internal/engine/hobbies/*_test.go`); a second test asserts `capex(elite tier) > capex(tier below)` by a documented multiple loaded from data, not a hardcoded ratio. **Balance-regime note:** the capex multiple and the access/reputation gating shape are placeholders; the *gating is real* (payoff not available at low access) is the invariant.
