## 1. Why this item exists

Aaron's brief (epic `FEAT-2326609792`, verbatim): "plan for emergency response time, based on real road and factor congestion... slow emergency response time is to add to unhappiness" (the unhappiness coupling is inc5's job, out of scope here, §6). The webconsole already has a CITYWIDE emergency coverage ratio (`serviceCoverageOf`'s `police`/`fire`/health cases, `data.ts:1383-1400`) — `population ÷ citywide capacity` — which tells you whether there is ENOUGH ambulance/fire/police capacity in aggregate, never whether a given tile can actually be REACHED quickly over the real, congested road network. A tile three segments from the nearest ambulance station down an uncongested A-road and a tile the same crow-flies distance behind a gridlocked residential cul-de-sac read identically today. inc3's segment graph + BPR delay (`FEAT-2326609796`) is the substrate that makes the difference visible: this increment routes FROM every station tile of each emergency service TO every demand tile over that graph, turns the result into response minutes and a coverage statistic against `emergency_response.json`'s real published targets, and gives it an optional overlay tint. It does NOT touch `serviceCoverageOf` — the two coexist as inc3's D1 already established for capacity bases: `serviceCoverageOf` answers "is there enough", this item answers "can it get there in time" (a genuinely different question, not a duplicate).

## 2. The design in one paragraph

**Station tiles (per service).** A service's dispatch points are its ONLINE, in-sim buildings of that `kind`: ambulance = `hea_ambulance` (`careTier: 'local'`, `data.ts:2251`); fire = `fire_post`/`fire_station`/`fire_hq` (`kind: 'fire'`, `data.ts:2299-2301`); police = `pol_station`/`pol_hq` (`kind: 'police'`, `data.ts:2007,2292`) — read via `SPECS[b.spec].kind` + `isOnline(s,b)`, never a hand-typed id list (a spec-id rename must not silently drop a service's stations). **Congested-network isochrone (AC-1/AC-2).** For each service, one multi-source Dijkstra over `segmentAdjacencyOf(s)` from every station's nearest road segment (same nearest-segment attachment inc3's `assignedFlowOf` already uses), edge weight = `segmentFreeFlowMinutesFor(seg, cfg)` DIVIDED by a `speedFactor` read from `emergency_response.json`'s `speedDegradation.curve` (piecewise-linear on the segment's `vOverC` from `segmentDelayOf(s)`, defaulting to `speedFactor=1.0` — free flow — for a segment absent from `segmentDelayOf` per its own honest-absence contract, AC-4 of inc3), further multiplied by `narrowClassPenalty.additionalSpeedFactorMultiplier` when the segment's road class is in `narrowClassPenalty.roadClassIds`. This is deliberately NOT inc3's plain BPR `t` (a private car's delay) — emergency vehicles partially bypass congestion per the data file's own `$comment`, so they get their OWN, shallower degradation curve, sourced not invented. **Response minutes per demand tile (AC-3).** Every `demandForecastOf(s)` tile attaches to its nearest road segment (same primitive, reused not re-derived — GR#3); `responseMinutes = min over stations of (isochrone minutes to that tile's segment) + baseAccessMinutes` (`data/traffic.json`, the same "leave the vehicle, reach the door" leg inc3's `commuteTimeDistributionOf` already charges at the OTHER end of a trip — same field, same convention). A tile with no reachable station (graph-disconnected, or the service has zero online stations) reports `null` — never a fabricated large number. **Coverage (AC-4).** `targetMinutesUrban` applies to every demand tile whose `ladderPointOf(s).densityBand !== 'rural'`; `targetMinutesRural` applies where it IS `'rural'` — the SAME `densityBand` field inc2's own demand forecast already reads off the current scale-ladder rung (one city-wide band today, since the ladder is population-driven not per-tile; a genuinely per-tile density band is a future increment's job, flagged §5). Coverage = (Σ `residentsActual+workersActual` on tiles with `responseMinutes <= target`) ÷ (Σ over all demand tiles), plus city-wide `p50Minutes`/`p90Minutes` (weighted by the same population basis, mirroring inc3 AC-5's percentile shape). **Hearse layer (AC-5).** `MOD-083`'s hearse mechanic and its host specs (`death_cemetery`, `death_crematorium`) do not exist in the webconsole today — both are `PH()` placeholders (`data.ts:2562-2563`, `placeholder: true`, `canEnterSim` rejects them unconditionally, `data.ts:1588`) — so there is no station tile to route from. This increment ships an honest-absence contract only (§3, AC-5), no speculative mechanic (GR#25). **Overlay (AC-6).** A minimal tint read from the isochrone, folded into the existing "Lines" toggle group per the Q100162 precedent (no new button) — see AC-6 for the exact minimal shape.

## 3. Acceptance Criteria

### Isochrone

**AC-1 — `emergencyIsochroneOf(s, service): Map<segmentId, number>` — one multi-source Dijkstra per service over the congested-but-degraded graph.**
New memoised export in `emergencyResponse.ts`, `service: 'ambulance'|'fire'|'police'`. Sources = every online station-of-that-service's nearest road segment (dedup by segmentId if two stations share one). Edge weight per segment = `segmentFreeFlowMinutesFor(seg, TRAFFIC) / speedFactorFor(seg, s)` (§2's degraded speed), where `speedFactorFor` reads `segmentDelayOf(s).get(segId)?.vOverC` (absent ⇒ `vOverC = 0` ⇒ `speedFactor = 1.0`, the curve's own `vOverC:0` anchor — never a hand-typed `1.0` bypassing the curve) through `emergency_response.json`'s `speedDegradation.curve` via its own stated `interpolationRule` (piecewise-linear, clamp outside `[0.0,1.25]`), then the narrow-class multiplier where applicable.
**Check:** a fixture with one station segment and a 3-segment chain, the middle segment forced to `vOverC = 0.9` (interpolated `speedFactor = 0.65` per the curve's own anchors) versus the same chain with the middle segment ABSENT from `segmentDelayOf` (free flow, `speedFactor = 1.0`) — the congested run's isochrone minutes to the far segment must be strictly greater, and the ratio must match `(1/0.65)` on that one segment to 1e-6 (not just "greater").
**False-pass note:** a fixture that only checks "isochrone minutes are positive" cannot distinguish "degradation curve applied" from "degradation curve ignored" — the Tester must use the paired congested-vs-free-flow comparison above with an exact ratio, not a direction-only assertion.
**Mutant:** apply inc3's plain BPR `t` (`segmentDelayOf(s).get(segId).t`) instead of the free-flow-time-over-speed-factor formula — reds the paired-ratio check (BPR's multiplier and the emergency curve's multiplier diverge numerically at `vOverC=0.9`: BPR gives a much larger delay than the curve's `0.65` factor implies).

**AC-2 — station discovery is spec-kind-driven, never a hand-typed id list; empty-service is honest, not a crash.**
Station tiles for a service = every online building whose `SPECS[b.spec].kind` matches the service's kind mapping (`ambulance→SPECS[...].id==='hea_ambulance'` via `careTier`, since `health` kind also covers clinics/hospitals which are NOT dispatch points this increment; `fire→kind==='fire'`; `police→kind==='police'`) — read from `SPECS`, never a literal `['hea_ambulance']` array duplicated from `data.ts`. A city with zero online stations of a service returns an EMPTY isochrone map (every tile `null` downstream), not a thrown error or a fabricated `Infinity`.
**Check:** a fixture with one `fire_post` and one `hea_hospital` (health kind, NOT ambulance) on the map asserts the `'ambulance'` isochrone has zero sources (empty map) while `'fire'` has exactly one.
**False-pass note:** a fixture using only `hea_ambulance` for the ambulance case cannot catch a kind-vs-id confusion (both `hea_ambulance` and `hea_hospital` share `kind:'health'`) — the Tester must include a same-kind, wrong-`careTier`/wrong-id decoy building as above.
**Mutant:** match on `sp.kind === 'health'` instead of the ambulance-specific id/careTier check — reds the decoy fixture (the hospital wrongly becomes a dispatch source).

### Coverage

**AC-3 — `responseMinutesOf(s, service): Map<tileKey, number>` — per-tile response minutes via nearest-segment attachment + access leg; unreachable tiles are `null`, never fabricated.**
Every `demandForecastOf(s)` tile attaches to its nearest road segment (the SAME primitive `assignedFlowOf` uses, reused not reimplemented — GR#3); `responseMinutes = emergencyIsochroneOf(s, service).get(nearestSegmentId) + baseAccessMinutes` (`data/traffic.json`, read not restated). A tile whose nearest segment never appears in the isochrone (disconnected graph component, or zero stations per AC-2) is OMITTED from the returned map (honest absence), never assigned `Infinity` or a fabricated cap value.
**Check:** a fixture with two demand tiles, one on the same graph component as the sole station and one on a graph-DISCONNECTED component (separate road island, no adjacency edge) — the reachable tile has a finite `responseMinutes`, the disconnected tile is absent from the map (`.has(key) === false`), tested directly, not inferred from a `NaN`/`undefined` value.
**False-pass note:** a test that checks `responseMinutes !== undefined` on a MAP LOOKUP without first asserting `.has()` will silently pass on a mutant that returns `undefined` for every tile (JS map lookup miss and "value is explicitly undefined" are indistinguishable without a `.has()` check) — the Tester must assert `.has()` is `false`, not merely that the read value is falsy.
**Mutant:** default an unreachable tile's response minutes to a large sentinel (e.g. `999`) instead of omitting it — reds the `.has()` assertion (a `999`-defaulting mutant makes `.has()` return `true`).

**AC-4 — `emergencyCoverageOf(s, service): {coverageShare: number; p50Minutes: number; p90Minutes: number}` — population-weighted coverage vs `emergency_response.json` targets, band-selected target, weighted percentile.**
Per demand tile with a defined `responseMinutesOf` value: target = `services[service].targetMinutesRural` when `ladderPointOf(s).densityBand === 'rural'`, else `targetMinutesUrban` (both read from `emergency_response.json`, never restated). `coverageShare` = (Σ `residentsActual+workersActual` on tiles with `responseMinutes <= target`) ÷ (Σ `residentsActual+workersActual` on every demand tile WITH a defined response, per AC-3's honest-absence contract — a city with zero routable demand reports `coverageShare = null`, not a division-by-zero `NaN`). `p50Minutes`/`p90Minutes` — the SAME weighted-percentile function `commuteTimeDistributionOf` (inc3 AC-5) already exports/uses internally, reused not reimplemented (import it, or its shared helper, from `trafficAssignment.ts` — GR#3; if it is not exported at dispatch time, export it additively from `trafficAssignment.ts` the same way inc3's own r2 rework additively exported `boundedNearestSourceMapOf`, BUG-857's precedent).
**Check:** a fixture with 9 demand tiles at hand-computed response minutes 1 through 9 with EQUAL population weight and a target of 5 asserts `coverageShare = 5/9` (tiles 1-5 inclusive) to the exact fraction, and `p50Minutes`/`p90Minutes` match the SAME weighted-percentile formula inc3's AC-5 test independently re-derives (cited, not assumed).
**False-pass note:** a fixture where every tile has the SAME response minutes cannot distinguish "coverage computed per-tile against the target" from "coverage returns a constant" — the Tester must use the 1-9 spread fixture above, and must assert its precondition (`responseMinutesOf` actually returns 9 distinct, defined values for the fixture) BEFORE asserting `coverageShare`, per the inc3 BUG-862 lesson (an assertion inside an implicit "if this fixture worked" must prove the fixture worked first, unconditionally).
**Mutant:** use `targetMinutesUrban` unconditionally (ignore the `densityBand === 'rural'` branch) — reds a rural-band fixture whose `coverageShare` is provably different under the correct (looser, 15-min) rural target than the wrong (stricter, 8-min) urban one.

## 5. Assumptions

- **ASM-1510** — emergency vehicles degrade speed via `emergency_response.json`'s OWN curve (§2), never inc3's plain BPR `t` (which models a private car's delay, not a priority vehicle's partial bypass). **What breaks if wrong:** if emergency vehicles should in fact experience the SAME delay as ordinary traffic (no bypass benefit at all), every isochrone in this increment under-estimates response minutes at high `v/c`; the data file's own `$comment` ("partially bypass congestion... but are not immune") is the basis for treating this as a real, sourced distinction rather than an invented one.
- **ASM-1511** — coverage target selection uses the CITY-WIDE `densityBand` (D2), not a per-tile density. **What breaks if wrong:** a large, mixed rural/urban city (not really possible yet since `scale_ladder.json` bands are population-driven, not geography-driven) would apply one target everywhere; today's single-tile-grid, single-rung city makes this a non-issue in practice, but is flagged for the day a genuinely mixed-density city model exists.
- **ASM-1512** — `baseAccessMinutes` (`data/traffic.json`, 15.0) is reused as BOTH inc3's commute-leg access minutes AND this increment's incident-arrival access leg (the "leave the vehicle, reach the door" delay). **What breaks if wrong:** an ambulance's turnout-to-scene access time and a commuter's front-door-to-road access time are physically different processes that happen to share a number today; if Aaron wants a distinct, service-specific access-leg figure, it belongs as a new field in `emergency_response.json` (not invented here without a source).
- **ASM-1513** — station discovery treats EVERY online `hea_ambulance`/`fire_*`/`pol_*` building as a dispatch point with no differentiation by crew count/appliance count (`data.ts`'s `served` capacity field is ignored for isochrone PURPOSES — it still governs `serviceCoverageOf`'s aggregate capacity check, untouched). **What breaks if wrong:** a single-crew `fire_post` (`served: 4,000`) and a `fire_hq` (`served: 80,000`) both count as EQUALLY-capable dispatch origins for travel-TIME purposes, when in reality a smaller station may have fewer available units at any moment; the isochrone answers "how fast COULD a vehicle from here reach this tile", not "how likely is a unit actually free" — a genuinely different, harder question this increment does not attempt.

**Assumption codes filed on `FEAT-2326609797`:** ASM-1510, ASM-1511, ASM-1512, ASM-1513 (`node claude-bow.js add assumption`, `webconsole/src/sim/emergencyResponse.ts`, `ui.webconsole`, per the mandate).
