# Acceptance criteria — engine.firms (MOD-058)

**BOW code:** MOD-058
**Spec refs:** §45 Firms — Entrepreneur Culture to Enterprise (`docs/METROPOLIS-MASTER-v2.1.md` lines 617-625: monthly, ambitious citizens — ambition × education × sector experience × access to capital × premises availability × local demand signal — spin out startups; progression Startup(1-5)→Small(6-25)→Medium(26-250)→Enterprise(250+, multi-site, may export/list); growth needs at every step: customers, premises (right zone/size), staff (skill-matched hiring from the real labour pool), credit, inputs (§33 chains); failure is normal and modelled — insolvency, acquisition; entrepreneur culture is a measurable city property (startups/1k) driven positively by university presence/incubators/professional-services depth/successful-founder alumni and negatively by rents/crime, culture compounds (exits fund angels fund startups); blue/white-collar demand is emergent from chains+construction+logistics+services (blue) vs firm overhead+finance+public administration (white); banking layer local branch→commercial→(M9) financial district takes deposits, extends firm credit and mortgages; central-bank environment is off-map/era-driven, a national base-rate cycle moves everyone's borrowing costs, player *positions* for it (F7 outlook), not controls it); §II.4 Economy (line 54: "Firms: founded by actual ambitious citizens, progressing startup→SME→enterprise, consuming professional services superlinearly, banked by a credit layer under an off-map central-bank rate cycle"); code.json `engine.firms` entry (inbound `FirmsAPI`, pattern "firm lifecycle events; credit conditions from rate cycle in external_world.json", consumers: `engine.comms`, `engine.fdi`, `engine.freight`; outbound calls: `engine.citizens`, `engine.finance`, `engine.market`, `engine.build`).

## User stories

- **US-1.** As a real citizen with a high ambition×education×sector-experience×capital-access×premises-availability×demand-signal composite, I need my own founding decision to be computed from MY OWN record's values via `CitizensAPI`, so entrepreneur culture is a property of who actually lives in the city, not a population-level rate applied indiscriminately (Option B, GR#3/#20).
- **US-2.** As a firm that fails, I need my staff's `employmentState` to change through `CitizensAPI` for the SPECIFIC citizen IDs who worked for me, so a firm's death is felt by the individuals who worked there, not merely subtracted from an aggregate employment count.
- **US-3.** As `engine.build` (a registered outbound dependency, MOD-026), I need a firm's growth step to be gated on a real premises query — right zone, right size — so "a scale-up with nowhere to move dies or leaves" (§45) is an enforced consequence, not flavour text.
- **US-4.** As `engine.finance` (a registered outbound dependency, MOD-022), I need firm credit requests to be priced against the off-map base-rate cycle and the bank's deposit pool, so a rate spike genuinely starves startups (§45's "citywide weather" framing) rather than firms borrowing at a fixed, rate-cycle-blind cost.
- **US-5.** As `engine.market` (a registered outbound dependency, MOD-020), I need a firm's §33-chain inputs priced/availability-bounded through `MarketAPI`, so a firm's growth is genuinely constrained by real commodity supply, not an unconditional input assumption.
- **US-6.** As `engine.freight`, `engine.comms`, and `engine.fdi` (registered inbound consumers), I need firm lifecycle events (founded/grown/failed/acquired) exposed through `FirmsAPI` as a subscribable stream, so downstream modules react to real firm state changes rather than polling.
- **US-7.** As the professional/financial-services sector, I need my demand to scale superlinearly with the count of OTHER (non-services) firms I serve, so "consuming professional services superlinearly" (§45, §II.4) is a real functional relationship, not a flat per-firm coefficient.
- **US-8.** As the banking layer (branch→commercial→financial district), I need firm credit extended to be bounded by the bank's actual deposit pool sourced from `engine.finance`, so credit creation cannot exceed what the deposit base and rate cycle would realistically support.

## Scope

The firm lifecycle (founding from a real citizen record → Startup→Small→Medium→Enterprise progression → failure/insolvency/acquisition) behind a single `FirmsAPI`; entrepreneur-culture measurement; the professional/financial-services superlinear-demand relationship; the banking layer (branch→commercial→financial district) turning deposits into firm credit and mortgages; consumption of the off-map central-bank rate cycle. No production-chain stage mechanics themselves (§33, `engine.freight`'s job — this module only registers chain stages AS firms per code.json's "stages register as firms" pattern), no zoning/build mechanics (`engine.build`'s job, only consumed here), no citizen record mechanics (`engine.citizens`'s job, only consumed here).

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional — founding from real citizens (the load-bearing clause)

- **AC-1 (GR#20; code.json inbound contract).** A `FirmsAPI` exists exposing firm lifecycle queries (stage, staff roster, financial state) and lifecycle commands (found/grow/fail/acquire), matching code.json's documented pattern "firm lifecycle events; credit conditions from rate cycle in external_world.json", consumed by `engine.comms`/`engine.fdi`/`engine.freight` through this interface alone. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms FirmsAPI` shows both a query surface and a lifecycle-event subscription surface; `grep -rln "firms\.\(firmState\|staffRoster\)\.\w\+ = " internal/engine/*/ internal/ui/*/` (excluding `internal/engine/firms` itself and `_test.go`) finds no direct exported-field writes bypassing the interface.
- **AC-2 (§45 founding composite; GR#20 outbound contract).** Monthly founding is evaluated PER CITIZEN, sourced through `CitizensAPI` (the registered outbound edge) — not from a population-level distribution sampled independently of any citizen record. The founding probability for a specific citizen is a documented function of that citizen's own ambition, education/attainment, sector experience, wealth (capital access), plus premises availability (`engine.build`) and local demand signal (`engine.market`/aggregate context). A founded startup's `FounderCitizenID` resolves, via `CitizensAPI`, to a real citizen record. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms Startup` (or equivalent) shows a `FounderCitizenID` field of the same ID type `CitizensAPI` uses; `grep -rn "citizens\.\(CitizenAt\|Citizen\)" internal/engine/firms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows the founding evaluator calling into `CitizensAPI` per-candidate, not once for an aggregate rate. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build that samples "N startups this month" from a Poisson/aggregate-ambition-mean rate and THEN labels each with an arbitrary citizen ID post-hoc (to satisfy "founder ID exists") would pass a naive existence grep — AC-3's isolation test is what catches this specific shape.
- **AC-3 (isolation — the check that distinguishes real per-citizen driving from an aggregate, per v1.9).** Constructing two otherwise-identical synthetic populations that differ ONLY in which specific citizen ID holds a given high-ambition value (the multiset of ambition/education/wealth/etc. values across the population is held IDENTICAL, only the labels/IDs are permuted) and running founding evaluation on each: the SET of `FounderCitizenID`s produced differs between the two runs in a way that tracks the permutation (the high-ambition individual's own ID appears as a founder in both runs, following the value, not the original position), while the TOTAL COUNT of firms founded is unaffected by the permutation. A second test perturbs exactly ONE citizen's ambition score (holding every other citizen's record fixed) and asserts ONLY that citizen's own founding probability changes — every other citizen's founding probability, and the aggregate city-wide founding rate function's shape, is unaffected except through that one citizen's own contribution. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Pp]ermut.*[Ff]ounder\|func Test.*[Ii]ndividualFounding\|func Test.*[Ff]oundingIsolat" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go` shows both the permutation test and the single-citizen-perturbation test. **This is the check a lazy-but-plausible aggregate model fails:** an aggregate implementation (sample a count from a city-wide ambition-distribution mean, then arbitrary-assign founder IDs) produces IDENTICAL founder-ID sets under label permutation (because the assignment is not actually driven by which individual holds which value) and cannot make a single citizen's perturbation move only that citizen's own probability, since there is no per-citizen probability to move — only a distribution parameter that, if touched, moves everyone's odds a little. The isolation test is structurally the same weapon `engine.wellbeing.md`'s AC-3 uses against a weighted-blend-with-post-hoc-labels implementation, applied here to firm founding instead of wellbeing attribution.
- **AC-4 (§45 staff are real citizens, not headcount).** A firm's staff roster is a set of real `CitizenID`s hired through `CitizensAPI`'s skill-matched labour-pool query (§45: "staff — skill-matched hiring from the real labour pool"), not an integer headcount field. Growth from one stage to the next (e.g. Startup 1-5 → Small 6-25) requires the roster to actually reach the new stage's staff-count floor with real hires, not merely incrementing a counter. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms Firm` shows a `Staff []citizens.CitizenID` (or equivalent typed set) field, not a bare `StaffCount int`; a passing test asserts a firm attempting to grow without enough real hires (labour-pool query returns fewer matches than needed) is blocked from advancing stage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]taffRoster\|func Test.*[Gg]rowthBlockedNoHire" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-5 (§45 failure is felt by real individuals — US-2).** When a firm fails (insolvency/closure, not acquisition), EVERY citizen ID in its staff roster at time of failure has its `employmentState` updated to unemployed through a `CitizensAPI` command — and citizens NOT on that roster are provably unaffected. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms` shows a failure path calling a `CitizensAPI` employment-state mutation command; a passing test fails a firm with a known staff roster, then queries `CitizensAPI` for each staff member (asserts unemployed) and for a control citizen not on the roster (asserts unchanged) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]ailure.*[Ee]mployment\|func Test.*[Ss]taffUnemployed" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`). **Lazy implementation this rejects:** decrementing an aggregate "jobs" counter on failure without ever touching a citizen record would satisfy "employment fell" on any city-wide dashboard read while leaving every individual citizen record silently stale — the check requires the specific staff IDs' own records to change.

### Functional — progression and inputs

- **AC-6 (§45 four-stage progression).** The four documented stages — Startup (1-5 staff, unit/home office), Small (6-25), Medium (26-250), Enterprise (250+, multi-site, may export/list) — are distinct, ordered states with the staff-count bands above enforced as advancement gates (not merely descriptive labels). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms Stage` (or equivalent enum) lists all four in order; a passing test asserts a firm cannot skip a stage (e.g. Startup directly to Medium) even if staff count would otherwise qualify (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]tageProgress\|func Test.*[Nn]oSkipStage" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-7 (§45 growth needs — premises via `engine.build`).** A growth attempt queries `engine.build` (the registered outbound edge) for a premises match (right zone class, right size for the target stage); a firm that cannot secure matching premises is blocked from advancing and, per §45's "dies or leaves" framing, enters a documented stalled/exit state distinct from simple failure. Check: `grep -rn "build\.\(PremisesQuery\|ZoneAt\)" internal/engine/firms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows a real call into `engine.build`'s package; a passing test denies premises and asserts the firm enters the stalled/exit state rather than silently advancing anyway (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Pp]remises.*[Bb]lock\|func Test.*[Nn]oPremises" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-8 (§45/§33 inputs via `engine.market`).** A firm consuming §33-chain commodity inputs queries `MarketAPI.Price`/`Availability` (the registered outbound edge) for its input commodities, and an input shortfall (per `engine.market`'s capacity-bounded availability, `engine.market.md` AC-5) constrains the firm's output/growth rather than being silently absorbed. Check: `grep -rn "market\.\(Price\|Availability\)" internal/engine/firms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows a real call; a passing test constrains a commodity's availability below a firm's requested input and asserts the firm's output/growth rate is reduced accordingly (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]nputShortfall\|func Test.*[Mm]arketConstrain" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-9 (§45 failure and churn are normal).** Insolvency and acquisition are both modelled as distinct, independently constructible outcomes (insolvency: assets/credit insufficient to continue trading; acquisition: absorbed into another firm, staff roster transfers rather than unemploys), and a passing test demonstrates a healthy economy churns — over a multi-month synthetic run with realistic founding/failure rates, both the founded-firm count and the failed-firm count are nonzero. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms` shows distinct `Insolvency`/`Acquisition` outcome types; a passing test constructs each independently and asserts an acquired firm's staff roster transfers to the acquirer (no employment-state change) while an insolvent firm's roster unemploys per AC-5 (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]nsolvency\|func Test.*[Aa]cquisition" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`). **Lazy implementation this rejects:** treating acquisition as a special case of failure (unemploying the acquired staff, then "re-hiring" them into the acquirer) would pass a shallow "acquisition happens" check while violating the roster-transfer distinction §45 implies by naming acquisition separately from insolvency.

### Functional — entrepreneur culture and professional services

- **AC-10 (§45 entrepreneur culture as a measured city property).** Entrepreneur culture (startups founded per 1,000 population, over a documented rolling window) is exposed as its own queryable value on `FirmsAPI`, computed from real founding events (per AC-2/AC-3), not a separately-maintained shadow counter. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms` shows a culture-index accessor; a passing test asserts the index rises when the underlying founding-event rate rises over the same window, using the same events AC-2's founding path produces (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]ulture[Ii]ndex\|func Test.*[Ss]tartupsPer1k" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-11 (§45 professional/financial services demand — superlinear).** Professional/financial-services-sector firm demand (from non-services firms consuming accounting/legal/insurance/banking) grows superlinearly with the count of non-services firms served: doubling the served firm count more than doubles the professional-services demand figure, not a flat per-firm coefficient. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms` shows a services-demand accessor parameterised by served-firm count; a passing test asserts `Demand(2n) > 2 * Demand(n)` for at least one representative `n` (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]uperlinear\|func Test.*[Ss]ervicesDemand" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`). **Lazy implementation this rejects:** `Demand(n) = k*n` (linear, the simplest plausible build) satisfies "services demand exists and grows with firm count" on a shallow read but fails this specific superlinearity assertion, which is exactly what §45/§II.4's "superlinearly" claim requires distinguishing.
- **AC-12 (§45 culture compounds — exits fund angels fund startups).** A citizen who was a founder of a successfully-exited (acquired, or grown to Enterprise and later exited) firm has a measurably higher subsequent founding/angel-investment probability than an otherwise-identical citizen with no exit history — sourced from that citizen's own founding-history record (queryable via `CitizensAPI` or a firms-owned founder-history ledger keyed by `CitizenID`), not a population-wide "culture is high" multiplier applied blind to individual history. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms` shows a founder-history-aware input to the founding evaluator; a passing test compares two otherwise-identical citizens, one with a logged successful exit and one without, and asserts the exited citizen's founding/angel probability is higher (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ee]xitHistory\|func Test.*[Aa]ngelBoost" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).

### Functional — banking and credit

- **AC-13 (§45 banking layer, deposits→credit).** Firm credit approvals (loan amount granted) are bounded by the bank's deposit pool (sourced via `engine.finance`, the registered outbound edge) — a credit request cannot be approved for more than the deposit-backed lending capacity allows, and lowering the modelled deposit pool lowers the maximum approvable credit for an otherwise-identical request. Check: `grep -rn "finance\.\(DepositPool\|LendingCapacity\)" internal/engine/firms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows a real call; a passing test lowers the deposit pool and asserts a previously-approved credit request is now denied or reduced (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]epositBound\|func Test.*[Cc]reditCap" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-14 (§45 off-map rate cycle — citywide weather, not a fixed cost).** Firm borrowing cost is a documented function of the off-map central-bank base-rate cycle (loaded from `external_world.json` per code.json's inbound pattern text), and a rate-cycle spike measurably raises the failure rate among credit-dependent (Startup/Small stage) firms in a synthetic run, without any player command altering the rate. Check: `grep -n "external_world" internal/engine/firms/*.go` finds the rate-cycle data source; a passing test drives a rate spike through the loaded cycle and asserts Startup/Small-stage insolvency events increase over an otherwise-identical baseline run (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]ateCycle\|func Test.*[Rr]ateSpike" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`). **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a fixed borrowing-cost constant that ignores the loaded rate cycle would satisfy "firms can get credit" while making §45's explicit "a rate spike is a citywide weather system: startups starve" claim untrue — the check requires the spike to actually move the failure rate.

### Error handling

- **AC-15 (GR#7).** Founding a firm against a `CitizenID` that does not resolve via `CitizensAPI`, or issuing a growth/failure command against an unregistered `FirmID`, returns a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code, claimed against `data/errors.json`'s reserved table at build time — no `engine.firms` sub-range exists yet) rather than silently creating a placeholder firm/citizen. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/firms/*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Uu]nknownCitizen\|func Test.*[Uu]nknownFirm" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly (BUG-100):** the test asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no placeholder firm/citizen record was created, not merely that a matching-named test function exists.
- **AC-16.** A growth command targeting a firm already at Enterprise stage (no further stage to advance to), or a negative/zero staff-count query, is rejected with a typed error, never silently clamped or silently ignored. Check: passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Aa]lreadyEnterprise\|func Test.*[Ii]nvalidStaffCount" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).

## Aggregate — vacancy-vs-workforce labour market (FEAT-167 JobAvailability)

> **Added 2026-08-19 (BA, FEAT-167).** This section specifies the vacancy-vs-workforce aggregate that makes migration's `JobAvailability` term — today a flat `baselineOneTermValue = 50.0` placeholder (`docs/planning/icd/engine.attract-terms.md` §3 "REMAIN PLACEHOLDER") — computable from real firm + citizen state. These ACs describe the aggregate surface in `engine.firms`; the composition-root read that feeds the term is a separate follow-up gated on a not-yet-registered consumer edge (see Escalations and `docs/planning/icd/engine.firms-labourmarket.md`). The aggregate introduces **no new outbound edge**: it consumes `CitizensAPI` (already registered) for the workforce side and computes the vacancy side from its own roster state.

### Aggregate functional

- **AC-21 (vacancies from real rosters, not a shadow counter — GR#15).** A `TotalVacancies()` accessor returns the city-wide vacancy count: Σ over every firm of `max(0, bandCeiling(stage) − len(Staff))`, where `Staff` is the firm's real `[]uint64` citizen roster (AC-4) and `bandCeiling` is **derived from `data/firms.json`** — for Startup/Small/Medium, the next stage's `minStaff − 1`; for Enterprise, a data-declared ceiling (see Escalations) — never a Go-literal `5`/`25`/`250` headcount (GR#15). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/firms TotalVacancies`; a passing test registers a Startup with 1 staff and a Medium with 10 staff and asserts `TotalVacancies` equals the headroom computed from the **loaded** `data/firms.json` floors, and that changing a floor in the fixture changes the result without any code change (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Vv]acanc" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a hardcoded `Σ(5−len)` formula would pass a fixed-fixture check while violating GR#15 — the check asserts the result tracks a mutated data floor, which a hardcoded build fails.

- **AC-22 (workforce from citizens — the registered edge).** The aggregate reads the labour-supply side from `CitizensAPI.TotalPopulation` (the registered `engine.firms → engine.citizens` edge, already wired via `SetCitizens`), exposed as `LabourMarket()`'s `Workforce` field — a live query, not a baked-in constant. Check: a passing test wires a `CitizensAPI` with a known population, asserts `LabourMarket().Workforce == thatPopulation`, then raises the population and asserts the field tracks it (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]abourMarket\|func Test.*[Ww]orkforce" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`). **Honesty note (not a FAIL):** `TotalPopulation` is a labour-*supply proxy* — `CitizensAPI` exposes no working-age/unemployment aggregate today (see Escalations); a real working-age filter would refine, not replace, the `Workforce` denominator.

- **AC-23 (the ratio — vacancy-vs-workforce).** `LabourMarket()` returns the ratio `VacancyRatePerMille = 0 when Workforce == 0, else clamp(vacancies × 1000 / workforce)` — integer arithmetic, division-by-zero guarded, never NaN/Inf — the figure the migration wiring maps onto `JobAvailability`. Check: a passing test asserts the ratio equals the exact integer division for a known `(vacancies, workforce)` fixture, and asserts directionality: adding a firm with vacancies (workforce held) strictly raises it; raising workforce (vacancies held) strictly lowers it (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Vv]acancyRate\|func Test.*[Jj]obAvailability" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).

- **AC-24 (GR#17 — unwired dependency fails loudly).** `LabourMarket()` called before `SetCitizens` returns `ErrDependencyMissing` (`MET-G1409`), never a zero `Workforce` that silently makes the ratio read "no jobs" (GR#17/GR#1). Check: a passing test constructs a `FirmsAPI` without wiring citizens and asserts the returned error's registry code is `MET-G1409`, not a zero-value return (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]abourMarket.*[Dd]ependency\|func Test.*[Uu]nwired" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).

### Determinism & safety

- **AC-25 (GR#21).** `TotalVacancies`/`LabourMarket` are deterministic: firms are iterated in ascending `FirmID` order (never `range` over the `firms` map on the aggregate path), and the ratio is a pure integer computation — repeated calls and worker-count variations produce byte-identical results. Check: `grep -n "sort\." internal/engine/firms/*.go` shows sorted iteration feeding the aggregate; a determinism test asserts two identical constructions return byte-identical `TotalVacancies`/`LabourMarket` across worker counts (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]etermnis\|func Test.*[Dd]eterminis" internal/engine/firms/*_test.go`).

- **AC-26 (SG-7; GR#21).** No `time.Now`/`time.Since` on the aggregate path — the ratio is a pure function of firm roster + citizens population at the moment it is called, never wall clock. Check: `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since" internal/engine/firms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows no new matches introduced by the aggregate.

### Documentation

- **AC-27.** `internal/engine/firms/doc.go` documents the aggregate's shape (`TotalVacancies`, `LabourMarket`), the band-ceiling derivation from `data/firms.json`, the `TotalPopulation` workforce proxy (AC-22's honesty note), and the ratio semantics (AC-23). Check: `grep -n "TotalVacancies\|LabourMarket" internal/engine/firms/doc.go` and `grep -n "bandCeiling\|workforce" internal/engine/firms/doc.go` both match.
