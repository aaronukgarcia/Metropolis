## North-star vetting (dev-team-process.md v1.12 BA mandate)

This item is a hybrid, and the vetting states which half is entertainment and which half is plumbing, rather than claiming a score it does not earn:

- **Q5 (long bet) — the headline, answered directly.** §6's own closing sentence is "a north-star long bet": *sell ore cheap now, or invest in the works and sell refined dear later.* That is exactly north-star Q5's "stretching, perhaps early, perhaps before you can comfortably afford it, to build the university, knowing the benefits pay long-term" — with "university" swapped for "cracker". The international market's world prices are what make the two legs of the bet (raw-now vs refined-later) concrete, quoted, and projectable. This item is the mechanism the long bet runs through; without it, "sell refined dear later" has no quoted price and the bet is a vibe.
- **Q1 (conflicting demand) — sharpened, inherited from the capex it gates.** Every pound of capex sunk into a cracker / integrated chemical works / pharma campus is a pound not spent on housing, transit, wellbeing, or the next district. AC-9 makes this a *quantified* trade (raw-export needs no capex; refinement needs data-sourced capex before the refined world price is reachable), which is the same graduated-choice spine `feat.extraction.md` documents for its ladder.
- **Q2 (felt no) — present in both directions.** Sell raw now and you forgo the refined margin forever (a reserve spent is spent, `feat.extraction.md` AC-4/AC-5); build the works and you tie up capex + land + a permit on a price the world may move against. Neither direction is free.
- **Q3 (consequence sticks) — present.** A built chemical works is sunk capex with a real blight footprint (MOD-063's top-blight-class stage machinery); the world price of refined product is an external data fact, not something the player can wish back up.
- **Q4 (snowball) — inherited, not caused.** The two-men-and-a-dog → Parker → integrated-works → pharma-camp ladder is the snowball; this item supplies the upper rungs' *content* (the works and the campus) and the prices that make climbing worthwhile. The snowball mechanism itself lives in `feat.extraction.md` (ladder) and MOD-063 (stage machinery).

**Framing carried into the ACs:** the *international market price board* (AC-1–AC-4) is necessary infrastructure — it answers none of the five questions on its own (no capex is spent, no consequence felt) but must not be allowed to be wrong, because every long bet built on it inherits its error. The *named facility archetypes + margin* (AC-5–AC-9) are the entertainment. The ACs below therefore hold the board to correctness/determinism/data-hygiene (like `feat.resourcedeposits.md` held the shuffle) and hold the chain/margin to the north-star property (a real, two-direction decision, never a strictly-dominant option).

## User stories

- **US-1.** As the operator of any extraction site (`feat.extraction.md`'s ladder, selling raw output), I need an always-available world price for my raw product — copper, tin, iron, uranium, REM, oil, gas, coal, and the sanctioned fictional resources — so I am never stuck with unsellable output from day one, and so the price I sell at is a real, quoted, external fact rather than a number I invented.
- **US-2.** As the same operator deciding whether to invest, I need a world price for every refined/manufactured product the works can produce (chemicals, plastics, solvents, pharma output), so the "sell refined dear later" leg of the long bet has a quoted, projectable number.
- **US-3.** As a player building toward the upper rungs, I need the spec-named facility archetypes — crackers, a Billingham/Wilton-class integrated chemical works, and a Groton-Pfizer-class pharma/R&D campus — to exist as distinct, buildable chain stages, each transforming my raw inputs into refined/manufactured outputs through the SAME chain-stage machinery `engine.chemicals.md` already specifies, not a parallel facilities system.
- **US-4.** As the player making the value-add decision, I need the refinement margin — refined world price minus raw-input world price minus processing cost — to be a queryable, data-sourced quantity, so "sell now vs refine later" is arithmetic I can see in projections, not a guess.
- **US-5.** As the player, I need refinement to be gated on real capex (and, per `feat.facilitypermits`'s separate item, a permit) so that neither "always sell raw" nor "always refine" is a strictly-dominant option — the decision must be able to go either way from real data, or the long bet has no losing side and no tension.

## Scope

The always-on international commodity market (a data-sourced world-price board for raw resource products and refined/manufactured products — the floor/ceiling), the named refinement/manufacture facility archetypes (crackers, Billingham/Wilton-class integrated chemical works, pharma/R&D campus) as data-defined chain stages layered onto `engine.chemicals`' `ChemAPI` stage machinery, and the value-add margin + capex gate that make sell-raw-now vs refine-dear-later a real decision. Does **not** include: `engine.chemicals.md`'s five-stage oil chain, pipeline network, make-vs-buy import, leak risk, or hazmat dispatch (MOD-063 — this item registers content *against* that machinery, it does not re-specify it); the extraction ladder, depletion, yield-variance, breakdown, or extraction-side price exposure (`feat.extraction.md` — this item only reads the raw-product keys that feature produces); the deposit taxonomy or world-gen shuffle (`feat.resourcedeposits.md`); prospecting/survey (`feat.resourcesurvey`); facility permits and decommissioning liability (§7 of the brief, `feat.facilitypermits`/`feat.decommission`); the FDI *bidding* mechanic that attracts the pharma campus (§46, `engine.fdi.md` — this item provides the campus's manufacture-side stage only); `engine.market.md`'s nine domestic needs-commodities and its `MarketAPI` internals (MOD-020 — consumed, never extended); mega-facilities (§8 THORP/CANDU/SpaceX/CERN — `feat.megafacilities`).

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional — the international market price board

- **AC-1 (GR#20; code.json inbound contract — currently unpopulated, mirrors `feat.resourcedeposits.md` AC-1's and `feat.extraction.md` AC-1's pattern).** A queryable international-market price surface exists on the shared `internal/engine/chemicals` package (e.g. `WorldPrice(product ProductKey) (int64, error)` or equivalent) returning, at minimum, the always-on world price for a given raw or refined product — distinct from MOD-063's `ChemAPI` chain-stage-firm surface and from MOD-020's `MarketAPI` (the domestic needs-commodity surface), and consumed by callers through this surface alone, not by reaching into a package-internal price table. Because code.json's `feat.commoditymarket` inbound contract is unpopulated (`name`/`format`/`pattern` null, `consumers: []`) and the feature shares `engine.chemicals`' package, the shared-package / no-separate-inbound-contract arrangement must be stated in the package doc. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` shows a world-price query function distinct from the `ChemAPI` stage surface and from any `MarketAPI` re-import; `internal/engine/chemicals/doc.go` notes the shared-package, no-separate-inbound-contract arrangement explicitly (`grep -n "feat.commoditymarket" internal/engine/chemicals/doc.go`).

- **AC-2 (§6 always-on floor/ceiling — raw AND refined coverage, the "never unsellable" claim).** The world-price board covers, at minimum: (a) every raw resource product in `feat.resourcedeposits.md`'s deposit taxonomy — metallic ores (copper, tin, iron, uranium, rare-earth metals), hydrocarbons/coal (gas, oil, coal), and the sanctioned fictional resource slot(s) — and (b) every refined/manufactured product this feature's own chain defines (AC-5's archetype outputs: chemical/petrochemical products, plastics/solvents, pharma output). Every one of these product keys resolves to a **positive, non-zero** world price — no product is ever unsellable, and no product is priced at zero (a zero price is the silent-unsellable case, indistinguishable from "free", and is explicitly forbidden by AC-12's error path). The product-key list and prices are data-sourced (GR#15, AC-4), never a hardcoded Go enum or literal. Check: a passing test loads the data file and asserts every product key in it resolves to a strictly-positive world price, and that the raw-product-key subset is non-empty and the refined-product-key subset is non-empty (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ww]orldPrice.*[Cc]overage\|func Test.*[Pp]ositivePrice" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a board that prices a "representative" sample (e.g. copper and one chemical) while leaving iron, uranium, or the pharma output unpriced would pass a shallow "a world price exists" read while leaving some extraction output unsellable — the check requires the *data file's own* key set to be fully covered, not a hand-picked example list.

- **AC-3 (§6 "always-on … consistent with §2.2" — a single, external clearing price, not a domestic supply/demand function).** The world price for a product is **one** price (a single clearing figure — no separate "buy" and "sell" legs, no bid/ask spread), it is **external** (a data fact about the off-map world, not a function of the city's own supply/demand or stock levels — v1 world prices are static per §16's shelved "dynamic world market" slot), and it is therefore stable across ticks when no command intervenes. This is what makes it a *floor/ceiling*: the player can always sell at it (floor — never stuck) and can never sell above it (ceiling — off-map buyers simply pay the world price, §2.2). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` shows exactly one price-returning method per product (not a `BuyPrice`/`SellPrice` pair that would create an arbitrage surface); a passing test calls `WorldPrice` twice for the same product with no intervening command and asserts byte-identical results, and asserts the returned price does not change when a locally-modelled stock/availability figure (injected in the test) is varied (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ss]inglePrice\|func Test.*[Ee]xternalPrice" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a price surface that silently wires the world price to the domestic `MarketAPI` price (which MOD-020 AC-4 establishes as a static-v1-but-dynamic-later seam) would make "external world price" and "domestic market price" the same quantity and collapse the make-vs-buy distinction this feature exists to price — the check requires the world price to be independent of injected local supply/demand state.

- **AC-4 (GR#15 — every world price and chain figure lives in a data file, never a code constant).** World prices (raw and refined), the facility-archetype definitions (AC-5), per-stage input→output conversion ratios, per-stage processing costs, and the refinement capex gate (AC-9) are ALL sourced from a data file (e.g. `data/commoditymarket.json`, following the `data/*.json` convention used by `errors.json`/`market.json`/`deposits.json`/`extraction.json`), none hardcoded as Go numeric literals, and each carries a non-empty placeholder/pending-tuning disclosure field per the balance-number regime (`data/market.json`'s convention, `feat.extraction.md` AC-12's precedent). Check: `grep -rn "0\.[0-9]\+\|[0-9]\{2,\}" internal/engine/chemicals/commodity*.go` (excluding `_test.go` and array indices/enum ordinals) shows no bare balance-shaped numeric literals; `internal/engine/chemicals/doc.go` names the data file path explicitly; a passing test asserts the board's output changes when the data file's price for a given product is edited between two test runs with the SAME seed (proving the price is actually read, not merely present in a file nobody loads) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]ataDriven.*[Cc]ommodity\|func Test.*[Ww]orldPrice.*[Rr]eload" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

### Functional — the refinement/manufacture chain (named facility archetypes)

- **AC-5 (§6 named archetypes, layered on MOD-063 machinery — GR#3 reuse, not re-specification).** At least the three spec-named facility archetypes — **crackers**, a **Billingham/Wilton-class integrated chemical works**, and a **Groton-Pfizer-class pharma/R&D campus** — exist as distinct, data-defined chain stages (each with its own input product set, output product set, jobs figure, and blight class), REGISTERED through MOD-063's `ChemAPI` chain-stage-as-firm surface (the same generic stage type `engine.chemicals.md` AC-2 specifies: input t/day → output t/day, jobs, blight class), NOT as a second, feature-owned facility/stage registry parallel to `ChemAPI`. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` shows the three archetypes as distinct stage definitions; `grep -rn "chemicals\.\(RegisterStage\|Stage\)\|ChemAPI" internal/engine/chemicals/commodity*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) confirms the archetypes are registered through the `ChemAPI` stage machinery rather than a parallel `CommodityFacility` registry with its own lifecycle; a passing test asserts the three archetypes resolve to distinct input/output product sets (a build that ships three names over one identical template fails) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Aa]rchetype.*[Ss]tage\|func Test.*[Tt]hreeArchetypes" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** three near-identical stubs differing only in a name string would satisfy "three named archetypes" on a shallow read while providing no real refinement *content* — the check requires the input/output sets to actually differ, which is what makes "cracker" vs "pharma campus" a real economic distinction.

- **AC-6 (§6 "extraction → refinement → manufacture" — the chain consumes raw products, produces refined).** The feature's stages draw their **input** product keys from the raw resource product set (AC-2's raw keys — the deposit taxonomy's outputs) and produce **refined/manufactured** product keys (AC-2's refined keys) as outputs, so the full chain "extraction → refinement → manufacture" is a real, data-expressed path from mined ground to a sellable refined product — not a self-contained loop that neither consumes extraction output nor feeds the international market. Check: a passing test loads the data file and asserts (a) at least one stage's input set is a non-empty subset of the raw product keys, and (b) at least one stage's output set is a non-empty subset of the refined product keys, and (c) the union of the refined product keys equals AC-2's refined key set (no refined product is priced by the board but unproduceable by any stage, and vice versa) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]hainDirection\|func Test.*[Rr]awToRefined" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

- **AC-7 (weakness pattern #2 — cross-package drift test for the raw-product keys).** The raw-product keys the board (AC-2) and the chain stages (AC-6) reference must agree with `feat.resourcedeposits.md`'s `DepositType` resource names — a player extracts copper, and the international market must price *copper*, so the two key sets are one fact duplicated across the `mining` and `chemicals` package boundary. Because `feat.commoditymarket`'s registered outbound edges are `engine.chemicals` + `engine.market` only (no `engine.mining` edge, verified in code.json), the raw keys are legitimately duplicated as data — so the divergence guard must be a **drift test** in a `_test.go` file (the sanctioned test-file-import exemption), asserting the board's raw key set equals the mining package's `DepositType` resource-name set, with a failure message explaining *why* the duplication exists and that changing one requires changing the other (dev-team-process.md weakness pattern #2's explicit requirements). Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Kk]eyDrift\|func Test.*[Dd]epositType.*[Kk]eys" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go` finds a test importing `internal/engine/mining`; the test's failure message names the duplication and the required coordinated change. (Blocked until the mining package exists — see Escalations.)

### Functional — the value-add margin (the long bet)

- **AC-8 (§6 "margins vs raw export … sell cheap now or sell dear later" — a queryable, real-arithmetic margin).** A margin query exists (e.g. `RefineMargin(stage) (int64, error)` or equivalent) returning, per refinement stage, the value-add: refined output world price (at the stage's documented conversion ratio) minus the raw-input world price (at the stage's documented input ratio) minus the stage's data-sourced processing cost — computed from the SAME world prices AC-1 exposes and the SAME data file AC-4 sources, not a separately hand-tuned "profit" scalar. The margin is **signed** — it must be able to come out positive *or* negative from the data, so refinement is a judgement call, not a guaranteed win. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` shows a margin query distinct from the raw `WorldPrice` query; a passing test edits the refined-product price (or the processing cost) in the data file between two runs and asserts the margin changes by the corresponding arithmetic amount, and a second test asserts the data admits at least one stage whose margin is non-positive at the placeholder values (proving the sign is not hardcoded) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]efineMargin\|func Test.*[Mm]arginArithmetic" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

- **AC-9 (§6/§7 the capex gate — sell-raw-now vs invest-and-sell-dear-later must be a genuine decision).** Reaching a stage's refined world price requires first paying that stage's data-sourced capex (and, downstream, the `feat.facilitypermits` permit that item owns — this file only requires the capex figure and the "refined price is reachable only after the stage is built" gate, it does not re-specify permits); selling raw at the raw world price requires no capex. The two quantities — raw-export price (sell now) and refined price + capex + margin (sell later) — are therefore all independently queryable, and the data must admit a case where raw-export is the better short-term choice (the refine path's capex is not trivially repaid by the margin at any plausible horizon at placeholder values), so neither option strictly dominates. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` exposes the raw-export price, the per-stage capex, and the per-stage margin as separate queryable figures (not one fused "export decision" flag); a passing test asserts that for at least one stage, at placeholder data values, `capex` is strictly positive and `margin` is not large enough to make the refined path a no-brainer at a documented placeholder horizon — i.e., a build that ships all-zero capex or all-huge margins (making refine always dominate) fails (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]apexGate\|func Test.*[Nn]otDominant" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** an always-win refine path (zero capex, or a margin so large the capex is cosmetic) would pass a shallow "a margin and a capex figure exist" read while gutting the north-star decision — this AC requires the *data* to make the decision real, the same way `feat.extraction.md` AC-3 requires the ladder to be non-degenerate.

- **AC-10 (GR#20 — consumes only registered edges, through interfaces only).** The feature consumes MOD-063's `ChemAPI` (stage machinery, AC-5) and MOD-020's `MarketAPI` (money representation, AC-11, and any domestic input prices the chain stages need) through those registered interfaces alone — no direct import of any unregistered package (`engine.mining`, `engine.freight`, `engine.fdi`, `engine.logistics`), no direct exported-field write into another package's state, and no reimplementation of a `ChemAPI` stage or a `MarketAPI` price inside this feature's own files. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/chemicals` shows the price/margin return type is an integer/fixed-point type; `grep -n "float32\|float64" internal/engine/chemicals/commodity*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no match on the price/margin path.

### Error handling

- **AC-12 (GR#7 — unknown product key).** Querying `WorldPrice` (or `RefineMargin`) for a product key not present in the data file returns a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code), never a panic and never a silently-returned zero price (a zero world price would be read as "this product is worth nothing / sellable for free", which is exactly the unsellable-but-misreported case AC-2 forbids). Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/chemicals/commodity*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage asserts the returned error's registry code matches the claimed `MET-E` code AND that no zero-valued price was returned as if it were real, not merely that a matching-named test function exists (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Uu]nknownProduct\|func Test.*[Ii]nvalidProduct" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`).

- **AC-13 (GR#7 — malformed/missing data file).** Loading the commodity-market data file (AC-4) when it is missing, malformed (invalid JSON), or fails schema validation (a negative or zero price, an unrecognised stage archetype name, an inverted or non-positive input→output conversion ratio, a stage whose input/output keys fall outside the board's own key set) produces a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code) and the board does NOT proceed with silent defaults or a partially-populated price table. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/chemicals/commodity*.go` finds a second, distinct registry code for the load path; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]issingCommodityData\|func Test.*[Mm]alformedCommodityData" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly:** the test asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no product was silently priced as a result of the failed load (an all-or-nothing load, not a partial one silently continuing).

### Determinism, safety & scale

- **AC-14 (GR#21).** World-price, margin, and chain-stage-definition queries are pure/deterministic functions of `(loaded data, tick, command state)` — byte-identical results across repeated runs and worker/shard counts (SG-7). Prices are static v1 data (AC-3), so the determinism claim is mostly about the margin/chain computations not introducing nondeterminism (e.g. map-iteration order in a stage lookup). Check: a passing determinism test exists and asserts identical output across repeated calls and (if feasible) worker counts (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]eterminis.*[Cc]ommodity\|func Test.*[Dd]eterminis.*[Ww]orldPrice" internal/engine/chemicals/*_test.go`); a companion assertion confirms a DIFFERENT data-file price produces a measurably different result, proving the data is actually consumed (the can-never-fail trap the mining siblings both flag as a real prior instance).

- **AC-15 (SG-7 scoped; GR#21).** `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since" internal/engine/chemicals/commodity*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches — prices and margins are functions of loaded data and simulation state only, never wall clock.
