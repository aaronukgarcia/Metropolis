## North-star vetting (dev-team-process.md v1.12 BA mandate)

Unlike `feat.resourcedeposits.md` (necessary infrastructure, answers none of the five questions directly), this item is the entertainment layer that infrastructure exists to enable — it should score strongly on multiple questions, and the case is made explicitly below rather than asserted:

- **Q1 (conflicting demand, sharpened directly).** Every unit of capex committed to an extraction tier upgrade is capex not spent on the rest of the pie (housing, transit, wellbeing, the next district). The ladder makes this a *graduated* choice rather than a single yes/no: artisanal is nearly free and barely dents the budget, but industrial-tier capex is a serious, felt claim on finite money (§10 "capex-hungry extraction vs everything else the pie funds" — this file is the mechanism that claim runs through).
- **Q2 (felt no).** Choosing to fund an extraction-tier upgrade is choosing NOT to fund something else this cycle; choosing to run artisanal-only is choosing to leave money on the table a Parker-scale operation would have banked, at lower risk exposure. Both directions have a felt no — under-investing costs foregone revenue (AC-3's ladder), over-investing costs breakdown/variance downside risk (AC-9/AC-10) and, eventually, the depletion bill (AC-6).
- **Q3 (consequence sticks, directly).** Deposits deplete and never refill (AC-5, cross-referencing `feat.resourcedeposits.md`'s fixed, never-reshuffled map) — a reserve spent is spent forever. A mine-dependent district that never diversified is the self-seeded §32 Detroit test (AC-6): the consequence is not a scripted event, it is the arithmetic of AC-5's draw-down finally reaching zero, which the player watched coming via AC-7's projection and chose not to act on.
- **Q4 (snowball, directly — this IS the ladder).** §5's own framing ("two men and a dog... the early-game snowball seed") makes the ladder itself the snowball mechanic: artisanal revenue at 2,000 residents funds the capex for an industrial upgrade later, exactly the "small numbers matter enormously early, compounding does the work late" pattern the north-star names for education. This file's AC-3 (monotonic capex/yield/risk ordering across rungs) is the mechanical spine of that snowball.
- **Q5 (long bet, directly).** Committing industrial-tier capex against a deposit whose reserve is known only to survey-confidence, not proven-reserve certainty (AC-9's yield-variance mechanic, `feat.resourcedeposits.md`'s Q5 framing of the underlying map as a fixed bet), is exactly "stretching before you can comfortably afford it, knowing the benefits pay long-term" — and AC-7/AC-8 (projection + warning ledger) are what make that stretch a judgement call rather than a blind gamble, per the north-star's own Q5 closing clause: "the deferred payoff real and visible enough to justify the stretch."

This item answers all five questions, which is unusual and is stated as a finding, not a rhetorical flourish: the extraction ladder is close to a distilled instance of the north-star thesis applied to one subsystem.

## User stories

- **US-1.** As a player with a freshly prospected, low-confidence deposit, I need an artisanal extraction option with trivial capex and low risk, so I can start banking revenue immediately without betting the treasury on ground I haven't proven — the early-game snowball seed §5 names.
- **US-2.** As a player who has banked artisanal revenue (or otherwise accumulated capital) and wants to scale, I need a documented ladder of intermediate rungs up to an industrial, Parker-style tier with serious throughput and serious capex, so the upgrade decision is graduated rather than an all-or-nothing leap.
- **US-3.** As the operator of any extraction tier, I need my output to draw down the SAME finite `feat.resourcedeposits.md` deposit record my site sits on, so "the ground resource is finite" is a real shared-state fact, not a per-tier fiction each rung reimplements independently.
- **US-4.** As the operator of an industrial-tier site with a large workforce, I need my site's exhaustion to feed the SAME workforce-at-risk figure `engine.mining.md` AC-10 already exposes at closure, so the §32 Detroit test is felt at the scale the ladder itself created (a bigger rung, a bigger bill) rather than as a flat, tier-blind number.
- **US-5.** As a player deciding whether to keep investing in a site, I need a projected months-to-depletion figure and a documented warning threshold BEFORE the site actually runs dry, so depletion is squeezed, never ambushed, per §5's explicit language and the projections module's own established `Margin*`/`WarningLedger` pattern.
- **US-6.** As the operator of any tier, I need my realised yield to sometimes diverge from the deposit's surveyed estimate — more so at lower survey confidence — and my industrial-tier operation additionally to carry a real chance of a breakdown that halts output for a period, so "real operating risk" is a mechanic I can feel, not a line in a design brief.
- **US-7.** As the operator of any tier, I need my extraction revenue to track the live `engine.market` commodity price rather than a price frozen at site creation, so "price exposure" is a real, ongoing risk exactly as §5 names it.

## Scope

The extraction-scale ladder itself (artisanal through industrial Parker-style, with the interior rung count and exact per-rung capex/yield/risk curve data-driven per GR#15); the draw-down mechanic that ties any tier's output to `feat.resourcedeposits.md`'s finite `Deposit` record and produces exhaustion; the extraction-side feed into `engine.mining.md`'s existing workforce-at-risk/closure surface (not a new closure mechanic — a data feed into the existing one); depletion-visibility projections and a warning ledger via the already-registered `feat.extraction`→`engine.projections` edge; yield-variance-against-survey-estimate as a stochastic operating-risk mechanic; industrial-tier breakdown/downtime events; price exposure via the already-registered (shared-package) `engine.mining`→`engine.market` edge. Does **not** include: the deposit taxonomy, geology co-location, or world-gen shuffle (`feat.resourcedeposits.md`); `BlightAPI`, siting, noise/viewshed, reclamation commands, or the closure/workforce-at-risk figure's own definition (`engine.mining.md` — this item only feeds it a rising figure as reserve depletes, it does not redefine it); survey/prospecting progression or proven-reserve confidence itself (`feat.resourcesurvey`/FEAT-050 — this item only consumes a confidence figure that item produces); the international market's own price-formation mechanics (`engine.market.md`/MOD-020 — this item only queries the live price); refinement/manufacture, facility permits, or decommissioning liability (§6-8 of the brief, separate feature items).

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional — the scale ladder

- **AC-1 (GR#20; shared-package registered call surface).** Extraction-tier commands (site an extraction operation at a given tier, upgrade/downgrade a tier, query current tier and its documented capex/yield/risk profile) exist on the shared `internal/engine/mining` package, distinct from `engine.mining.md` AC-1's `BlightAPI`/siting surface and `feat.resourcedeposits.md` AC-1's `DepositAt` query, consuming both via direct calls rather than reimplementing either. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/mining` shows an extraction-tier command/query set distinct from the `BlightAPI` and `DepositAt` accessors; `grep -n "mining\.\(BlightAPI\|RegisterBlightingObject\)\|DepositAt" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` confirms the extraction code calls into the siting and deposit surfaces rather than duplicating them; `internal/engine/mining/doc.go` notes the shared-package, no-separate-inbound-contract arrangement (mirroring `feat.resourcedeposits.md` AC-1's convention, since `feat.extraction`'s own code.json inbound contract is currently unpopulated — `consumers: []`).
- **AC-2 (§5 the two spec-named anchor tiers, minimum ladder).** At least two distinct, named extraction tiers exist and are documented: an **artisanal** tier ("two men and a dog" — trivial capex, small yield, low risk) and an **industrial** tier (Parker-style — serious capex, serious throughput, real operating risk), each with its own capex cost, base yield rate, and risk profile (AC-9/AC-10) sourced from the GR#15 data file (AC-12) rather than hardcoded. The exact count and shape of any interior rungs between these two anchors is data-driven and not specified numerically by this file (balance regime, see Escalations) — but the two spec-named anchors themselves are mandatory, not optional data-file entries that could be omitted. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/mining` shows a `Tier` (or equivalent) enum/type with at least an artisanal-equivalent and an industrial-equivalent value; `grep -rn "func Test.*[Tt]ierTaxonomy\|func Test.*[Aa]rtisanal.*[Ii]ndustrial" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go` finds a passing test asserting both named anchor tiers exist and resolve to distinct, non-zero-valued profiles from the data file.
- **AC-3 (§5 monotonic ordering — the ladder's mechanical spine, US-2/Q4).** Across the full tier ladder (from artisanal to industrial), capex cost and base throughput/yield rate are both monotonically non-decreasing rung-to-rung, and at least one documented risk-exposure metric (breakdown probability, yield-variance magnitude, or both — AC-9/AC-10) is also monotonically non-decreasing, so a higher rung is never simultaneously cheaper, lower-yield, AND lower-risk than the rung below it (which would make the ladder pointless — there'd be no reason to ever choose a lower rung). Check: a passing test loads the full tier list from the data file and asserts capex and base-yield are non-decreasing across the ordered tier sequence, and that the risk-exposure metric is non-decreasing across the SAME sequence (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]adder.*[Mm]onotonic\|func Test.*[Tt]ierOrdering" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a data file that merely lists tiers with plausible-looking numbers, never checked for the ordering property, could accidentally (or via a careless balance edit) produce a "free lunch" rung that dominates on every axis — this test is what catches that at data-load time rather than in a much later balance review.

### Functional — finite depletion (draws down `feat.resourcedeposits.md`)

- **AC-4 (cross-reference, not duplication — the shared reserve).** Extraction output at any tier is computed against, and reduces, the SAME `Deposit` record's remaining reserve that `feat.resourcedeposits.md` AC-1's `DepositAt` query returns for that cell/site — there is no tier-local or extraction-owned copy of remaining reserve. Check: `grep -n "DepositAt\|deposit\.Remaining\|deposit\.Size" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows extraction code reading and mutating the SAME record type `feat.resourcedeposits.md` defines, not a parallel field; a passing test extracts via one tier, re-queries `DepositAt` for the same cell, and asserts the returned remaining-reserve value has decreased by the extracted amount (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]rawDown\|func Test.*[Rr]eserveDeplet" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`).
- **AC-5 (§5 "run out" — finite exhaustion, ties to `engine.mining.md` AC-9's `Reclaim`).** A deposit's remaining reserve reaching zero halts further extraction output at that site regardless of tier (an industrial-tier site draws down faster per AC-3's higher throughput, and therefore reaches zero SOONER on an identical-size deposit than an artisanal-tier site would — this file does not re-derive that from first principles, it is the direct arithmetic consequence of AC-3's throughput ordering applied to AC-4's shared reserve), and the exhausted site becomes eligible for `engine.mining.md` AC-9's `Reclaim` command — this file does not reimplement reclamation, it only produces the exhausted state that command consumes. Check: a passing test drives a deposit's reserve to zero via repeated extraction and asserts (a) further extraction attempts are rejected (AC-13's registry error) and (b) the site's exhausted state is the SAME state `engine.mining.md` AC-9's `Reclaim` test fixture checks for, not a separate exhaustion flag `Reclaim` would need to be taught to recognise (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ee]xhaust" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`); a companion test seeds two identical-size deposits, extracts at artisanal tier from one and industrial tier from the other at their respective documented throughput rates, and asserts the industrial-tier deposit reaches zero reserve in strictly fewer ticks.
- **AC-6 (§32/§5 the Detroit-test feed — cross-reference, not a new closure mechanic).** As an industrial-tier site's deposit reserve falls below a data-sourced (GR#15) low-reserve threshold, the workforce headcount this file's tier data associates with that site is what `engine.mining.md` AC-10's workforce-at-risk query returns for it — this file supplies the tier-scaled headcount figure and the falling-reserve trigger condition; `engine.mining.md` AC-10 owns the query surface and the "queryable before closure" contract itself, which this file does not restate. An artisanal-tier site (by AC-2's "two men and a dog" definition) carries a workforce-at-risk figure of at most a handful of people, never the large figure an industrial site carries — proving the Detroit-test severity is felt at the scale the player's OWN tier-upgrade choice created. Check: a passing test seeds an industrial-tier site and an artisanal-tier site with equal remaining reserve, drives both below the low-reserve threshold, and asserts `engine.mining.md`'s workforce-at-risk query returns a materially larger figure for the industrial site (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ww]orkforceByTier\|func Test.*[Dd]etroitTest.*[Tt]ier" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a fixed, tier-blind workforce-at-risk constant would pass `engine.mining.md` AC-10's own test (which only checks "nonzero, tied to a specific mine") without this file's tier-differentiation ever being exercised — this AC is what proves the ladder's scale actually reaches the closure consequence, not just that a closure consequence exists somewhere.

### Functional — depletion visibility (squeezed, never ambushed)

- **AC-7 (§5 "depletion is visible... surveys, projections" — the concrete anti-ambush check, US-5).** `ProjectionsAPI` (via the already-registered `feat.extraction`→`engine.projections` call edge) exposes a query (e.g. `MonthsToDepletion`, mirroring `engine.projections.md` AC-17/AC-18's `Margin*` naming and `Computed`/`Extrapolated`/`Unavailable` confidence pattern) returning a time-to-exhaustion estimate for a given extraction site, computed by extrapolating the SAME reserve-draw-down curve AC-4/AC-5 produce — this file does not reimplement `engine.projections.md`'s extrapolation/confidence machinery, it registers a curve provider against it. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/mining` (or `internal/engine/projections`, whichever owns the registered provider) shows a depletion-projection query; a passing test registers the extraction site's real draw-down curve at three successively worsening extraction-rate states (paused → artisanal-only → industrial-tier-upgraded) and asserts the returned months-remaining figure strictly decreases across the three, then simulates a tier downgrade back to paused and asserts the figure increases again (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]onthsToDeplet" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go internal/engine/projections/*_test.go`) — the same monotonic-and-recovering shape `engine.projections.md` AC-17 already requires for `MarginToInsolvency`, applied here to reserve depletion. **False-pass risk:** a one-way "reserve is low" flag that never recovers when extraction is paused would pass a shallow "a warning exists" read while failing to prove the figure tracks the actual draw-down rate, which is what makes it trustworthy enough for a player to plan against.
- **AC-8 (`engine.projections.md` AC-19's `WarningLedger` — reused, not duplicated).** Crossing a data-sourced (GR#15) low-reserve warning threshold for a given site produces a `WarningLedger` entry (sim month, site, reserve-remaining-at-crossing) via the SAME ledger `engine.projections.md` AC-19 already specifies for `MarginToInsolvency`/`MarginToGhostCity` — this file does not create a second, extraction-owned ledger. Check: a passing test drives a site's reserve down through the documented threshold at a known sim month and asserts a `WarningLedger` entry appears for that site at that month via `engine.projections`'s existing query-by-metric surface, and that a query for a month range entirely before the crossing returns no entry for that site (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ww]arningLedger.*[Dd]eplet\|func Test.*[Dd]epletionWarning" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go internal/engine/projections/*_test.go`).

### Functional — yield variance, breakdowns, price exposure (real operating risk)

- **AC-9 (§5 "yield variance against survey estimates" — a real stochastic mechanic, US-6).** Realised extraction output per tick diverges from `feat.resourcedeposits.md`'s surveyed size/density estimate for that deposit by a stochastic amount whose magnitude is inversely related to the deposit's survey confidence (a lower-confidence survey produces a wider variance band; a fully-proven reserve produces a narrow or zero band) — data-sourced (GR#15) curve shape, not a fixed percentage applied uniformly regardless of confidence. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/mining` shows a yield-variance accessor distinct from the base-rate accessor (AC-2); a passing test compares realised-yield variance across two otherwise-identical sites differing only in survey-confidence level and asserts the lower-confidence site's variance is measurably wider (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Yy]ieldVarian" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a flat ±X% variance applied to every site regardless of survey confidence would satisfy "yield sometimes differs from estimate" on a shallow read while failing the actual "against survey estimates" claim, which requires confidence to modulate the band.
- **AC-10 (§5 "breakdowns" — industrial-tier operating risk, US-6).** The industrial tier (and any interior rung documented as carrying mechanical throughput, per the data file) carries a data-sourced (GR#15) breakdown probability per tick that, when triggered, halts that site's output for a documented downtime duration; the artisanal tier ("two men and a dog") carries zero or negligible breakdown risk, documented explicitly as a deliberate tier distinction rather than an oversight. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/mining` shows a breakdown/downtime accessor; a passing test forces a breakdown trigger on an industrial-tier site and asserts output is zero for the documented downtime window before resuming, and a companion assertion over a large artisanal-tier sample shows breakdown events at or near zero frequency (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Bb]reakdown" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`).
- **AC-11 (§5 "price exposure" — the shared `engine.mining`→`engine.market` edge, US-7).** Extraction revenue for realised yield is computed against the LIVE price `engine.market`'s `MarketAPI` returns at the time of sale (via `engine.mining`'s own already-registered outbound edge to `engine.market`, shared-package — this file does not register a second, competing edge), not a price snapshotted at site creation or tier upgrade. Check: `grep -n "market\.MarketAPI\|market\.Price" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) confirms the revenue path queries the shared market edge; a passing test computes revenue for an identical realised-yield amount under two different injected market prices and asserts the two revenue figures differ proportionally (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Pp]riceExposure\|func Test.*[Mm]arketPrice.*[Rr]evenue" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`).

### Functional — GR#15 balance data

- **AC-12 (GR#15 — every ladder/risk parameter lives in a data file, not code constants).** Per-tier capex, base yield rate, breakdown probability and downtime duration, yield-variance-curve shape (and its survey-confidence modulation), and low-reserve warning thresholds (AC-2/AC-3/AC-6/AC-8/AC-9/AC-10) are ALL sourced from a data file (e.g. `data/extraction.json`, following the `data/*.json` convention already used by `errors.json`/`modes.json`/`deposits.json`), none hardcoded as Go numeric literals, and each entry carries a non-empty placeholder/pending-tuning disclosure field per the balance-number regime (`data/market.json`'s "pending M2 Batch tuning" convention, `feat.resourcedeposits.md` AC-6's precedent). Check: `grep -rn "0\.[0-9]\+\|[0-9]\{2,\}" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` (excluding `_test.go` and array indices/enum ordinals) shows no bare balance-shaped numeric literals; `go doc ./internal/engine/mining` or `internal/engine/mining/doc.go` names the data file path explicitly; a passing test asserts the ladder's output changes when the data file's industrial-tier capex or breakdown-rate value is edited between two test runs with the SAME seed, proving the parameter is actually read (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]ataDriven.*[Ee]xtraction\|func Test.*[Tt]ierParams" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`); a companion test asserts every tier entry carries a non-empty disclosure field (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Pp]laceholderDisclosure" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`).

### Error handling

- **AC-13 (GR#7).** Attempting to extract from an already-exhausted deposit (AC-5), site an extraction tier without a valid `feat.resourcedeposits.md` deposit record present (i.e. against ungeologically-gated ground `engine.mining.md` AC-11 already rejects at siting time), or upgrade to an undefined/out-of-taxonomy tier, produces a registry-sourced error (new `MET-E`-range code) rather than a silent no-op or a zero-yield success. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage asserts the returned error's registry code matches the claimed `MET-E` code AND that no extraction/tier-change record was created or mutated as a side effect, not merely that a matching-named test function exists (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ee]xhaustedExtract\|func Test.*[Ii]nvalidTier" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`).

- **AC-14 (GR#21).** Yield-variance draws (AC-9) and breakdown-trigger draws (AC-10) use `engine.world`'s counter-based hash stream (`hash(worldSeed, tileID, cellID, tick, purposeTag)` or equivalent) exclusively — never a shared/global `math/rand` source — so identical `(worldSeed, tick, prior state, commands)` reproduce byte-identical extraction outcomes across repeated runs and worker/shard counts (SG-7). Check: `grep -n "rand.New\|math/rand\"" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no shared/global RNG source; a passing test runs identical extraction commands twice with the same seed (once single-threaded, once at the package's configured worker count) and asserts byte-identical yield, variance, and breakdown outcomes (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]eterminis.*[Ee]xtraction\|func Test.*[Ss]hardInvarian.*[Ee]xtraction" internal/engine/mining/*_test.go`). - **AC-15 (SG-7 scoped; GR#21).** `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since" internal/engine/mining/extraction*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches — extraction outcomes are a pure function of `(worldSeed, tick, deposit state, tier, commands)`, never wall clock.
