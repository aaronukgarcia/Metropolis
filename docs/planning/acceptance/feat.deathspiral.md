## User stories

- **US-1.** As the player with a finite monthly budget, I need policing spend (clearance + prevention + the MI5 liaison) to genuinely compete with education/health/fire spend — so every credit into police is a credit out of the social infrastructure, and the consequences of the trade are visible in both directions (crime falls but schools/health strain; or the reverse).
- **US-2.** As the game, I need a single unified decay score `S ∈ [0,1]` computed each tick that aggregates the four causal families (crime, blight/refuse, uncollected bodies/burnt structures, financial distress) with the same weights I can inspect — so the Death Spiral is one legible number, not four disconnected meters, and it is the same number every consumer (growth, migration, warnings, death gate) reacts to.
- **US-3.** As the player, I need growth to slow as decay rises (mild at `S > 0.2`, emigration onset at `S > 0.6`) and to recover when I fix the causes — so the spiral is escapable, matching §12's "recovery is possible but expensive", never a one-way scripted descent.
- **US-4.** As the player who ignores decay, I need `S ≥ 0.8` sustained across a consecutive window to log to the WarningLedger and eventually terminate as the canonical ghost-city game-over (`ErrGhostCityNoWarning`) — so the death gate is legible and consistent with the existing §12 ghost-city ending, never a second, parallel "you died" path.
- **US-5.** As a player in a cash-strapped city, I need to be able to buy spare jail capacity with an "Import Out-of-Tile Inmates" contract earning a fixed monthly per-inmate payout — but with the honest risk that high occupancy under low staffing/funding triggers riots, escapes, and recidivism that leak real offenders back into my crime problem (and degrades my rehabilitation numbers the way §36 sold places already do).
- **US-6.** As a tester/QA, I need the four verification criteria from the Desc to be runnable as automated scenario tests — diverting police→education raises crime and eases school bottlenecks; importation revenue tracks spare capacity; high rubbish + crime drives `S → 1.0` halting growth; sustained `S ≥ 0.8` warns then game-overs — so the balance is evidence-driven, not anecdotal.

## Scope

Three coordinated changes: (1) the unified `S ∈ [0,1]` decay metric on `engine.spiral` — a weighted aggregate of the four causal families, data-sourced weights, published through the existing `SpiralMetric` subscription, feeding (2) growth dampening through the existing spiral→attract edge and (3) the existing WarningLedger→GhostCity death path (a second trigger on the same canonical `ErrGhostCityNoWarning` verdict, NOT a parallel death enum); plus (4) the police↔education budget trade-off wired through the two EXISTING funding surfaces (`crime.SetStrategyMix`, `education.SetStageFunding`); plus (5) an **extension of the built `engine.prison`** (MOD-056 — the package and its `PrisonAPI` capacity/occupancy/sold-places/reoffending surfaces already exist on origin/main) adding the spare-capacity-computation, the inmate-importation contract with finance-ledger posting, and wiring the existing riot/escape/recidivism leak back into `engine.crime`. It does **not** re-implement finance bookkeeping, the crime generation model, the stage machinery, the ghost-city verdict, or migration — it consumes and integrates them.

## Acceptance criteria

### Design 1 — The S-metric (engine.spiral, the keystone)

- **AC-1 (US-2; S exists, bounded, aggregated from the four causal families).** `engine.spiral` computes a unified decay score `S ∈ [0,1]` per tick as a weighted aggregate of exactly the four causal families from the Desc: (a) local crime rates (from `engine.crime` — the registered crime→spiral edge), (b) blight and uncollected refuse mass (from `engine.refuse` — **new edge, Escalation 1**, plus spiral's own decay state), (c) uncollected dead bodies / burnt structures left unrepaired (from `engine.wellbeing`/spiral's decay state), (d) financial credit distress / insolvency warning signals (from `engine.finance` — registered edge). The score is clamped to `[0,1]` and published through the existing `SpiralMetric` subscription (a new `S` field), so any consumer reacts to the same number. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/spiral SpiralMetric` shows the `S` field; a passing test drives each family high independently and asserts (i) `S` is monotone non-decreasing in that input, (ii) the other three at baseline contribute ~0, (iii) `S` never leaves `[0,1]` under adversarial extremes (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]ecayScore\|func Test.*[Ss]core.*[Bb]ound\|func Test.*[Ww]eight" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a score that is one input re-labelled (e.g. only crime drives S, refuse/wellbeing/finance are cosmetic) — assert each family's independent contribution; a score computed but never published (dead field) — assert the subscription value, not the internal field.

### Design 2 — Growth dampening (spiral→attract)

- **AC-2 (US-3; `S` damps growth and suppresses attraction through the existing edge, reversibly).** Urban growth slows when `S > 0.2` and citizen attraction is suppressed with self-reinforcing emigration onset when `S > 0.6` — wired through the **registered spiral→attract outbound edge** (`engine.spiral → engine.attract`), NOT a parallel growth clamp inside `engine.attract` and NOT a one-way scripted state machine. The thresholds are data-sourced (see AC-5); the shape is what this AC pins: dampening is monotone non-decreasing in `S`, and reversing `S` (fixing crime/refuse/finance) restores growth — recovery is possible, per §12. Check: a passing test drives `S` through the 0.2 and 0.6 bands and asserts growth/attraction responses are strictly more damped at the higher band; then drives `S` back down and asserts growth resumes toward the undamped baseline (the reversibility the existing stage tests already demand — see `engine.spiral.md` AC-5's precedent) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]amp\|func Test.*[Ss]piral.*[Aa]ttract\|func Test.*[Ee]migrationOnset" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go internal/engine/attract/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** a dampening that runs alongside the existing system (dead branch — growth still computes its old value) or a hard "halt at S>0.6" cliff instead of smooth suppression — assert the shaped response and its reversal, not a binary gate.

### Design 3 — The consistency death gate (WarningLedger → GhostCity)

- **AC-3 (US-4; `S ≥ 0.8` for a consecutive window writes the WarningLedger entry and drives the EXISTING death verdict, never a parallel one).** When `S ≥ 0.8` holds for the data-sourced consecutive-window (see AC-5; the Desc prose says "e.g. 24 months" but the live config `spiral.json` `ghostCity.warningThresholdMonths` is **12** — reconcile in Escalation 2), a qualifying entry is written to `engine.projections`' WarningLedger (the same ledger `death.go` already reads at lines 92-102), and the death path proceeds through the **canonical** `GhostCityTrigger`/`EvaluateDeath` → `ErrGhostCityNoWarning` (MET-G1102). There is exactly one ghost-city verdict; the S-gate is a second TRIGGER on the same verdict, never a second death type. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/spiral` shows no new death-error code beyond the canonical set (errors.go:40 family); a passing test drives `S ≥ 0.8` across the full window and asserts (i) the WarningLedger carries the qualifying MarginToGhostCity entry before death, (ii) the returned error code is `ErrGhostCityNoWarning`, (iii) driving `S` below the threshold mid-window resets the consecutive counter and NO death fires (the counter is not sticky) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Gg]hostCity.*[Ss]core\|func Test.*[Ss]ustained.*[Gg]ate\|func Test.*[Mm]arginToGhostCity" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go`). **False-pass risk:** an S-gate that bypasses the ledger (writes its own "you died" and short-circuits the verdict — the built-but-not-wired family) or a counter that survives recovery (death on a recovered city) — assert the ledger round-trip and the reset.

### Design 4 — The police↔education budget trade-off

- **AC-4 (US-1; diverting police budget to education raises crime while easing school bottlenecks — both directions asserted through the existing funding surfaces).** The player's allocation is exercised through the two EXISTING surfaces — `engine.crime.SetStrategyMix` (police/clearance/prevention weights) and `engine.education.SetStageFunding` (school funding) — no new parallel budget line. Reducing police allocation and raising education funding must (a) raise local crime generation (all else equal) while (b) easing the school bottleneck (education `StageQuality`/capacity strain falls). Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Tt]radeoff\|func Test.*[Pp]olice.*[Ee]ducation\|func Test.*[Dd]ivert" internal/engine/crime/*_test.go internal/engine/education/*_test.go`; a passing test runs the same district under three allocations (baseline / police-heavy / education-heavy) and asserts crime is monotone *inverse* in police share while the school bottleneck metric moves the other way. **False-pass risk:** a trade-off that only exists in prose — one side changes and the other doesn't (e.g. education spend rises but crime is unaffected) — assert BOTH directions from the same allocation delta.

### Design 5 — Prison spare capacity + inmate importation (extend the built engine.prison, MOD-056)

- **AC-5 (US-5/US-6; all magnitudes are data-file placeholders — the balance-regime spine of the whole item).** Every player-felt magnitude FEAT-142 introduces — the `S` thresholds (0.2 / 0.6 / 0.8), the consecutive-window months, the four `S` weights, the per-imported-inmate monthly payout, the staffing/funding occupancy-ratio that trips riots/escapes — lives in a data file (`spiral.json` for the S-metric; the prison package's own data file, or the `data/`-level precedent) with a `disclosure`/`$comment` block marking each a placeholder pending Aaron's balance pass (the `engine.crime` `crime.json` precedent: "PLACEHOLDER (balance-number regime, ASM-327)"). NO bare literal outside doc prose in Go source. Check: `grep -n "disclosure\|PLACEHOLDER\|\\\\$comment" internal/engine/spiral/spiral.json <prison data file>` matches for each magnitude; `grep -rnE "[0-9]+(\\.[0-9]+)?%|[0-9]+p" internal/engine/spiral/*.go internal/engine/prison/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) finds no bare rate/threshold literal; a passing test mutates each placeholder in the data file and asserts the engine follows (read-the-file, not shadowed) (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Mm]utate\|func Test.*[Rr]ate.*[Ff]ollows" internal/engine/spiral/*_test.go internal/engine/prison/*_test.go`). No numeric value is pinned by this file.
- **AC-6 (US-5; spare capacity is computed per institution and importation revenue tracks it, posted through engine.finance).** `engine.prison` (MOD-056, BUILT on origin/main — `SetCapacity`/`Capacity`/`SetSoldPlaces`/`SoldPlaces`/`DomesticPopulation`/`Admit`/`IntakeCount` at prison.go:200-252) models per-institution capacity and occupancy; the FEAT-142 extension adds the spare-capacity COMPUTATION surface + the importation contract. Spare capacity is `capacity − occupancy` (with §36 sold places already counted as occupied capacity, per the module's own existing overcrowding framing — `engine.prison.md`/MOD-056 AC-5). Toggling the "Import Out-of-Tile Inmates" contract earns a fixed monthly payout per imported inmate at the data-sourced rate, posted through `engine.finance`'s ledger under the prison revenue line (**new prison→finance edge, Escalation 1**) — the prison keeps no parallel money total. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]mport\|func Test.*[Ss]pareCapacity\|func Test.*[Pp]rison.*[Rr]evenue" internal/engine/prison/*_test.go`; a passing test drives two institutions with different spare capacities and asserts (i) importable capacity = the computed spare, (ii) monthly revenue = spare × per-inmate rate, (iii) the amount appears in `engine.finance`'s ledger (the amount is posted, not merely computed). **False-pass risk:** revenue that fills when the institution is at capacity (importing into occupied beds) or revenue computed but never posted (silent ledger zero) — assert the spare-capacity negative case and the ledger line.
- **AC-7 (US-5; overcrowding under low funding leaks offenders back into crime — the honest risk).** High occupancy under low staffing/funding triggers riots, escapes, and higher recidivism, and the escaped/reoffending offenders — real citizen `uint64` IDs — return to `engine.crime`'s generation pool via the **registered prison→crime edge** (already in code.json). Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]iot\|func Test.*[Ee]scape\|func Test.*[Rr]ecidivism.*[Ll]eak" internal/engine/prison/*_test.go internal/engine/crime/*_test.go`; a passing test drives occupancy above the data-sourced ratio at low funding and asserts (i) a leak event fires, (ii) the leaked IDs are the institution's offender cohort (traceable, not a count), (iii) they are re-eligible in crime generation the following month; the same scenario at low occupancy/full funding leaks nothing. **False-pass risk:** a leak that is a city-wide headcount (no per-ID trace) or that never reaches crime generation (fires but crime ignores it) — assert the ID path and the crime-side effect.
