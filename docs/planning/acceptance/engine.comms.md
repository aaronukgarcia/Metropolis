# Acceptance criteria — engine.comms (MOD-048)

**BOW code:** MOD-048
**Spec refs:** §35 Communications, Internet & E-commerce (`docs/METROPOLIS-MASTER-v2.1.md` lines 482-486: eras of connectivity — telephone exchange → dial-up → broadband hub → fibre backbone → cellular masts (2G→5G coverage overlay) → submarine cable landing station; internet quality gates office tiers, data centres, university research rate, and remote-work share ("a traffic tool disguised as telecoms" — good fibre lets a personality/sector-dependent slice work from home, cutting commute demand and boosting the dormitory strategy §21); post & parcels: letters decline by era (managed-decline story) vs parcels grow with wealth/era/e-commerce share; e-commerce share of retail demand shifts online with connectivity+wealth, requires a fulfilment centre (huge shed, thousands of jobs, big rates income, serious van+truck traffic) and last-mile depots (vans on the same real roads); tension: fulfilment convenience raises satisfaction/jobs while draining high-street retail → vacancy → §12 blight risk in the core; counterplay: entertainment zoning, markets, pedestrianisation); Comms & logistics catalogue supplement (line 522-523: sorting office, parcel hub, fulfilment centre, last-mile depot, cellular mast variants, fibre backbone node, submarine cable landing station, data centre); code.json `engine.comms` entry (inbound `CommsAPI`, pattern "era gates from unlock trees; e-commerce share a modelled coefficient", consumers: none registered; outbound calls: `engine.services`, `engine.logistics`, `engine.firms`, `engine.traffic`).
**Date:** 2026-08-11 (BA-S10a, first pass)
**Status:** draft-ahead — `MOD-025` (engine.logistics), `MOD-033` (engine.services), `MOD-058` (engine.firms) are all `open`; this item is not in the ready queue until all three land. Content written now, ahead of all three.

## User stories

- **US-1.** As the unlock-tree system, I need each connectivity era (exchange→dial-up→broadband→fibre→2G-5G masts→submarine cable) to gate a documented set of downstream capabilities (office tiers, data centres, research rate, remote-work share) so an era advance is a real capability unlock, not a cosmetic label change.
- **US-2.** As `engine.traffic` (a registered outbound edge), I need remote-work share to reduce commute-trip generation as connectivity quality rises, so "a traffic tool disguised as telecoms" (§35) is a real, measurable effect on the network, not description-only.
- **US-3.** As `engine.logistics` (a registered outbound edge, MOD-025), I need fulfilment-centre and last-mile-depot delivery traffic to be real logistics movements on real roads, so e-commerce's van/truck load is felt by the same junction-slot mechanism every other freight movement uses.
- **US-4.** As `engine.firms` (a registered outbound edge, MOD-058), I need a fulfilment centre to be a real firm (thousands of jobs, rates income) registered through `FirmsAPI`, not a comms-owned pseudo-employer.
- **US-5.** As `engine.services` (a registered outbound edge, MOD-033), I need the post-and-parcels sorting/delivery infrastructure (sorting office, parcel hub) to draw from the same generic service-funding→quality/staffing pool other public/quasi-public services use, so a funding cut degrades delivery reliability like any other service.
- **US-6.** As the high-street retail system, I need e-commerce's retail-share erosion to be a real, queryable coefficient that a downstream vacancy/blight consumer can subscribe to, so "drains high-street retail → vacancy → core blight risk" (§35) is a live number, not a described-but-inert flavour loop.
- **US-7.** As the player exercising counterplay (entertainment zoning, markets, pedestrianisation, §41's café culture), I need the high-street vitality countervailing force to be a real input the e-commerce drain model reads, so the "nothing is free" trade-off is genuinely two-sided rather than e-commerce being a one-way ratchet.

## Scope

The connectivity-era ladder and its capability gates (office tiers, data centres, university research rate, remote-work share); letters-vs-parcels volume trends by era; the e-commerce retail-share coefficient and its fulfilment-centre/last-mile-depot infrastructure requirement; the high-street-drain mechanic (queryable coefficient only — the vacancy/blight consumer side is out of scope, see Escalations). No unlock-tree mechanics themselves (`engine.unlocks`'s job, only consumed here as a gate source), no zoning/blight mechanics (a downstream consumer's job), no traffic assignment itself (`engine.traffic`'s job, only consumed here).

## Acceptance criteria

### Functional — connectivity eras and capability gates

- **AC-1 (GR#20; code.json inbound contract).** A `CommsAPI` exists exposing current-era queries, per-capability gate queries (office-tier ceiling, data-centre eligibility, research-rate modifier, remote-work-share coefficient), and e-commerce/post-parcel queries, matching code.json's documented pattern "era gates from unlock trees; e-commerce share a modelled coefficient". Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms CommsAPI` shows both an era-query surface and a per-capability gate surface.
- **AC-2 (§35 six-era ladder).** The six documented connectivity eras — telephone exchange, dial-up, broadband hub, fibre backbone, cellular masts (2G→5G sub-tiers), submarine cable landing station — are an ordered, monotonic progression (no era skip permitted by a single unlock command; each subsequent era strictly supersedes the prior one's gate values, never regresses a capability). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms Era` (or equivalent enum) lists all six in order; a passing test asserts advancing from broadband directly to submarine-cable (skipping fibre and cellular) is rejected or requires the intermediate eras to already be reached (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ee]raProgress\|func Test.*[Nn]oEraSkip" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-3 (§35 four named capability gates).** Each of the four documented gates — office-tier ceiling, data-centre eligibility, university research-rate modifier, remote-work-share coefficient — is an independently queryable value that changes as era advances, and at least two gates move independently of each other across at least one era transition (proving the four are not a single blended "internet quality" scalar duplicated four ways). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms` shows four named gate accessors; a passing test advances one era and asserts at least two of the four gate values change by DIFFERENT amounts (not a uniform proportional bump), demonstrating each gate has its own era-to-value mapping (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Cc]apabilityGate\|func Test.*[Gg]ateIndependence" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`). **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a single `InternetQuality float64` field with the four "gates" computed as `quality * fixedWeight` each would satisfy "four gates exist and rise with era" on a shallow read but move in lockstep, proportionally, across every era transition — the independence test catches this because a genuinely gate-specific data table (e.g. fibre unlocking data-centre eligibility as a step function while research-rate rises continuously) would NOT move all four proportionally.
- **AC-4 (§35 remote-work share — US-2, the traffic-tool clause).** Remote-work share is a documented function of connectivity-era gate value AND a personality/sector-dependent per-citizen or per-sector slice (§35: "a personality/sector-dependent slice work from home"), not a flat city-wide percentage applied uniformly regardless of era or sector. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms RemoteWorkShare` (or equivalent) takes an era/sector-aware input; a passing test asserts two sectors with different documented remote-work-affinity values produce different shares at the SAME era (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Rr]emoteWork" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`). **Blocked pending BUG-058 (see Escalations):** code.json has no `engine.traffic`↔`engine.comms` inbound edge (comms's own inbound `consumers` list is empty), so this AC's output cannot yet be legally consumed by `engine.traffic` to reduce commute-trip generation under GR#20 — this AC requires only that `RemoteWorkShare` be a real, independently queryable value on `CommsAPI` now; wiring it into traffic's trip generation is `engine.traffic`'s later obligation once the edge exists.

### Functional — letters, parcels, e-commerce

- **AC-5 (§35 letters decline, parcels grow).** Letter volume and parcel volume are two distinct, independently-trending queryable series: letter volume trends downward as era advances (the "managed-decline mini-story"), parcel volume trends upward as a function of wealth, era, and e-commerce share — the two series must be able to move in OPPOSITE directions within the same period (proving they are not one "post volume" number split by a fixed ratio). Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms` shows distinct `LetterVolume`/`ParcelVolume` accessors; a passing test constructs a scenario where e-commerce share rises while era/wealth are held fixed and asserts parcel volume rises while letter volume does not rise in step (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ll]etterDecline\|func Test.*[Pp]arcelGrowth" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-6 (§35 e-commerce share coefficient).** The e-commerce share of retail demand is a documented function of connectivity-era gate value AND wealth (§35: "share of retail demand shifts online as connectivity + wealth rise"), exposed as its own queryable coefficient, and the coefficient's growth REQUIRES a fulfilment centre and at least one last-mile depot to be operational — a share above a documented floor with no fulfilment centre registered is rejected/capped, not silently permitted. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms ECommerceShare` shows the coefficient accessor and a fulfilment-centre-registered precondition; a passing test asserts the coefficient cannot exceed its no-infrastructure floor while no fulfilment centre is registered, and can exceed it once one is (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ee]CommerceShare\|func Test.*[Ff]ulfilmentRequired" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-7 (§35 fulfilment centre as a real firm — US-4).** A fulfilment centre is registered as a real firm through `engine.firms`'s registered outbound edge (thousands of jobs at modest wages, real rates income), not a comms-owned pseudo-employer with an internal jobs counter. Check: `grep -rn "firms\.RegisterFirm" internal/engine/comms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows a real call into `engine.firms`; a passing test asserts a registered fulfilment centre is queryable back through `FirmsAPI`'s `Firm(id)` (RegisterFirm returns a `freight.Firm` snapshot), with its jobs carried as `InputRequired` at the documented "thousands" scale for its tier — a fresh firm carries an `InputRequired` roster, not a populated one (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ff]ulfilmentFirm" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-8 (§35 delivery traffic — US-3).** Fulfilment-centre and last-mile-depot delivery trips are real `engine.logistics` movements, not a comms-owned parallel van-routing model. Per FEAT-083 the `engine.logistics` stub has **no junction-slot `Movement` scheduler yet** (deferred), so e-commerce deliveries reuse `LogisticsAPI`'s existing stock-draw surface: a delivery whose demand cannot be satisfied in-tick maps to `Draw`'s stock-draw shortfall, which is the current logistics surface's analogue of `engine.logistics.md` AC-4's "saturated junction → next-day queue". Check: `grep -rn "logistics\.\(Movement\|Draw\|Stock\)" internal/engine/comms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) shows delivery movements constructed through `engine.logistics`'s exported surface; a passing test asserts a delivery against a depleted junction/stock produces the same shortfall/queue behaviour `engine.logistics.md` AC-4 defines (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]eliveryMovement\|func Test.*[Ll]astMile" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`).
- **AC-9 (§35 high-street drain coefficient — the counterplay clause; US-6/US-7).** A queryable "high-street drain pressure" figure is exposed as a documented function of e-commerce share, and independently, a queryable "counterplay offset" input exists (documented as sourced from §41 café-culture vitality / entertainment-zoning density / pedestrianisation, per §35's explicit counterplay list) that reduces the NET drain pressure without ever fully cancelling e-commerce share's raw pull — i.e. the offset dampens but does not zero the drain, so "nothing is free" remains true even at maximum counterplay. Check: `go doc ./internal/engine/comms` shows both a raw drain accessor and a net (offset-applied) accessor, with the two provably distinct; a passing test asserts raising the counterplay offset input lowers net drain pressure but never to zero while e-commerce share is nonzero (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Hh]ighStreetDrain\|func Test.*[Cc]ounterplay" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`). **Blocked pending BUG-058 (see Escalations):** the actual consumer of this drain pressure (whichever module owns high-street vacancy/blight state) has no registered edge to `engine.comms` — this AC requires only that the coefficient and its counterplay-offset relationship be real and queryable now.

### Error handling

- **AC-10 (GR#7).** Querying a capability gate for an era value outside the documented six-era enum, or registering a fulfilment centre without a valid firm reference, returns a registry-sourced error (new `MET-G`-range code — `engine.comms` claims `G3300`–`G3399` at build time against `data/errors.json`'s reserved table; the `MET-E` layer is exhausted and `BUG-234`'s three-to-four-digit widening mandates the G overflow block) rather than a silently-created zero-value era state. Check: `grep -n "MET-" internal/engine/comms/*.go` finds a registry code reference; passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Ii]nvalidEra\|func Test.*[Nn]oFirmRef" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`) — **GR#7 assertion, stated explicitly (BUG-100):** the test asserts the returned error's registry code matches AND that no zero-value era state or fulfilment-centre record was created, not merely that a matching-named test function exists.
- **AC-11.** Out-of-domain inputs are rejected with a typed error, never silently clamped or silently ignored: a negative letter/parcel volume, a wealth input outside `[0,1]` (the wealth boundary is what drives e-commerce share — the share itself is a modelled coefficient with no raw setter, per AC-6), and an era value outside the six-era enum. Check: passing test coverage (`grep -rn "func Test.*[Oo]utOfRange" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`).

### Determinism, safety & scale

- **AC-12 (GR#21).** Era-gate resolution, e-commerce share computation, and remote-work-share computation are deterministic functions of `(worldSeed, tick, prior state, commands)` — repeated runs from identical starting state and command sequence produce byte-identical era state, gate values, and coefficients, across worker counts. Check: `grep -rn "func Test.*[Dd]etermnis\|func Test.*[Dd]eterminis" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go`.
- **AC-13 (SG-7; GR#21).** `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since" internal/engine/comms/*.go` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches.
- **AC-14.** `go test ./internal/engine/comms/... -race -count=1` passes; `grep -n "go func()" internal/engine/comms/*_test.go` finds at least one concurrency test.

### Documentation

- **AC-15.** `internal/engine/comms/doc.go` states the module key `engine.comms`, cites §35, states the four-capability-gate independence contract (AC-3) in prose, states the fulfilment-centre-as-firm contract (AC-7), and states the two outstanding BUG-058 edges (traffic→comms for remote-work consumption, and the high-street-drain consumer edge) explicitly so a future reader does not assume either wiring already happened. Check: `grep -n "engine.comms" internal/engine/comms/doc.go` and `grep -n "§35\|section 35" internal/engine/comms/doc.go` both match; `grep -n "BUG-058" internal/engine/comms/doc.go` matches.

## Out of scope

- The unlock-tree mechanics that actually advance eras (`engine.unlocks`, out of scope — this item only reads the current era, per AC-2).
- `engine.logistics`'s own movement-scheduling/queueing mechanics — this item only drives that machinery for delivery-typed movements (AC-8).
- `engine.firms`'s own firm lifecycle mechanics — this item only registers the fulfilment centre as a firm (AC-7).
- Whichever module ultimately owns high-street vacancy/blight state and consumes AC-9's drain coefficient — out of scope until BUG-058's consumer-side edge lands.
- §41's café-culture vitality computation itself — this item only consumes a documented counterplay-offset INPUT (AC-9); the vitality index math belongs to `engine.cafe`.
- The specific per-era gate-value magnitudes, e-commerce share growth curve, and drain-pressure/counterplay-offset weighting — balance data pending M2 tuning (see Escalations).
