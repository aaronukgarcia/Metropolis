## 1. User stories

- **US-1.** As the **Firms** layer (§45/§46), I need every wage I pay to be an `int64` micro-pound (`finance.Money`) figure keyed by the worker's education/skill bracket, so the wage-extraction loop is exact, overflow-safe money — not a float or an un-bracketed lump.
- **US-2.** As a **Citizen**, I need my remaining post-tax wage to leave my hands through real, physical channels — food in tonnes (§17/§37), utility bills (§17), leisure tickets (§42) — so the "consumption drain" is a conserved flow, not an abstract satisfaction percentage.
- **US-3.** As the **Municipal Ledger**, I need the whole circuit — wage extraction, every tax, consumption spend, policy cost — to balance `in == out ± delta` to the micro-pound, so every pound is accounted for and `engine.invariant`'s conservation check holds end-to-end (§7/§54).
- **US-4.** As the **Player**, I need finite monthly revenue to be a single pool I split between short-term critical needs (police, refuse, fire) and long-run investment (schools, research, FDI), so the choice is zero-sum and felt (§54 Public Service Pie; north-star Q1/Q2).
- **US-5.** As the **Player** who underfunds police/garbage/fire, I need a single, bounded, monotonic Death Spiral score `S ∈ [0,1]` that rises from real service failures (crime spikes, rubbish, uncollected bodies, burnt buildings), so I can *see* the spiral coming rather than being ambushed (§12; north-star Q3).
- **US-6.** As the **Player** who invests in schools over police, I need the long-fuse payoff to be real and legible — years of ticks before graduates move into higher skill brackets that unlock high-wage pharma/R&D FDI — so the deferred bet is a judgement call, not a gamble (§27/§45/§46; north-star Q5).
- **US-7.** As the **Player** pulling a policy lever (green-energy subsidy, business-tax incentive, threat-dial funding), I need every lever to be structural with a real physical trade-off, so nothing is a "magical modifier" that improves one number while touching nothing else (§52).
- **US-8.** As the **Physical World** (§33/§25), I need products and refuse tracked in physical tonnes with mass conservation, so "food in → waste out" and "import → warehouse → supermarket" are conserved, not abstracted away.

## 2. Acceptance criteria

### 2.1 Conservation of money & overflow safety (GR#3, GR#16, GR#7)

- **AC-1 (GR#3; whole-circuit conservation — EXTENDS `engine.finance` `TestMoneyConservationOver120Months` (conservation_test.go) and `engine.fiscal` AC-9).** Every monetary figure the circuit produces — wages extracted, taxes collected, consumption spend, policy cost, municipal revenue — is a recomputation or a ledger posting through `engine.finance`'s `FinanceAPI`; the circuit owns **no second, independently-summed money total**. The whole-circuit accounting satisfies `in == out ± delta`, where `delta` is the ledger's own documented external inflow/outflow (reserve interest, imports, opex paid away), not a hidden balancing slack. **Check:** a headless test wires `engine.finance` to the circuit path (extraction + one tax + consumption + one policy), runs ≥ N months, and asserts `engine.finance`'s conservation invariant reports zero violations over the run — reusing the existing `TestMoneyConservationOver120Months` shape, not a new parallel invariant. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a circuit that keeps its own running "treasury" accumulator and reports a balanced figure by construction (without ever posting through the real ledger) would satisfy a naive "totals add up" check while leaving `engine.finance`'s invariant unaware of the flows — the check requires the *ledger* to balance, not a local sum to be self-consistent.

- **AC-2 (GR#16; overflow-safe arithmetic — NEW; grounds the SEC-094/SEC-098/SEC-099/SEC-130 weakness class).** Every multiplication, addition, or subtraction of `finance.Money` in the circuit path (wage × rate, tax × base, consumption × price, policy cost × coefficient) goes through `foundation/num`'s `SafeMul`/`SatAdd`/`SatSub`/`SatAddChecked` (or a thin `finance.Money`-typed adapter over them, per `engine.finance`'s `satAddMoney`/`moneyTimesRate` precedent) — **never a bare `+`, `-`, or `*` on a `Money` operand**, and never a `float32`/`float64` monetary field. **Check:** `grep -rn "float32\|float64"` over the circuit's money-bearing types finds no monetary field (rate *percentages* and *ratios* are legitimately fractional); `grep -n "num.SafeMul\|num.SatAdd\|num.SatSub\|num.SatAddChecked\|satAddMoney\|moneyTimesRate"` finds the saturation call sites. **Lazy implementation this rejects (and why this is load-bearing):** SEC-094 proved `engine.services`' `NetFiscalCost` lost the tax clawback to int64 overflow and silently returned *net ≈ gross* at 100% tax; SEC-099 proved a `moneyFromFloat` that bypassed `num.ClampInt64FromFloat` turned `NaN` into `MinInt64` negative money. A check that only asks "is money an int64?" passes both broken builds — this check requires the *arithmetic* to be overflow-checked, not merely the storage type to be wide.

- **AC-3 (GR#7 + GR#16; overflow is loud, never wrapped — NEW).** When any monetary aggregation in the circuit saturates (the `SatAddChecked`/`SafeMul` `overflow` bool is true), the caller returns a **registry-sourced error** (new `MET-E`/`MET-G`-range code) naming the operation and the operands — it never silently keeps the wrapped `int64` value. **Check:** a test drives a synthetic sum/product past `math.MaxInt64` (e.g. two near-`MaxInt64` wages summed, or a base × a huge rate) and asserts the returned error's registry code matches and the result is discarded, not stored. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build that calls `num.SatAdd` (which clamps to `MaxInt64`) and then stores the clamped value *without checking the bool* would "not wrap" but would silently write a wrong `MaxInt64` figure into a conserved total — this check requires the `overflow` signal to surface as an error, mirroring `engine.fiscal`'s `ErrFiscalOverflow` in `moneyTimesRate`.

- **AC-4 (GR#17; fail-closed on unwired dependencies — EXTENDS `engine.fiscal` AC-10 / `requireFinance`/`requireTax`).** Any circuit operation that reads a downstream module (finance/tax/policies/services/spiral) returns a registry-sourced `ErrDependencyMissing` naming the operation and the dependency when that dependency is not wired — never a fabricated zero/non-zero figure standing in for unbuilt or unwired data. **Check:** a test calls the circuit operation before wiring the dependency and asserts a registry error (code + dependency name), not a silent default.

### 2.2 Pillar 1 — The Wealth Extraction Circuit (tax & consumption drain)

- **AC-5 (NEW; §7/§45/§46 — wage extraction loop).** Firms pay wages as `finance.Money` (int64 micro-pounds), keyed by the worker's **education/skill bracket**, with the bracket taxonomy **loaded from data** (a data file), not a hardcoded Go `switch`/enum. A wage figure is always a `finance.Money`, never a float. **Check:** `grep -n "float32\|float64"` over the wage path finds no monetary field; the bracket taxonomy is read from data (a `data/*.json` file carries the brackets with a `$comment`/`meta` block citing §7/§45/§46 and the ASM numbers); a test loads two different bracket definitions and asserts a worker's wage changes with the bracket. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a `switch skill { case "graduate": wage = 50.0 ... }` in Go with a float wage would pass "wages differ by bracket" but violate GR#15 (hardcoded) and GR#16 (float money) — the check requires data-loaded brackets and `finance.Money` arithmetic.

- **AC-6 (REUSES `engine.tax` AC-3/AC-4/AC-8 — income tax on wages, no re-taxing).** The income tax on wages is computed **by `engine.tax`'s income-tax instrument** (the registered `engine.tax → engine.finance` edge) and posted through `engine.finance`; the circuit **does not** compute a second income-tax figure. Net-of-tax wage = gross wage − the `TaxAPI`-computed clawback. **Check:** `grep -n "tax\."` over the circuit's wage path shows a call into `TaxAPI` (or `engine.fiscal`'s already-registered `engine.fiscal → engine.tax` edge), and no local `wage * incomeTaxRate` arithmetic survives; a test asserts the posted income-tax amount equals `TaxAPI.Revenue("income")`-derived value for a synthetic wage, not a parallel formula.

- **AC-7 (REUSES `engine.tax` AC-6/AC-7 — sales/business/property tax consumed, not re-taxed).** Sales/VAT on shop/supermarket transactions and property/council tax on households are computed **by `engine.tax`** (sales via the sales-tax instrument; property via `BusinessRateRevenue`'s zone × `finance.LandPrice` shape, or the residential instrument), and consumed by the circuit as a source node in the Sankey. The circuit re-taxes nothing. **Check:** a test wires `TaxAPI` with a known base and asserts the circuit's "tax extracted" figure equals what `TaxAPI` reports for the same state; `grep` finds no second sales/property-tax computation in the circuit package.

- **AC-8 (NEW; §12/§25/§28 + `engine.tax` AC-7 — land-value degradation feeds the council/property tax base).** The property/council-tax base scales **dynamically** with district land value, and that land value is **degraded by blight (§12 decay), waste/refuse backlog (§25), and crime (§28)** — the degradation is a derived input from real subsystem signals (`engine.spiral`'s decay drag, `engine.refuse`'s overflow, `engine.crime`'s district pressure), never a hardcoded scalar penalty. **Check:** a monotonicity test holds land-value inputs equal and varies only the blight/refuse/crime signal; it asserts the resulting council-tax base is **strictly lower** under worse blight/refuse/crime than under clean conditions, and that the ordering is monotonic (more blight ⇒ no higher base). **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build that applies a fixed "bad district = −10%" scalar would pass a check that only asks "does blight affect tax?" — this check requires the base to move *in proportion to the live signal*, so a constant penalty cannot pass.

- **AC-9 (NEW; §17/§37/§42 — consumption drain as municipal revenue, distinct from tax).** Citizens spend their remaining post-tax wage through **physical channels**: food in tonnes (§17.1 staples/fresh → §37 grocery), utility bills (`engine.consumption`'s `BilledAmount` per `engine.consumption` AC-20), and leisure tickets (§42 venues). When the player **runs** the venue/grid (owns/operates it), that spend posts to the treasury as **municipal/user-charge revenue through `engine.finance`** — a category distinct from tax collection. **Check:** an end-to-end test wires a citizen's post-tax wage → a player-run utility/grid/venue, and asserts (a) the spend amount is conserved (wage → food+utilities+leisure+residual, no money created/destroyed) and (b) the player-run portion appears as a ledger credit in a revenue category, **not** the tax category. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build that adds a flat "consumption tax revenue" number without tracing the actual spend (or that double-counts consumption both as citizen spend and as tax) fails the conservation assertion (a).

### 2.3 Pillar 2 — Physical Products & Services Tracking (mass conservation)

- **AC-10 (NEW; §33/§8 — tonnes accounting, not percentages).** Food, ore, and refined goods flow import → warehouse → supermarket as **physical tonnes/month**, with the movement accounted as `imported == stored_delta + sold + lost + exported` (mass-conserved; "lost" only where a documented shelf-life/spoilage rule exists). The circuit-level fiscal view of these flows is **tonnes, not satisfaction points**. **Check:** a conservation test runs a synthetic chain (import X t, warehouse stores Y t, supermarket sells Z t) and asserts the tonne identity holds exactly across a month boundary, and that a spoilage rule (if any) is the only sink besides sale/export. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a "product flow" that only tracks an abstract satisfaction 0–100 and never reconciles tonnes would fail the identity check outright.

- **AC-11 (NEW; §25 + `engine.consumption` AC-5 — mass-conserved refuse).** Household food-tonne consumption maps **directly** to the refuse tonnage the player pays to collect/compost, on a **data-defined collection cadence** (the brief's "bi-weekly" is the default placeholder, not a hardcoded literal). The mapping conserves mass: `food_consumed_tonnes → (recycled + composted + landfilled + residual_bin_overflow)`, with the cadence deciding the per-collection batch. **Check:** a test feeds a known household food-tonnage for a month and asserts the refuse tonnage equals the mapped value (per the data-defined per-capita waste coefficient), and that bin overflow (missed collection) appears as a distinct, conserved residual rather than vanishing. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build that computes "waste = some percentage of food, rounded to look plausible" without a conserved identity would fail the exact-equality assertion across a multi-month run where overflow accumulates.

- **AC-12 (NEW; §17/§26/§28 — coverage is per-cell physical service-draw, not area-of-effect).** Service coverage for water, power, police response latency, and healthcare capacity is expressed as **physical per-cell service-draws** — a cell is covered iff it is on the real network reach (utility network / response path / capacity catchment), never inside an area-of-effect circle. A cell **outside** the network/response path reports **uncovered**, even if it sits geometrically adjacent to a covered cell. **Check:** a test constructs a covered cell and a geometrically-adjacent but network-disconnected cell, and asserts the disconnected cell's coverage reads uncovered (zero draw) while the connected cell's reads covered — a radius-only implementation cannot pass because it would cover both. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** an AoE-circle "coverage radius" implementation is the exact thing this check is written to fail, per the brief's explicit "not area-of-effect circles" requirement.

### 2.4 Pillar 3 — The Spend-Allocation Tension (crisis vs investment)

- **AC-13 (NEW; §54 Public Service Pie + `engine.services` — zero-sum allocation over one revenue pool).** The player allocates **finite monthly revenue** across short-term critical needs (police, garbage/refuse, fire) and long-run investment (schools/education, research grants, FDI incentives) **from the same pool**, and the allocation is **zero-sum**: `Σ allocations ≤ available_revenue`, enforced by `engine.finance` (the same ledger that owns the budget). Per-service staffing/pie sliders stay in `engine.services` (MOD-033); this circuit-level tension is a composition over `engine.services` + `engine.education` + `engine.finance`, not a reimplementation of the per-service sliders. **Check:** a test sets available revenue R, allocates critical + investment summing to > R, and asserts the over-allocation is rejected (registry error) or the budget balance goes negative in a way `engine.finance` surfaces — never a silent "allocated anyway" state. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build that lets the two allocation panes draw from separate, independently-unbounded pools (so the player can fund everything at once) defeats the "pie is only so big" thesis — the check requires the single-pool, zero-sum constraint to be enforceable and enforced.

- **AC-14 (NEW; §12 — the Death Spiral score `S`, monotonic and bounded).** `S` is a single scalar in **[0,1]**, **monotonic** in the sense that *reducing* critical-service funding never *lowers* `S` and *increasing* funding never *raises* it (same starting state, all else equal), and it is **derived from real underfunding signals** — crime spikes (§28), refuse/overflow backlog (§25), uncollected bodies (deathcare backlog), burnt buildings (fire losses, §26) — not a magic counter that increments on a timer. `S` is a **leading indicator layered on** `engine.spiral`'s `DecayAPI` metrics, not a replacement for them. **Check:** (a) a bounds test asserts `0 ≤ S ≤ 1` across a sweep of funding levels; (b) a monotonicity test holds the city state, runs one tick at low police/refuse/fire funding and one at high funding, and asserts `S_low ≥ S_high` with strict inequality for at least the worst-underfunded case; (c) a derivation test asserts `S` responds to a change in a *real* signal (e.g. injecting a refuse backlog) — so a constant `S` fails. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build where `S` is a scripted countdown or a static number (or one that reads real signals but never moves them) fails (b)/(c) — this is the same "no scripted loss" discipline `engine.spiral` AC-2 encodes, applied to the score.

- **AC-15 (NEW; GR#7 — the `S ≥ 0.8` game-over threshold, with a non-colliding error name).** When `S ≥ 0.8` for a **data-defined number of consecutive monthly cycles** (the brief leaves the count open; precedent: `engine.finance`'s `insolvencyMonthsForGameOver = 3` consecutive months), the game-over fires with a **registry-sourced error**. **The error must NOT reuse the "GhostCity" name**: `engine.spiral` already owns the population-collapse death path (`DeathGhostCity`) and `ErrGhostCityNoWarning` (MET-G1102) for §12's *different* condition. This new path uses a distinct code (recommended `ErrServiceCollapse` / `ErrDeathSpiral`, `MET-G`-range) and is a **third death condition** alongside insolvency (§7) and population ghost-city (§12) — it is added to `engine.spiral`'s `EvaluateDeath` verdict set, not smuggled into the existing ghost-city gate. **Check:** (a) a threshold test drives `S` to exactly 0.79 and asserts no game-over, then 0.80 and asserts the trigger arms; (b) a consecutive-cycles test asserts a single-cycle excursion above 0.8 does **not** fire, and that the counter resets when `S` drops back below the threshold (mirroring `engine.finance`'s insolvency counter reset); (c) a name test asserts the returned code is distinct from `ErrGhostCityNoWarning`/`DeathGhostCity`. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** firing on the first cycle at 0.8 (ignoring "consecutive") or reusing the ghost-city code (so the two death conditions are indistinguishable) both fail (b)/(c).

- **AC-16 (NEW; §7/§II.7 — the self-sufficiency runway; EXTENDS `engine.finance` reserve-months).** The treasury exposes a **self-sufficiency runway** (months of runway at the current burn rate), reusing `engine.finance`'s existing reserve-months machinery (`reserves ÷ monthly revenue`, `credit.go`) rather than a second parallel counter. The **18-month** figure the brief names is a **data-defined placeholder threshold** (pending Aaron), below which a warning is raised (not a game-over). **Check:** (a) a query test asserts the runway figure equals `reserves ÷ burn` exactly for a synthetic state; (b) a warning test asserts the warning fires when the computed runway drops below the data-loaded threshold, and clears when it recovers above it. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a second "runway counter" maintained by the circuit independently of `engine.finance`'s reserves would drift from the ledger (GR#3) and fail (a)'s exact-equality assertion against the real reserve balance.

- **AC-17 (NEW; §27/§45/§46 — the long-fuse education → skill → FDI compounding).** The circuit exposes the **long-fuse chain** as a real, verifiable data-flow, not an instant unlock: schools/education funding graduates citizens into **higher skill brackets over years of ticks** (§27's stage pipeline), those brackets feed the workforce skill supply (`engine.census`'s skill axis, §13 F6), and high-skill supply is what **unlocks high-value pharma/R&D FDI anchors** (§46) paying high wages that compound into the tax base (§45). **Check:** a multi-year headless test asserts that (a) at month 0 a freshly-funded school produces **zero** graduates in a high skill bracket (no instant graduation), (b) after the data-defined stage duration, graduates appear in the higher bracket, and (c) an FDI pharma/R&D prospect is **not** unlockable before the skill supply reaches its data-defined threshold, and *is* unlockable after. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** an implementation that grants the FDI anchor as soon as the school building exists (or graduates citizens instantly) fails (a)/(c) — the point is that the payoff is *deferred and gated on real skill supply*, per §27's "longest fuse in the game".

### 2.5 Pillar 4 — Policy Sliders as Modeled Instruments (no magical modifiers)

- **AC-18 (NEW; §52/§49 — green-energy subsidy is a real three-way trade-off; EXTENDS `engine.policies` AC-2/AC-3).** A green-energy subsidy lever **simultaneously** (a) reduces blight and the Death Spiral score `S`, (b) **drains the treasury**, and (c) **raises grid OPEX** — all three effects are queryable and each is a **structural coefficient move** (mechanism = data-defined coefficient deltas per `engine.policies`), not a bare "+X% wellbeing" modifier. **Check:** a single-enactment test enacts the subsidy against a snapshot and asserts (i) a blight/`S`-relevant coefficient moved down, (ii) a treasury debit posted through `engine.finance`, and (iii) an OPEX coefficient moved up — all three in the *same* enactment, with no effect free. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a subsidy that only lowers blight (a pure buff) or only costs money (no mechanical benefit) fails the three-simultaneous-effect assertion — this is the "no free lunch" check.

- **AC-19 (NEW; §39/§46/§52 — business tax incentive is a real two-way trade-off; EXTENDS `engine.policies` + `engine.tax`).** A business-tax incentive lever **both** (a) attracts firms and jobs (firm-count/jobs coefficient up) **and** (b) **starves immediate revenue** (a tax-rate or tax-base coefficient down, so collected revenue falls). Both effects are queryable and structural. **Check:** a single-enactment test asserts the firm-attraction signal rose **and** the short-run tax-revenue figure fell, in the same enactment. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** an incentive that attracts firms with no revenue cost (or costs revenue with no attraction) fails the two-simultaneous-effect assertion.

- **AC-20 (NEW; §52/§55/§28 — threat-dial funding has zero local economic yield).** A threat-dial/security funding lever **prevents catastrophic safety failures** (a security/terror/fire-safety event suppressed or its probability reduced) while contributing **zero local economic yield** — it does not raise GDP, wages, or the tax base. **Check:** a test enacts threat-dial funding and asserts (a) the modelled safety-failure probability/severity fell, and (b) the economic-yield signals (wage bill, GDP, tax base) are **unchanged** by the funding. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** a build where "security funding" quietly also boosts a productivity coefficient (so it is secretly good for the economy) fails (b) — the lever must be honest about being pure insurance.

### 2.6 Cross-cutting (GR#21 determinism, race safety, no-magic sweep, documentation)

- **AC-21 (GR#21; EXTENDS the determinism ACs in `engine.fiscal`/`engine.tax`/`engine.policies`).** Every cross-district/cross-shard/cross-instrument monetary or tonne aggregation in the circuit iterates in a **fixed, sorted order** — never unordered Go map iteration — and no circuit computation reads `time.Now`/`time.Since`. **Check:** `grep -n "for .* := range"` on any map feeding a monetary/tonne sum is sorted (`grep -n "sort\."`); `grep -rn "time.Now\|time.Since"` (excluding `_test.go`) returns no matches; a determinism test asserts byte-identical aggregate output across repeated runs.

- **AC-22 (race safety; EXTENDS the `-race` ACs).** `go test ./internal/engine/... -race -count=1` passes with no data race when the circuit's allocation/score/tax queries run concurrently with `engine.finance`/`engine.tax` ledger updates. **Check:** a concurrency test (`grep -n "go func()"`) exercises the concurrent path.

- **AC-23 (NEW — the no-magic-modifier sweep).** A sweep asserts that **no player lever anywhere in the circuit** (tax, policy, subsidy, funding) is implemented as a bare scalar that improves one headline number while touching no underlying mechanism. Every lever's effect is a **coefficient move or a real flow** (a ledger posting, a tonne move, a network draw), traceable to the module that owns that coefficient. **Check:** `grep -rn "Satisfaction\s*+=\|Wellbeing\s*+=\|Attractiveness\s*+="` (and analogous bare-increment patterns) over the circuit's lever code finds no direct headline-stat mutation that is not the *result* of a coefficient/flow change. **Lazy implementation this rejects:** the "magical modifier" the brief's pillar 4 explicitly forbids — a lever that writes `attractiveness += 10` directly would fail this sweep.

- **AC-24 (GR#6; documentation).** Each owning module's `doc.go` (or the circuit's design note) states the module key, cites its spec §, states that conservation/`finance.Money` ownership stays in `engine.finance`/`engine.invariant` (GR#3), and — for the `S`-score and runway — states the placeholder nature of every threshold (GR#15). **Check:** `grep -n` for the module key, the spec §, and "placeholder"/"GR#15" in the owning `doc.go`.

## 3. North-star vetting (`docs/planning/design-north-star.md`, five-question test)

This circuit is **the** load-bearing entertainment system of the game — it answers **four of the five** questions, and the fifth is answered by the warning machinery it feeds.

1. **Which conflicting demand does this sharpen?** *Crisis-suppression now vs investment later, on one finite monthly revenue pool.* The whole circuit exists to make "the pie is only so big" felt: every pound into police is a pound not into schools, and the two demand different time horizons. This is north-star idea #1 made mechanical.
2. **What does the player give up?** *Choosing police over schools buys down `S` today and gives up the snowball* — the education → skill → FDI compounding that pays off years later. The player who always funds the crisis ends up safe but poor; the player who always funds the future gambles near-term survival on `S` staying below 0.8.
3. **Does the consequence stick?** Yes — `S`, the runway, and burnt-down blocks are persistent and compounding (§25's "waste cuts are invisible for months, then vermin, then illness, then emigration"); there is no scripted rescue.
4. **Does it snowball?** Yes — education → skill brackets → high-wage FDI → tax base → more investment (north-star idea #3, the 2,000-resident university that hums at 200,000).
5. **Can a long bet be made on it?** Yes — the school funded early, before the city can comfortably afford it, is the §27/§46 long fuse (north-star idea #5).

**The tax lever specifically sharpens this conflict:** raising tax to fund services is *extraction from the citizen now*, but the same citizens' post-tax spend is itself municipal revenue via venues and grids — so the tax lever is a genuine two-directional dial (extract now vs let the citizen spend and grow the revenue base), sharpened further by `engine.tax`'s Laffer elasticity (push the rate too far and the base shrinks). This is exactly the "both directions on every dial" pillar (§1.3, Pillar 6).

## 5. Escalations

### 5.1 Spec-vs-brief conflicts (must be ruled on by the lead/Aaron before dispatch)

- **The Death Spiral score `S` and its game-over are not in §12.** §12 defines *two* death conditions (insolvency §7, population < 10% of historic peak) and describes the spiral as an **event chain**, not a 0–1 score. The brief's pillar 3 introduces a *third* abstraction (`S`, a bounded score driven by underfunded critical services) and a *third* death path (`S ≥ 0.8 → game-over`). AC-14/AC-15 encode it as a new leading indicator + third death condition that composes with (does not replace) `engine.spiral`. **Ruling needed:** is `S` a new third death condition, or a reframing of §12's spiral that should replace one of the existing two?
- **The `ErrGhostCity` name collides with §12's ghost-city ending and `engine.spiral`'s `DeathGhostCity` + `ErrGhostCityNoWarning` (MET-G1102).** Reusing "GhostCity" for a service-collapse death path makes two distinct death conditions share a name. AC-15 mandates a distinct code (recommended `ErrServiceCollapse`/`ErrDeathSpiral`).
- **"18-month runway" is not a spec number.** §7 names "reserve months" as a credit-rating input; nothing sets 18. AC-16 treats it as a data placeholder and reuses `engine.finance`'s reserve-months machinery.
