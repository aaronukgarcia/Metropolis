> **Five findings the brief did not anticipate. They matter more than anything else in this file.**
> 1. **The +12 GB monthly transient is already gone from the tick path.** `coldParamsLocked` streams one shard at a time (`internal/engine/citizens/registry.go:1937-1950`, `sampling.go:96-130`). `allColdRecordsLocked` survives only behind `BuildSample` (`registry.go:1130-1137`), which has no production caller.
> 2. **An open browser tab keeps the cloud container awake forever.** `IdleEvictTimeout` (5 min) only fires with zero connections (`cmd/metroserve/cityhost.go:75`), and Container Apps counts an open WebSocket as load. One forgotten tab on a 2 vCPU / 4 GiB replica is about £125/month. That is **6× the £20 cap**, from a single tab.
> 3. **Shutdown does not snapshot.** On SIGTERM, `CityHost.Close` → `stop()` cancels, joins and closes, with no final snapshot or explicit journal flush (`cityhost.go:~115-132, 633-661`). Every wake replays up to 360 day-ticks. At the measured 10M tick cost (624 ms/day-tick) that is **~3.7 minutes**, not 15 seconds.
> 4. **Two tabs on one hosted city race today.** `wsserver` supports one pump per `Transport` (doc `internal/protocol/wsserver/server.go:56-67`). But `CityHost.Acquire` is a refcount that admits a second connection to the same city (`cmd/metroserve/main.go:266-275`). Nothing refuses it. This is a live defect, not a future multiplayer concern.
> 5. **The parallel shard pool exists but runs with one worker in production.** `runShardsParallel` (`internal/engine/citizens/coldpass.go:95-121`) is fed by `c.workers`. That is initialised to 1 (`registry.go:235`), and only tests set it. The determinism gate already proves 1-worker and 14-worker runs give identical hashes (`internal/engine/detgate/gate_test.go:33-38`).

# Part 1 (Round 2): the 100M engine, the memory spike and the data tier

## 1.1 The `allColdRecordsLocked` transient and the real memory profile

### On disk today

- **Definition.** `allColdRecordsLocked` is at `internal/engine/citizens/registry.go:1728-1736`. It appends `s.recordAt(i)` for every row of every shard into one unsized `[]ColdRecord`.
- **Its one production caller** is `BuildSample` (`registry.go:1130-1137`). `BuildSample` is called only by `ColdParams` (`:1141-1146`). A grep of non-test Go found no caller of either. Test callers: `rowof_scale_gate_test.go:41` and `attack_bug663_round_test.go:573`.
- **The monthly tick path no longer uses it.**
  - `AdvanceDayTick` calls `coldParamsLocked` when `dayTick == 0` (`registry.go:1422-1424`).
  - `coldParamsLocked` streams through `stratifiedSampleBuilder` one shard at a time, reusing one per-shard `[]ColdRecord` scratch buffer (`registry.go:1926-1950`, `sampling.go:96-130`).
  - Peak transient is O(N/256). The doc comment says it replaced the flat-slice shape the proving plan measured at >12 GB.
- **`ColdRecord`** (`coldshard.go:354-379`) has 24 fields and is about 104 B with padding (UNVERIFIED; the test logs `sizeof` at `rowof_scale_gate_test.go:44`).
- **Per-citizen resident cost.** The SoA columns (`coldshard.go:53-119`) sum to about **75 B**. The BUG-666 index is a per-shard `map[uint64]int32`, measured at **38 B/citizen** (`coldshard.go:386-403`), about 3× the 6–8 B design estimate. `bytesPerCitizen()` reports about **113 B** (`coldshard.go:427-461`).
- **The proving plan's 82.9 B/citizen live heap** at 10M (`docs/planning/go-engine-100m-proving-plan.md:70-76`) was measured **before** the index landed. Treat 113–125 B as current. Re-measure.
- **A second full-population transient the brief missed.** `liveResidentIDs()` (`internal/engine/compose/compose.go:4110`) builds a full `[]uint64` of every live resident, about 8 B/citizen. It feeds the serial monthly walks in `moneycirc.go`. It is cached, but a cache miss materialises 80 MB at 10M, 240 MB at 30M and 800 MB at 100M.
- **Disk paging exists and is off by default.**
  - `EnableDiskPaging(dir, maxResident, cid)` (`registry.go:326-343`) and the `-citizen-paging` / `-citizen-page-budget` metroserve flags cap resident shards.
  - LRU eviction via `evictOverBudgetLocked` (`registry.go:726-753`) gob-encodes shards to disk (`paging.go:288-318`).
  - The BUG-799 work landed today (8209164) made the page-directory claim safe across processes.

### Proposed

1. **Delete `allColdRecordsLocked` and `BuildSample`/`ColdParams`** (GR#18 dead code), or re-implement `BuildSample` on the streaming builder if a public sampling API is wanted. Either way the materialising shape disappears from the codebase, not just from the tick.
2. **Replace `liveResidentIDs()` with a shard-ordered visitor** for the monthly walks. `GatherInShardOrder` (`registry.go:559`) already walks in shard order, so the id slice is redundant.
3. **Peak RSS estimate after (1) and (2).** These are estimates to be replaced by measurement at each rung:
   - Live cold store at ~118 B/citizen (columns + index + slice slack).
   - Go GC headroom at the default `GOGC=100` roughly doubles the live heap at peak. With `GOMEMLIMIT` set a little under the container limit, the collector works harder instead of growing the heap.
   - Snapshot payload: today `PutSnapshot` takes one `[]byte` (`internal/persist/store.go:19-189`). The whole compressed snapshot sits in memory once. §4.3 estimates 150–250 MB at 10M.
   - Fixed overhead (building grid, roads, ledger, WebSocket buffers, runtime): assume 300–500 MB. **UNVERIFIED**, measure at inc1.

| Rung | Live cold store | Peak with `GOGC=100` | Peak with `GOMEMLIMIT` + snapshot + overhead | Fits 8 GiB Consumption? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10M | ~1.2 GB | ~2.4 GB | ~2.0–2.8 GB | **Yes, comfortably** (4 GiB replica is enough) |
| 30M | ~3.5 GB | ~7.0 GB | ~4.5–5.5 GB with `GOMEMLIMIT=7GiB` | **Marginal without paging, safe with paging** |
| 100M | ~11.8 GB | ~23.6 GB | ~16–20 GB | **No.** 32 GiB floor, 64 GiB provisioned (proving plan §2.4 stands) |

**The 30M answer:** a 30M city survives in 8 GiB only with `GOMEMLIMIT` set **and** disk paging on. For example, `-citizen-page-budget 160` keeps about 160 of 256 shards resident, cutting the resident cold store by ~37%. The cost is page-in latency on cold shards, and that latency hits the monthly walks, which touch every shard. Measure before committing. Without paging, 30M on 8 GiB is a GC-pressure gamble.

## 1.2 The monthly pass: CPU budget, tick-slicing or a parallel barrier

### On disk today

- **Clock.** 1 engine tick = 1 game day; `DailyTicksPerMonth = 30` (`internal/engine/core/clock.go:15`).
- **Pacing.** Real-time pacing belongs to the transport (`clock.go:~57-59`). metroserve's `-tick-interval` defaults to 250 ms; the Dockerfile CMD uses 1 s.
- **Browser speeds.** `SPEED_MS = {1: 900, 2: 420, 3: 160}` (`webconsole/src/sim/engine.ts:489`).
- **Small discrepancy:** the proving plan's budget is 1000 ms Normal (`:27-28`), the browser ships 900 ms.
- **Option B already exists for the citizen cold pass.**
  - `ColdPassSchedule` spreads the 256 shards over the 30 days of the month: shard `i` runs on day `i*30/256`, 8–9 shards per day (`coldpass.go:27-39`).
  - Each day's shards go through `runShardsParallel` (`registry.go:1459`), with results summed in ascending shard order.
  - Mortality, education, job matching and health drift therefore already run flat.
- **What still spikes at month end:**
  - `DeathQueue.RealiseDrained` runs sequentially at `dayTick == DaysPerMonth-1`, with dissolution and `removeAt` per death (`registry.go:~1530`).
  - `applyFertilityLocked` (`fertility.go:335`) is sequential every day over the scheduled shards, with a cross-shard partner read.
  - The compose money passes run serially on the month boundary (`compose.go:~2540-2810`):
    - `markEmploymentAndCount` (`moneycirc.go:335`) is a full `GatherInShardOrder` walk applying employment `LifeEvent`s.
    - `distributeWagesToResidents` (`moneycirc.go:508-546`) is a full walk crediting `LifeEventWealth` per employed resident.
    - `formResidentHouseholds` (`moneycirc.go:450`, called `compose.go:3283`) is a full walk.
  - Consumption, council tax and rent are aggregate posts, not per-citizen: £55/household constant (`moneycirc.go:79/88`), rent `1_000_000` (`:125`), `postConsumptionAndTax` (`:586`).
  - No per-citizen power, water, food or transport billing exists. `moneycirc_inc2.go` was not read: UNVERIFIED.
- **Parallelism in production is off.** `c.workers` is 1 (`registry.go:235`). There is no `go func` in `moneycirc.go`.
- **Measured day-tick cost** (proving plan `:70-76`, pre-BUG-666): 17.6 ms at 1M, 127 ms at 3M, 624 ms at 10M. The post-BUG-666 numbers are not recorded on disk (UNVERIFIED). The `perf-population-probe` gate now asserts probe counts, not time (`bug665_population_perf_gate_test.go:~135, 262-274`).

### The month-end stall: an estimate to be measured

The three serial compose walks each do, per citizen:
- a shard-ordered visit,
- a map lookup (`rowOf`),
- a callback,
- often a `LifeEvent` write through the command path.

Assume 80–200 ns per visit, which is cache-miss-bound at this scale. Then one walk is 0.8–2 s at 10M, and three walks are **2.4–6 s at 10M, 7–18 s at 30M and 24–60 s at 100M**, all on one core, on one tick. Against the 160 ms turbo budget that is a hard stall at every month boundary from about 1M upward. Parallelism helps, but the walks are memory-bandwidth-bound: expect ~3× on 4 vCPU and ~8–10× on 16 vCPU, not linear. **These figures are an estimate, not a measurement.** The first job of inc4's 10M rung is to measure them.

### Option A (parallel barrier at month end) against Option B (staggered ring)

| | A: parallel barrier | B: staggered ring (extend what exists) |
|---|---|---|
| Month-end stall | Shrinks by the parallel speed-up but stays a spike (~1–2 s at 10M on 4 vCPU) | **Flat**: each day does 1/30 of the work |
| Determinism | Safe if every worker writes only its own shard's slot and the reduction runs in shard order (the existing `results[shard]` pattern; detgate already proves worker-count independence) | Safe by the same rule, already in production for the cold pass |
| Fiscal reporting | Natural: the month closes with complete numbers | Needs **per-shard partial accumulators** posted to the ledger as one entry at day 30, so the monthly statement stays one balanced post |
| Semantic change | None | **Real.** Wages would be paid on the employment state as of the citizen's shard-day, not month end. A citizen hired on day 20 whose shard ran on day 5 is unpaid that month. Households formed on day 3 see partners whose shard runs on day 25 at last month's state. |
| Cross-citizen reads | Barrier snapshot: everyone sees the same month-start state | Cross-shard reads (fertility partners, households) see mixed-age state. Needs a month-start snapshot of the read-only columns, or acceptance of up to 29 days of staleness |

**Recommendation: both, split by what each pass reads.**

1. Keep and extend **Option B** for passes whose inputs are **per-citizen and shard-local**:
   - the cold pass (already done);
   - wage crediting, which is a per-employed-citizen `LifeEventWealth`, with the monthly total accumulated per shard and posted once at day 30;
   - employment marking.

   Define employment for payroll as "**state at the start of the citizen's shard-day**". That is a balance rule for Aaron (see Q100155).
2. Use **Option A** for passes with **cross-shard reads**: household formation, fertility partner matching, death realisation. They run at month end over `min(GOMAXPROCS, 8)` workers against a month-start read snapshot, with a shard-ordered reduction.
3. **Code seams:**
   - Turn on `c.workers` from config: a new `WithWorkers(n)` metroserve option, defaulting to `GOMAXPROCS` capped at 8.
   - Convert the three `GatherInShardOrder` walks in `moneycirc.go` into per-shard functions dispatched by `ColdPassSchedule` (B) or `runShardsParallel` (A).
   - Delete `liveResidentIDs`.
   - The detgate's 1-worker against 14-worker comparison (`gate_test.go:33-38`) is the safety net. Extend its digest (`StateDigest`) to cover per-citizen wealth so a wrong reduction order goes red.

## 1.3 Replacing the NDJSON snapshot and evolving `persist.Store`

### On disk today

- **`Store`** (`internal/persist/store.go:19-189`): 14 methods; snapshots are a single opaque `[]byte`.
  - `PutSnapshot(ctx, city, []byte) (SnapshotID, error)`
  - `GetSnapshot`, `ListSnapshots`, `DeleteSnapshot`
  - `AppendJournal`, `ReadJournal`
  - seed and game-mode sidecars
- **Implementations.** Only `DiskStore` (`diskstore.go`, 902 lines) and `MemStore`. **No Blob implementation exists.** No Azure SDK is imported anywhere.
- **Snapshot payload.** A **zip of the `Composition.Save` bundle directory** (`internal/engine/compose/snapshot.go:47-57, 859`). Citizens go in as **NDJSON + gzip `BestCompression`**, one gzip stream per shard, one JSON object per citizen (`internal/foundation/serialize/ndjson.go:21-23, 78, 125`; `coldCitizenWire` has 25 fields, `participant.go:182-212`).
  - The citizen stream is bounded per shard (`coldStream`, `participant.go:464-496`, about 26 MB per shard at 100M).
  - The **zip is materialised into one `[]byte`** for `PutSnapshot`, so the compressed snapshot is resident at write time.
- **Cadence.** `SnapshotCadenceTicks = 360` and `MaxRetainedSnapshots = 5` (`snapshot.go:63, 74`). Writes go through `atomicWrite`: temp file, fsync, rename, dir fsync (`diskstore.go:219-258`).
- **Paging codec.** `coldShardWire` gob (`paging.go:98-160`), described as a placeholder pending `int.serializer`. It is separate from snapshots.
- **code.json.** `int.persist` (`code.json:11786-11819`) has outbound calls **[]**. Its only registered consumer is `feat.compositionroot`. Three real consumers are unregistered: engine.core, the checkpoint feature, and cmd/metroserve (`persist/doc.go:23-24`; design §9 P7).

### The three options

| | 1. Packed binary SoA per shard | 2. Parquet per shard (`parquet-go/parquet-go`) | 3. Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bytes/citizen raw | **~75 B** (columns as stored; the index is rebuilt on load, not saved) | ~75 B before encoding; columnar compression is good | as 1 |
| Compressed (10M) | ~150–250 MB (design §4.3 estimate) | similar or slightly smaller | as 1 |
| Encode/decode cost | Near-memcpy: little-endian column buffers plus a header. No reflection | Reflection or codegen; heavier encoder | as 1 on the hot path |
| New dependency | None with stdlib gzip. A zstd codec (~3–4× faster than gzip) means `klauspost/compress`, a GR#10 dependency decision | `parquet-go` plus its tree (large); design §13.6 says it needs a new `int.persist` → codec edge first | as 1, plus 2 off the hot path |
| Determinism | Byte-stable if column order and header are fixed (the NDJSON gzip header pinning in `ndjson.go:36-40` is the precedent) | Writer metadata can embed library version and timestamps; must be pinned | as 1 |
| Analytics | None without a reader | Query-ready (DuckDB etc.) | Export on demand |
| Fits the 15 s wake | **Yes** | Probably, slower | Yes |

**Recommendation: Option 3 in this order.**
- Ship **(1) packed binary SoA** for engine snapshots in inc2–inc4. Start with stdlib `compress/gzip` at a fast level, not `BestCompression`, which is the slowest setting for the hottest path. Move to zstd only if the wake budget needs it and Aaron approves the dependency (Q100156).
- Keep the NDJSON path as the **portable, human-readable export** and the cross-version fallback.
- Parquet stays the Phase-5+ analytics export the design doc already places there (§13.5).

### `Store` interface changes

Add these methods. Do not break `PutSnapshot`, which stays for small cities and the single-blob case below the 10 MB threshold (design §4.4 rule 2).

```go
// Multi-part snapshot. Parts are written first (immutable), then CommitSnapshot
// atomically publishes the manifest. A crash before commit leaves orphan parts
// that ListSnapshots never sees and a sweeper deletes.
BeginSnapshot(ctx, city CityKey, tick int64) (SnapshotWriter, error)
type SnapshotWriter interface {
    PutPart(ctx context.Context, name string, data io.Reader) error // e.g. "citizens/shard-017", "core"
    Commit(ctx context.Context, m SnapshotManifest) (SnapshotID, error) // DiskStore: rename dir + fsync; Blob: PUT manifest.json If-Match:<etag>
    Abort(ctx context.Context) error
}
OpenSnapshot(ctx, city CityKey, id SnapshotID) (SnapshotReader, error)
type SnapshotReader interface {
    Manifest() SnapshotManifest
    OpenPart(ctx context.Context, name string) (io.ReadCloser, error) // parallel-safe
}
type SnapshotManifest struct {
    Tick, JournalSegment int64       // journal position this snapshot covers (see 1.4)
    Epoch                uint64      // writer epoch (see 1.4)
    Parts                []PartInfo  // name, size, sha256
    Format               string      // "soa-v1+gzip", "ndjson+gzip"
}
```

- **GR#25 and code.json.** A Blob backend lives in `internal/persist/` as a second implementation of the existing inbound `Store` contract. That needs **no new code.json edge**, per the design's §9.
  - The Azure SDK is a third-party Go module, not a code.json module. Whether `edge-lint` (BUG-482) inspects third-party imports is **UNVERIFIED**. Check before inc2.
  - The three missing **inbound** consumer edges (P7) should be registered in the same change. That is overdue graph debt, not new design.
- **Dirty-shard tracking: mostly not worth it.** Shards are hash-partitioned by citizen id (`det.ShardForEntity`, `registry.go:1741`), and the monthly cold pass touches **every** shard every month. At the 360-tick (12-month) cadence every shard is dirty at every snapshot. It pays only for sub-month snapshots, such as snapshot-on-hibernate after a short session. Implement it as a cheap per-shard "mutated since last snapshot" bit, and expect it to skip almost nothing in normal play.

## 1.4 Journal durability, BUG-480, compaction and writer fencing

### On disk today

- **Framing.** `[4B big-endian len][payload][4B CRC32-IEEE over header+payload]` (`internal/persist/journal.go:43-56, 78-88`).
- **Torn tail.** `decodeFrames` stops at the first bad frame and returns the good prefix (`journal.go:97-125`). The torn tail is **not truncated** on disk.
- **Every append** opens the file with `O_APPEND`, writes, calls `f.Sync()` and closes, per record, under an in-process per-city mutex (`diskstore.go:265-314`).
- **No sequence numbers and no dedup** on journal records.
- **The record** is `json.Marshal(protocol.Command)`. The Command carries `protocolVersion`, `correlationId`, `issuedAtTick`, `kind` and `payload` (`internal/protocol/envelope.go:103-109`; `compose/persistjournal.go:157-178`). No actor, tenant or seq.
- **Single file, never pruned.** `journal.dat` (`diskstore.go:44`; `store.go:56-60`). Restore reads the **entire journal into memory**: `RestoreCommands` → `ReadJournal`, which calls `io.ReadAll` and decodes all frames (`compose/snapshot.go:323-384`, `persistjournal.go:206-220`).
- **BUG-480 is done** (f9dd209, closed 2026-09-04).
  - Restore walks back through older snapshots when the newest is ahead of the journal (MET-G810 path).
  - A journal-dirty latch refuses to write a snapshot after a swallowed append (MET-G813).
  - A failed durable append halts the engine (BUG-472, `internal/engine/core/commands.go:475+`).
  - The design's "inc2 blocked on BUG-480" is no longer true.
- **Fencing.** None across processes. Exclusivity is in-process only: the `cityEntry` first-claimant (`cityhost.go:51-53, 160-194`) and the `DiskStore` mutex. Design §1.4 says two `metroserve` processes on one `persist-dir` would interleave appends and corrupt the city, and nothing detects it.

### Proposed

**(1) Idempotent, batched appends: ack means durable.**
- **Record v2:** `{v:2, seq:uint64, epoch:uint64, kind:"cmd"|"ext", session:string, body:<Command or ExternalEvent>}`.
  - `seq` is dense and assigned by the single writer.
  - `epoch` is the writer epoch from (3) below.
- **Dedup key:** `(session, correlationId)`.
  - The server keeps a bounded recent-key window per session, for example the last 4096 keys or 10 game-days.
  - A retried command after a reconnect returns the original `CommandResult` and is not re-applied.
  - The browser must reuse the correlationId on retry. `protocolClient.ts` has no retry today (see 2.1), so this is built together with reconnect.
- **Group commit.**
  - Accepted commands append to an in-memory batch.
  - The batch flushes on whichever comes first: N records (e.g. 64), T ms (e.g. 200 ms), or a tick boundary.
  - Each flush is one `f.Sync()` on disk or one `AppendBlock` on Blob.
  - **`CommandResult` is sent only after the batch containing it is durable.** No player-visible action is ever acknowledged and then lost. That replaces per-record fsync without introducing silent loss.
- **On restart, truncate the torn tail** to the last good frame, logging a registry warning code. Today the bad bytes stay on disk forever and every later append lands after them.

**(2) Segments, sealing and compaction under Q100149.**
- The journal becomes `journal/<epoch>-<firstSeq>.seg`.
- A segment is **sealed at every snapshot commit**. The snapshot manifest records `JournalSegment` and `seq`: the exact position it covers.
- **Restore** reads the manifest, loads the snapshot, then streams **only the segments after it**. The restore cost becomes O(tail), not O(history).
- **Retention.** Keep the segments needed to replay from the oldest retained snapshot (`MaxRetainedSnapshots = 5`) in hot storage. Under Q100149's recommendation ("never prune the journal in the cloud"), move older sealed segments to the Blob **cool/archive tier**, never delete them. Rewind and hard-reset genesis replay (FEAT-1972079897) read from there.
- **"Compaction" here means only moving segments out of the restore path.** It never rewrites a journal record. The journal stays append-only, per design §4.4 rule 3.

**(3) Ownership lease and epoch fencing.**
- **Azure.**
  - On `GetOrCreate`, the host acquires a **60 s Blob lease** on `<tenant>/<city>/manifest.json` and renews it every 15–20 s.
  - On acquiring, it reads `manifest.epoch`, writes `epoch+1` with `If-Match:<etag>` under the lease, and keeps that value in memory.
  - Every journal `AppendBlock` and every manifest `PUT` carries the lease id, so a writer that has lost the lease gets HTTP 412 and **halts the city** (same policy as BUG-472).
  - Every journal record and segment name carries the epoch. On restore, records from an epoch lower than a later segment's epoch, and appearing after it, are rejected with a new registry code, so a zombie's late write is detectable even if it slipped through.
  - A spot eviction or a hung old revision cannot renew. Its lease lapses within 60 s, the new owner takes epoch+1, and the old one's next write fails.
- **Local disk and pix.** The same epoch in `meta.json`, plus an **OS advisory lock** on the city directory. Reuse the `LockFileEx`/`flock` primitive BUG-799 landed today in `internal/engine/compose/citizen_paging_lock_*.go`, which the kernel releases on process death. Two `metroserve` processes on one `persist-dir` then refuse instead of corrupting.
- **Order:** the lease is a prerequisite for **automatic** deploys (2.3) and for any `max-replicas > 1`. The design has it at inc4; it must move before deploy-on-green.

### Needs Aaron

Q100149 (journal retention) is still open in the design doc. This document assumes its recommendation.

# Part 2 (Round 3): FinOps under £20, wake time, deploy safety

## 2.1 Cold-start anatomy and the ≤15 s wake at 10M

### On disk today

- **Wake order.** A browser connects to `/ws`. The port-knock check runs (`cmd/metroserve/portknock.go`), then the handshake (5 s server deadline, `wsserver/server.go:89, 500`). The resolver calls `host.GetOrCreate` → `buildCity` → `RestoreLatestSnapshotOrGenesis`: unzip the snapshot, decode NDJSON citizens, replay the whole journal tail.
- **The client** (`webconsole/src/sim/protocolClient.ts`):
  - states `connecting / handshaking / live / refused / closed / error`;
  - **no reconnect, no backoff, no client handshake timeout** (`MET-P012` is declared at `:59` and never used);
  - on close it records `ERR_ENGINE_UNREACHABLE` and stops (`:290-300, 608-615`).
- **The badge** shows "LIVE ENGINE connecting / unreachable / refused" (`LiveEngineBadge.tsx:137-143`).
- **No "waking" progress** is defined in the protocol.

### Wake budget at 10M

Every line is an estimate to be replaced by an inc1/inc2 measurement.

| Step | Today | With the proposals |
|---|---|---|
| Container Apps activation from 0 + image start (distroless static Go) | **UNVERIFIED**, typically a few seconds | same; keep the image small |
| Blob lease + `manifest.json` GET | n/a | ~0.1–0.3 s |
| Fetch the snapshot (~150–250 MB compressed) | one blob, sequential | 256 parts at **concurrency 16–32**, ~1–3 s in-region (UNVERIFIED throughput) |
| Decompress + decode citizens (~750 MB raw) | NDJSON + gzip BestCompression: JSON parse of 10M objects, tens of seconds (UNVERIFIED) | packed SoA: ~0.5–1.5 s on 2–4 cores; zstd faster still |
| Rebuild the BUG-666 index (10M map inserts) | included above | ~0.5–1 s, parallel per shard |
| Journal tail replay | **up to 360 day-ticks × ~624 ms ≈ 225 s**, because shutdown never snapshots | **0 ticks** after a clean hibernate (snapshot on hibernate and on SIGTERM); crash recovery only replays since the last periodic snapshot |
| Handshake + first viewport delta | <1 s | <1 s |
| **Total** | **minutes** | **~5–10 s p50**; p95 ≤ 15 s is plausible but must be measured |

**What it takes to hold p95 ≤ 15 s:**

1. **Snapshot on every hibernate and every SIGTERM.** This is the single biggest lever, and it is missing today. The tail is then empty on every normal wake.
2. **Periodic snapshot cadence by cost, not by fixed ticks.** For crash recovery, size the cadence so the worst-case tail replays within ~5 s. At 10M and today's ~624 ms/day-tick that is a snapshot every **~8 game-days**, not 360. Make the cadence `min(360 ticks, ceil(5 s ÷ measured ms-per-tick))`. Snapshot cost is dominated by encoding 750 MB, so packed SoA is what makes frequent snapshots affordable.
3. **256 parts at 16–32 concurrent GETs,** and per-part decode on a worker pool.
4. **Packed SoA** instead of NDJSON.

### Client behaviour during wake

- **Protocol.** Add a server notification `server.waking {phase, pct}` sent after the handshake while the city loads. The handshake must not wait on the city load: the resolver call moves after the handshake reply.
- **Client.**
  - A visible "Waking city… N%" state.
  - A 60 s wake timeout separate from the 5 s handshake timeout.
  - Reconnect with exponential backoff and jitter (1, 2, 4… up to 30 s).
  - **Never** silently switch to the offline mock sim. The mock is a different game: TS aggregate against Go individuals (a1.md §1). Falling back should be an explicit player choice.

     - snapshots;
     - sends `server.hibernating`;
     - closes the socket with code 1000 and reason `hibernate`.

2. **A runaway or stuck replica.** Budget alerts lag by hours.

3. **Blob transactions.**
   - Per-record journal appends at 1 tick/s for 4 h/day come to about 430k write operations a month, a few pounds at hot-tier rates.
   - Group commit (1.4) at one flush per ~10 s cuts that by ~10×.
   - Lease renewals every 15 s are ~240/h, negligible.
   - Snapshots are 256 parts per snapshot, so frequent crash-recovery snapshots at 10M (2.1) need their write-op cost forecast. That is another reason snapshot-on-hibernate beats a short fixed cadence.
4. **Log Analytics.**
   - Set a **daily ingestion cap** on the workspace.
   - Run metroserve at warn level in Azure.
   - Never log per tick.
   - Keep inside the 5 GB/month free allowance.
5. **inc4 as designed (£33–71) breaks the £20 ruling.** Either inc4's warm 2 vCPU / 4 GiB play hours are capped by the in-app meter (~125 h), or the 10M demo runs on pix (Q100154).

### Proposed: a seamless deploy sequence

1. **Build BUG-1097** (relax the gate). This must land before any automatic deploy.
2. On SIGTERM, inside Container Apps' termination grace period, the server:
   - stops accepting commands, replying with a retryable `server.draining` error;
   - finishes the current tick;
   - flushes the journal batch;
   - **writes a snapshot**;
   - sends `server.draining {reconnectAfterMs}`;
   - closes the WebSocket with code **1012** (service restart).

   The grace period must exceed the snapshot time at the largest hosted rung. The Container Apps limit and default should be checked: **UNVERIFIED**.
3. The client treats 1012/`draining` as "reconnect quietly". It reconnects with backoff, re-handshakes and **re-subscribes**: `Delta.Seq` is per subscription and restarts at 1 (`internal/protocol/deltas.go:11-36`). Retried commands reuse their correlationIds (1.4). No page reload, no lost action.
4. **The lease (1.4) must exist before step 2 runs automatically.** During a revision swap the old and new revisions can overlap on the same city. Without a lease they share the Azure Files city directory and can interleave journal writes.
5. Only then switch `azure-deploy.yml` to deploy on green `main`.

# Part 3 (Round 4): from multi-tenant single-player towards multiplayer

## 3.1 The three pillars under the two modes

### On disk today

- **Determinism.**
  - `det.NewStream(worldSeed, entityID, month, purpose)` (`internal/foundation/det/rng.go:69`) keys FNV-1a over the inputs plus Philox 2×64 draws. It is position-independent.
  - Each city's seed is SHA-256 over the tenant and city ids (`cmd/metroserve/cityhost.go:924-940`).
  - The CI `determinism-gate` (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:107-126`) replays 120 months three times (1 worker, 1 worker, 14 workers) and compares state digests. A red result is P0 under GR#21.
- **Journal content.**
  - Only **player commands** are journaled.
  - All external inputs are **generated inside the engine** today:
    - migration draws `det.NewStream` (`internal/engine/attract/migration.go:53, 712`) against `attract.WorldPool`, default `StaticWorldPool{0}` (`config.go:121-122`);
    - market prices are static data (`data/market.json`, `pricingMode` accepts only "static");
    - external commuting reads `data/external_world.json`;
    - tourism and coastal arrivals are RNG.
  - The `Event` type (`internal/protocol/events.go:26`) is engine output, not journaled input.
- **IDs.**
  - `CityKey{TenantID, CityID}` (`internal/persist/key.go:13`); TenantID is a placeholder ("local").
  - Entity ids are bare per-city `uint64`s: citizens, firms, roads, and buildings addressed by `CellRef`.
  - **No actor or player id** exists on any command or envelope.
- **Connections.**
  - One pump per `Transport`. A second connection races, and is admitted today (finding 4 above).
  - `CheckOrigin` always returns true (`wsserver/server.go:356`). Origin filtering is only the optional env allow-list in `portknock.go`.
- **Subscriptions are per named view** with params, e.g. `f1.viewport` with origin and extent (`internal/protocol/commands.go:134-137`; `subscription.go:18-37`). Region parameters on other views are UNVERIFIED.
- **Reserved seams:** `attract.WorldPool`, the market `pricingMode` flag, and `TransportResolver`/`CityHost` multi-tenancy. FEAT-023's acceptance file requires these slots to **stay inert** (`docs/planning/acceptance/future.slots.md:33-38`). Nothing below may build multiplayer; it only names the seams.

### Mode A: separate cities trading through a regional market

- **What breaks if done naively.** City A reading City B's live state mid-tick makes A's replay depend on B's timing. Determinism per city is lost.
- **The design that keeps every city independently replayable:**
  - Cross-city effects enter a city **only as journaled external events**: a new record kind `ext` (1.4 record v2). For example:

    ```
    ExternalEvent{Kind:"regional.settlement", SourceCity, SettlementID,
                  EffectiveMonth, Lines:[{Commodity, Qty, PriceMicropounds}]}
    ```

  - The receiving city applies it at the start of `EffectiveMonth`, after journaling it.
  - **Replay reads the city's own journal only.** The market is never consulted on replay.
  - Outbound offers are engine **outputs** (events), never inputs.
  - The market service matches offers in **game-month epochs**, so cities at different speeds, or paused, simply receive settlements when they reach that month.
  - The existing seams are exactly where these land:
    - `WorldPool` becomes a journal-fed pool (migration between cities);
    - `pricingMode` gains "settled" (prices from journaled settlements);
    - extcommute's external job pools read journaled settlements instead of `external_world.json`.
  - RNG stays per city, because events carry values, not draws.

### Mode B: two mayors editing one live city

- **Single writer survives.** All commands from all sessions still funnel through the one engine command queue. The journal records the accepted order, so replay is unchanged.
- **Conflicts are resolved by order plus validation.** The second "build on cell X" is rejected by the normal validation path, with an error code back to that session, not merged.
- **Needed in `wsserver`:**
  - a **fan-out pump**: one engine transport, N sessions, each with its own outbound queue and back-pressure;
  - subscriptions owned **per session**, not per transport;
  - per-session viewport subscriptions. Player A's `f1.viewport` deltas never go to player B; the view-name grammar already carries viewport params;
  - an `actorId`/`sessionId` on every command, journaled for attribution and audit.
- **Auth.** The port-knock (Q100025: "obscurity plus a password, not authentication") must be replaced by real identity (e.g. Entra ID / OIDC tokens). `TenantID` and roles (owner, co-mayor, viewer) come from the token.

## 3.2 Low-cost seams to build in during inc1–inc4

Each costs a few lines now and saves a migration of every saved journal later:

1. **A versioned journal record envelope** `{v, seq, epoch, kind, session, body}`. It is needed anyway for dedup and fencing (1.4). The `kind` field separates **commands** from **external events** from day one.
2. **`actorId`/`sessionId` on the `Command` envelope.** Optional, defaulting to "local", always journaled.
3. **A real `TenantID` per account plus the environment namespace** (2.3), instead of the "local" placeholder (`key.go:9-12`).
4. **A writer epoch** in the manifest and in every record and segment (1.4).
5. **Cross-city references are always `(CityKey, id)`.** Never assume entity ids are globally unique: they are per-city `uint64` counters today and should stay so. One line in `internal/persist/key.go`'s doc plus a lint note.
6. **Reserved server message kinds** `server.waking`, `server.draining` and `server.hibernating`, with the client's reconnect path (2.1, 2.3). These are needed now for scale-to-zero, and later for fan-out.
7. **Subscriptions keyed by session id** inside `wsserver`, even with one session. This also fixes finding 4 properly: either refuse the second connection cleanly, or give it its own subscriptions.
