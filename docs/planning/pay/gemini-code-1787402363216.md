# System Prompt: Metropolis Macroeconomics, Wage Engine & UK Tax Architecture

## 1. Core Economic Directives
You are building the economic engine for a Metropolis simulation. The economy is closed, tightly controlled, and strictly tracks the M1 Money Supply. 
**Crucial Rule:** Do not create random jobs on the fly. Jobs strictly trace to a predefined occupation matrix triggered by building construction. 
Citizens operate entirely on cash (no debt). Only the **Player** can utilize debt, borrowing from external markets at standard floating LIBOR rates.

## 2. Generosity Slider & Base Wages
The global standard of living is controlled by a central `Generosity` state variable, $G$, where $G \in [-5, 5]$. Baseline market rate is $0$.

The annual salary for any occupation is calculated using:
$$S_{final} = S_{base} \times R_{occ} \times (1 + \alpha G)$$
*   $S_{base}$ = Global Baseline Salary (e.g., 50,000)
*   $R_{occ}$ = Occupation-specific multiplier
*   $\alpha$ = Scaling factor per point of Generosity (e.g., 0.1 for a 10% swing per point)

## 4. The M1 Liquidity Flow (The Closed Loop)
The game must constantly calculate and display the **M1** (Total liquid currency currently held by citizens and local businesses). The M1 is NOT the player's treasury; it is the taxable base.

**The Flow Rules:**
1. **Injection:** The player injects money into the M1 by paying civic salaries ($E_{civic}$).
2. **Velocity:** Citizens spend their remaining net income at commercial buildings. The volume of M1 dictates the success and upgrade rate of Commercial/Retail zones.
3. **Extraction:** The player extracts money from the M1 via Taxes ($R_{tax}$).
4. **Liquidity Trap:** If the player taxes too heavily and underpays (negative $G$), the M1 shrinks. Commercial zones fail, causing a cascading drop in future tax revenue.

## 5. Dynamic Debt & LIBOR Mechanics
The player's borrowing limit is dynamically capped by their **Net Operating Income (NOI)**, defined as $NOI = R_{tax} - E_{civic}$. 

*   **Borrowing Capacity:** $C_{debt} = \frac{NOI}{r_{libor}}$
*   **The LIBOR Rate:** $r_{libor}$ is a floating rate that recalculates annually based on the Generosity slider (inflation control) and macro shocks.
    *   $r_{libor} = 0.02 + (0.01 \times G) + \epsilon_{shock}$
*   **The Default State:** If the player's required interest payment ($I$) exceeds their $NOI$ for 3 consecutive ticks, the city enters **Municipal Default**. During Default, the player cannot build new structures, and civic buildings operate at 50% capacity until debt is paid down or NOI is increased.

## 6. UK-Centric Tax & Revenue Mechanics
Total Gross Revenue ($R_{tax}$) is the sum of four distinct UK tax streams:
$$R_{tax} = T_{paye} + T_{vat} + T_{rates} + T_{council}$$

*   **PAYE & National Insurance ($T_{paye}$):** Extracted directly from the civic wage pool before citizens receive their net pay.
    *   *Math:* $T_{paye} = \sum (S_{final} \times r_{paye})$ (Default rate: 20%).
    *   *Friction:* High rates trigger a "Brain Drain," causing high-tier workers (Doctors, Scientists) to emigrate first.
*   **Value Added Tax ($T_{vat}$):** A consumption tax applied when citizens spend their Net Income at Commercial and Retail zones.
    *   *Math:* $T_{vat} = M_{spent} \times r_{vat}$ (Default rate: 20%).
    *   *Friction:* High VAT reduces consumer purchasing volume, leading to High Street commercial abandonments.
*   **Business Rates ($T_{rates}$):** Applied to non-domestic properties (Commercial and Industrial) based on Rateable Value, completely insulated from M1 fluctuations.
    *   *Math:* $T_{rates} = \sum (V_{rateable} \times m_{multiplier})$ (Default multiplier: 0.50).
    *   *Friction:* High rates stifle industrial demand and choke the base of the wage cascade.
*   **Council Tax ($T_{council}$):** A banded property tax applied to residential homes as a fixed cost independent of citizen income.
    *   *Math:* $T_{council} = \sum (H_{band} \times r_{council})$.
    *   *Friction:* High Council Tax disproportionately hurts Tier 1 workers; if $(Net Income - Council Tax) \le 0$, citizens become destitute and cut off commercial spending.

## 7. System UI Requirements
*   **Demand UI:** When a user queues a building, calculate and display the labor demand at current slider rates (e.g., *"Demand: 10 Doctors @ 100,000/yr"*).
*   **Macro Dashboard:** Always display current Outstanding Debt, the Dynamic Debt Ceiling, the Total M1 Liquidity pool, and the four broken-down UK tax revenue streams.
