# System Prompt: Municipal Tax Revenue Mechanics

## 1. Core Taxation Directives
Total Gross Revenue ($R_{tax}$) is the sum of three distinct tax streams: Residential Income, Commercial Sales, and Industrial Property.
$$R_{tax} = T_{inc} + T_{sales} + T_{prop}$$

The player can adjust these three tax rates independently. Each targets a different part of the economic simulation and carries a unique mechanical penalty if pushed too high.

## 2. Residential Income Tax ($T_{inc}$)
This tax is extracted directly from the civic wage pool *before* the money hits the citizens' pockets. It dictates their resulting disposable income.

*   **The Math:** $T_{inc} = \sum (S_{final} \times r_{inc})$
    *   $S_{final}$ = The total gross salaries paid out by the wage engine.
    *   $r_{inc}$ = The Income Tax Rate (Player controlled, default 15%).
*   **The Friction Penalty:** High $r_{inc}$ reduces citizen Disposable Income. If average Disposable Income drops below the regional poverty line, citizens begin emigrating, leaving jobs vacant.

## 3. Commercial Sales Tax ($T_{sales}$)
This taxes the *velocity* of M1 liquidity. It is applied when citizens spend their remaining disposable income at Commercial Zones. 

*   **The Math:** $T_{sales} = M_{spent} \times r_{sales}$
    *   $M_{spent}$ = Total disposable income spent by citizens in a tick. (M1 minus savings).
    *   $r_{sales}$ = The Sales Tax Rate (Player controlled, default 8%).
*   **The Friction Penalty:** Sales tax acts as a friction coefficient on Commercial Zone growth. High sales tax reduces the volume of goods sold. If sales volume falls below a building's break-even point, the Commercial Zone abandons.
*   **Strategic Note:** Sales tax revenue drops naturally if the player raises Income Tax, because citizens have less $M_{spent}$.

## 4. Industrial Property Tax ($T_{prop}$)
A flat tax based on the assessed land and building value of Industrial (and potentially Civic) zones. This tax is completely insulated from the M1 liquidity pool and citizen wages.

*   **The Math:** $T_{prop} = \sum (V_{bldg} \times r_{prop})$
    *   $V_{bldg}$ = The fixed structural value of a constructed building (e.g., a Factory is worth 1,000,000).
    *   $r_{prop}$ = The Property Tax Rate (Player controlled, default 5%).
*   **The Friction Penalty:** High property taxes stifle industrial demand. If $r_{prop}$ exceeds the industrial profit margin (derived from goods exported), new factories will not spawn, choking the base of the wage cascade.

## 5. Integration with the Debt Ceiling
*   The sum of these three taxes constitutes $R_{tax}$. 
*   This directly feeds the Net Operating Income ($NOI$) calculation for the LIBOR borrowing system: $NOI = R_{tax} - E_{civic}$.
