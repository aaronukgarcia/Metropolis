# System Prompt: UK-Centric Tax & Revenue Mechanics

## 1. Core Taxation Directives
The player acts as the sovereign financial authority. Total Gross Revenue ($R_{tax}$) is the sum of four distinct UK tax streams:
$$R_{tax} = T_{paye} + T_{vat} + T_{rates} + T_{council}$$

Each tax targets a different segment of the economy. The player can adjust these rates independently, but pushing any single tax too high creates a specific economic friction.

## 2. PAYE & National Insurance ($T_{paye}$)
This combined income tax is extracted directly from the civic wage pool *before* citizens receive their net pay.

*   **The Math:** $T_{paye} = \sum (S_{final} \times r_{paye})$
    *   $S_{final}$ = The total gross salaries paid out by the wage engine.
    *   $r_{paye}$ = The combined Income Tax & NI rate (Player controlled, default 20%).
*   **The Friction Penalty (Brain Drain):** High $r_{paye}$ sharply reduces citizen Net Income. If Net Income drops below the regional baseline, high-tier workers (Doctors, Expert Scientists) will emigrate first, leaving critical civic jobs vacant.

## 3. Value Added Tax (VAT) ($T_{vat}$)
This taxes the *velocity* of M1 liquidity. It is a consumption tax applied when citizens spend their Net Income at Commercial and Retail Zones.

*   **The Math:** $T_{vat} = M_{spent} \times r_{vat}$
    *   $M_{spent}$ = Total Net Income spent by citizens in a tick.
    *   $r_{vat}$ = The VAT Rate (Player controlled, default 20%).
*   **The Friction Penalty (High Street Collapse):** VAT acts as a friction coefficient on Commercial Zone growth. High VAT reduces the volume of goods citizens can afford. If sales volume falls below a building's break-even point, Commercial Zones abandon (simulating boarded-up high streets).

## 4. Business Rates ($T_{rates}$)
A tax applied to non-domestic properties (Commercial and Industrial). This is based on the "Rateable Value" of the property and is entirely insulated from M1 fluctuations.

*   **The Math:** $T_{rates} = \sum (V_{rateable} \times m_{multiplier})$
    *   $V_{rateable}$ = The fixed assessed value of a business building.
    *   $m_{multiplier}$ = The Uniform Business Rate multiplier (Player controlled, default 0.50 or "50p in the pound").
*   **The Friction Penalty (Industrial Stagnation):** High Business Rates stifle industrial demand. If rates exceed the industrial profit margin (derived from goods exported), new Factories and Fab Labs will not spawn, choking the base of the wage cascade.

## 5. Council Tax ($T_{council}$)
A banded property tax applied to residential homes. Unlike PAYE, this is a fixed cost citizens must pay regardless of their income. 

*   **The Math:** $T_{council} = \sum (H_{band} \times r_{council})$
    *   $H_{band}$ = The property band of the residential zone (Bands A through H, with H paying the most).
    *   $r_{council}$ = The baseline Council Tax rate (Player controlled).
*   **The Friction Penalty (Cost of Living Crisis):** Because Council Tax is a flat fee per house tier, high rates disproportionately hurt Tier 1 workers (Factory/Retail). If a citizen's $(Net Income - Council Tax) \le 0$, they become destitute, generating negative Generosity/unrest and ceasing all commercial spending.

## 6. Integration with the Debt Ceiling
*   The sum of these four streams constitutes $R_{tax}$. 
*   This directly feeds the Net Operating Income ($NOI$) calculation for the LIBOR borrowing system: $NOI = R_{tax} - E_{civic}$.
